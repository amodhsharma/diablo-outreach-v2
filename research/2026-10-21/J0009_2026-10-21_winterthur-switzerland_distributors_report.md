## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly: Coop holds about 43% and Migros about 37% of food and near food sales (Nielsen Retail Monitor, reported November 2025), and Coop set a sales record again in 2025. Denner (Migros group), Aldi and Lidl take most of the rest. Winterthur sits in the Migros Zurich cooperative area and is also the head office of fenaco's Volg Konsumwaren, which runs the Volg village shop network and supplies independent shops, so a key convenience gatekeeper is local. Both majors buy centrally, so a national listing decision, not a Winterthur one, decides most shelf space. Foreign confectionery usually reaches shelf through a Swiss importer that handles customs, labelling compliance and the listing negotiation, then supplies the chains, kiosks and convenience (Valora), Volg and Spar, pharmacies and drugstores, health food stores and online shops such as Galaxus. Most national importers and wholesalers that serve Winterthur are based in the wider Zurich region, so in practice the route to market is a German speaking national partner rather than a city specialist. The distributor research found no new qualifying company based in or focused on Winterthur.

## Barriers to entry

- Retailer power: Coop and Migros together control about four fifths of food sales and have publicly delisted brands to force price cuts (Coop removed 95 branded products in 2011). Listing fees and distributor margins were not verified in this run.
- Importer responsibility: the Swiss importer must ensure goods comply with Swiss food law and carries self-monitoring duties, so partners will ask for full specifications and Swiss compliant labels.
- Labelling: mandatory items include name, ingredients with allergens highlighted, date marking, the manufacturer or distributor address and country of production; Switzerland asks for more origin detail than the EU. Labels must be in at least one official language and German is expected for Winterthur shoppers (the exact rule wording was not rechecked in this run).
- Sweeteners and polyols: foods with more than 10% added polyols must carry a laxative effect warning; foods with sugar plus sweeteners must say so on pack. The additives list is understood to follow EU rules (unverified product by product).
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar thresholds are understood to mirror EU rules but were not confirmed for Switzerland in this run.
- Market access: EU or EEA compliant food can generally be sold under the Cassis de Dijon principle unless on a negative list; whether UK made goods qualify for this route needs checking.
- Duty: chocolate, sugar confectionery and biscuits are processed agricultural products with an agricultural duty element based on raw material content, so landed cost varies by recipe; exact rates for Diablo lines were not checked.
- Trade agreement: the UK and Switzerland trade under a continuity agreement; an enhanced FTA was concluded in July 2026 but awaits ratification. Its food tariff terms were not confirmed.
- Logistics: most UK to Switzerland freight transits the EU, adding border time; plan shelf life at entry accordingly (no Swiss minimum verified).
