## Market structure

Aarau sits in the German speaking canton of Aargau and is served by the same national route to market as the rest of Switzerland. Grocery is concentrated: Migros and Coop together hold around 70% of grocery sales, and a 2025 Nielsen based report put Coop at 43% and Migros at 37.4% of food sales. Aargau is covered by the regional cooperative Migros Aare, which built an operations centre in Suhr next to Aarau. Discounters Aldi and Lidl (Lidl plans 300 more shops) plus Denner and Volg make up most of the rest. Private label is about half of sales, so branded imports compete for limited shelf space and listings are decided centrally by the two cooperatives' national buying teams, not in Aarau. Outside the big chains, product reaches kiosks, petrol station shops, independent grocers, health food shops and vending through specialist importers and national wholesalers, which are the practical gatekeepers for a new foreign brand. Online grew from about 8.3% of retail sales in 2019 to 11.8% in 2020; more recent online figures for food were not verified in this run.

## Barriers to entry

- Dominant players: Coop and Migros control most grocery shelf space and buy centrally; Migros historically favours its own brands and private label is about 50% of sales, which limits room for branded imports.
- Listing fees, exclusivity, distributor margins and credit terms: no reliable public figures found in this run (unverified); expect importers to ask for national exclusivity and marketing support.
- Import duty: Switzerland abolished industrial tariffs on 1 January 2024, but processed agricultural products (HS chapters 1 to 24, so chocolate, sweets and biscuits) still pay duty by weight. Check each line in the Swiss tariff (Tares).
- UK trade: a UK Switzerland agreement has applied since Brexit and talks on an enhanced FTA began in 2023; whether they have concluded and how they treat confectionery was not confirmed in this run.
- Claims: the Swiss food information ordinance (LIV, SR 817.022.16) Annex 13 sets the conditions for "zuckerfrei" (sugar free) and "ohne Zuckerzusatz" (no added sugar), in line with the EU rules; exact wording not checked in this run.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted under the Swiss additives ordinance; foods with more than 10% added polyols must carry the warning "kann bei übermässigem Verzehr abführend wirken" (source dated 2012, so confirm against current law). Steviol glycosides are now approved in the EU; current Swiss status assumed equivalent but unverified.
- Labelling: mandatory information mirrors EU rules (name, ingredients, date, nutrition table, origin); a Swiss address is not required. Labels are normally expected in at least one official language; for Aarau that is German (exact rule unverified in this run).
- Shelf life at entry: no published norm found; unverified.
