## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly. Coop (CHF 35.5 billion turnover in 2025) and Migros (CHF 31.9 billion) hold roughly 43% and 37% of food and near-food sales between them, so their central buying teams are the main gatekeepers for any branded confectionery. Denner (872 outlets), Aldi Suisse and Lidl Suisse follow at a distance, with Lidl expanding its store network. Coop and Migros run large own-label and in-house manufacturing ranges, which narrows space for imported brands; the smaller chains, Manor, independent grocers, pharmacies, drugstores, health food shops and online sellers are the realistic first targets in Lausanne. For a foreign brand the usual route is a Swiss importer that holds stock, clears customs, relabels where needed and takes legal responsibility as the Swiss distributor named on pack. Many of the obvious Romandie importers and confectionery wholesalers are already on Diablo's researched list, so the remaining gatekeepers are mid-size specialist importers and pharmacy or drugstore suppliers. Retailer and channel shares beyond the top two chains are estimates, not published figures.

## Barriers to entry

- Retailer power: Coop and Migros together control about four fifths of food sales and have publicly delisted brands to force price cuts (Coop removed 95 branded products in 2011). Expect hard price negotiation; listing fees and distributor margins were not verified in this run.
- Importer responsibility: the Swiss importer must ensure goods comply with Swiss food law and carries self-monitoring duties, so partners will ask for full specifications and Swiss-compliant labels.
- Labelling: mandatory items include name, ingredients with allergens highlighted, date marking, the manufacturer or distributor address, country of production and ingredient origin where it differs; Switzerland asks for more origin detail than the EU. Labels in French are expected for Lausanne shoppers (the exact language rule was not confirmed in this run).
- Sweeteners and polyols: foods with more than 10% added polyols must carry "Une consommation excessive peut avoir des effets laxatifs"; foods with sugar plus sweeteners must say "avec sucre(s) et édulcorant(s)". The additives list is understood to follow EU rules (unverified product by product).
- Claims: sugar-free and no-added-sugar claim thresholds are understood to mirror EU rules but were not confirmed for Switzerland in this run.
- Market access: EU or EEA compliant food can generally be sold under the Cassis de Dijon principle unless on a negative list; check whether UK-made goods qualify for this route.
- Duty: chocolate (chapter 18), sugar confectionery (1704) and biscuits (1905) are processed agricultural products and attract an agricultural duty element based on raw material content (sugar, milk powder, flour), so landed cost varies by recipe; exact rates for Diablo lines were not checked.
- Trade agreement: the UK and Switzerland trade under a continuity agreement; an enhanced FTA was concluded in July 2026 but needs ratification, with entry into force expected in 2027. Its food tariff terms were not confirmed.
- Logistics: most UK to Switzerland freight transits the EU, adding border time; plan shelf life at entry accordingly (no Swiss minimum verified).
