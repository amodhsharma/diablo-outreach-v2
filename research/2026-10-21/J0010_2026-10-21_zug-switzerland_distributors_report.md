## Market structure

Zug is a small, wealthy canton served by national route to market structures rather than local ones. Swiss grocery is highly concentrated: Coop and Migros together hold around 70% of grocery retail, and a Nielsen based report put Coop at 43% and Migros at 37.4% of food and near food in 2025. Denner (Migros owned), Lidl, Aldi and Volg make up most of the rest, and private label is around half of sales, which makes the national buying desks of Coop and Migros the main gatekeepers. Outside the big chains, product reaches kiosks, petrol station shops and independent grocers through a handful of national confectionery and kiosk wholesalers and brand building importers that deliver nationwide from depots in other cantons. Valora (owned by FEMSA since 2022) runs the k kiosk and avec convenience network and is a gatekeeper for impulse sales. Canton Zug itself mostly hosts head offices of brand owners and commodity traders, so local distributors are scarce (see the Distributors section). Online grocery has grown since 2020, led by the two majors and marketplaces such as Galaxus; its current share is unverified.

## Barriers to entry

- Retail concentration: Coop and Migros control most grocery shelf space and push private label, so a listing usually needs a Swiss importer or distributor who already holds their buyers. Listing fees and promotional contributions are common practice but exact levels are unverified.
- Distributor norms: national kiosk and confectionery wholesalers and importers typically ask for national exclusivity and marketing support; typical margin and credit terms are unverified for this run.
- Trade agreement: the Switzerland UK trade agreement carries over the Switzerland EU free trade agreement and agriculture agreement terms, with UK Swiss cumulation and diagonal cumulation with the EU under Protocol 3 rules of origin.
- Import duty: processed agricultural products such as chocolate and cakes pay an industrial element plus variable components that bring the raw material cost (sugar, milk, flour) up to Swiss prices. Exact rates for UK origin confectionery should be checked in the Tares tariff; treatment of sugar-free recipes (polyols or inulin in place of sugar) is unverified.
- Food law: Switzerland has its own food law, closely modelled on EU rules. Nutrition claims such as "zuckerfrei" are governed by Annex 13 of the Food Information Ordinance (LIV) and only listed claims may be used; the EU threshold for "sugar free" is 0.5 g per 100 g, and the Swiss conditions are understood to match but were not verified line by line.
- Sweeteners and warnings: permitted sweeteners and polyols by product type follow the Swiss Additives Ordinance, understood to be aligned with EU Regulation 1333/2008, including the "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect" warning above 10% added polyols. Not verified on the Swiss text in this run.
- Labelling: Swiss labels must be in at least one official language; for the German speaking Zug area German is expected. EU compliant foods that differ from Swiss technical rules can be sold under the Cassis de Dijon principle only after an FSVO general ruling, and this route is for EU products; how UK made products are treated is unverified.
- Shelf life at entry: no fixed Swiss rule found; residual shelf life is set by each distributor and retailer.
