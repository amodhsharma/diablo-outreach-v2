## Market structure

Packaged confectionery reaches Georgian consumers mainly through importer-distributors that hold stock, run their own vans and supply both chains and the large traditional trade. Traditional, unorganised shops still hold an estimated 59% of FMCG sales (TBC Capital via Investor.ge, 2024), so a distributor with corner shop coverage matters as much as a chain listing. Tbilisi dominates: an older source puts 81% of trade activity in the capital. Modern grocery is concentrated. Daily Group (SPAR, Ioli, Magniti and others) holds roughly a third of organised FMCG and owns its own distributor, Foodservice Ltd; Nikora has about 19% with some 617 stores (GBC, November 2024). Carrefour (Majid Al Futtaim), Goodwill and Agrohub lead the premium and hypermarket end and import part of their range directly. Online grocery is small, about 2% of grocery spend, though quick commerce (Wolt Market, Glovo) is visible in Tbilisi. The real gatekeepers are therefore the four to six large national distributors (for example GDM, Megaco, Kanti, Orbita) and the central buying teams at Daily Group and Nikora. Specialist health retail is a handful of small Tbilisi shops plus European importer chains such as Europroduct.
Sources: [Investor.ge: Georgia's retail sector taking off](https://www.investor.ge/2024/12/05/georgias-retail-sector-taking-off), [GBC: FMCG sector finds it difficult to increase wages](https://gbc.ge/en/news/busines-snews/fmcg-sector-finds-it-difficult-to-increase-wages), [Bank of Scotland Trade: Georgia distribution](https://www.bankofscotlandtrade.co.uk/en/market-potential/georgia/distribution), [Finspot: distribution companies 2024](https://finspot.ge/blog/distribution-companies-2024)

## Closing analysis

- Europroduct (europroduct.ge): appears in all three Channels as an importer, distributor and retailer of premium European brands that already merchandises a sugar free range, so one conversation covers import, wholesale and its own Tbilisi shelves. Check which sugar free brands it already holds before pitching.
- GDM (gdmco.ge): a top four national distributor already moving imported European confectionery through modern and traditional trade with in-store merchandising and no sugar free line seen, so Diablo fills a gap and gains the corner shop reach that matters in a market where traditional trade still holds most FMCG sales.
- Organika Bio Shop (organika.ge): the clearest specialist retail fit, with an existing diabetic and keto category built on European brands; a fast, low-risk proof of sale in Tbilisi before approaching Carrefour, Agrohub or Daily Group.
- Warning: Master Trade and D&L Service share an address with Diplomat Holding, linked to the excluded Diplomat Georgia; confirm they are independent before any contact.

## Barriers to entry

- Exclusivity: national distributors typically work as exclusive representatives of foreign brands (Orbita, Europroduct describe this model), so expect to grant country exclusivity to one partner.
- Retailer fees: in 2025 and 2026 the government blamed supermarket and distributor mark-ups for food inflation; the minister said chains apply "nine additional charges" to suppliers. Exact listing fee and bonus levels are unverified. A parliamentary commission and State Security Service inquiry into retail pricing began in early 2026, so fee practices may change.
- Concentration: Daily Group owns both a retail network and the distributor Foodservice Ltd, which can favour in-house routes; Nikora is the other single large buyer.
- Distributor margin and credit norms: no reliable published figures found (unverified); assume deferred payment terms are expected by chains.
- Labelling: every product sold in Georgia must carry a Georgian-language label (an EU language may sit alongside it), including ingredients, allergens, importer name and address, batch and durability date. Food information rules are in Government Resolution No. 301 of 2016, in force since 1 September 2018. A Georgian back-label sticker applied by the importer is common practice but this was not verified in law.
- Claims: nutrition and health claims are governed by Resolution No. 510 of 2016 (in force 1 March 2018). Whether its "sugar free" and "no added sugar" conditions mirror EU Regulation 1924/2006 exactly was not verified; Georgia's DCFTA commits it to approximate EU food law, so EU-compliant packs are a reasonable starting point.
- Sweeteners and polyols: a Georgian technical regulation lists permitted additives including sweeteners (Resolution No. 379 of 2019 per FAOLEX). Whether steviol glycosides and each polyol are permitted by product type, and whether the EU "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects" warning is required, was not verified; check with the National Food Agency.
- Shelf life at entry: no published minimum remaining shelf life rule found (unverified); importers commonly set their own.
- Duties and trade agreements: Georgia's import duty bands are 0%, 5% and 12%, plus 18% VAT on most imports. The UK-Georgia Strategic Partnership and Cooperation Agreement keeps preferential tariffs for UK-origin goods, but only if UK rules of origin are met; the exact duty line for each Diablo product was not verified.
