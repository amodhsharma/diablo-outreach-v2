## Market structure

Peru is still a traditional trade market. Kantar (2025) puts more than 80% of household mass consumption spending through bodegas and neighbourhood markets, and an older industry estimate put 79.9% of chocolate volume through kiosks and bodegas against 20.1% in supermarkets (2018, likely dated). Modern retail is concentrated in Lima: three groups dominate, Cencosud (Wong, Metro), Falabella (Tottus) and InRetail's Supermercados Peruanos (Plaza Vea, Vivanda, Mass), with discounters and convenience chains growing fastest. Pharmacy chains are a second modern channel for diabetic and better-for-you products (not verified in this run).

The real gatekeepers are the Lima importer distributors. A Peruvian domiciled importer must hold the DIGESA sanitary registration for every product, so a foreign brand cannot sell without one. These importers then sell to the chains and, through their own sales forces or wholesalers and markets, into the bodega network. Imported premium confectionery will in practice sit mainly in modern retail, pharmacies and specialist stores in higher income Lima districts; the bodega channel is price led and dominated by local and regional majors (Arcor bought Sayón from Alicorp, for example).

## Closing analysis

- **Natural Foods Peru (Wibgus SAC):** the closest specialist fit, an importer wholesaler already selling sugar free and better for you lines into the three main supermarket chains, with only a candy overlap (Koochikoo) leaving chocolate, biscuits and spreads open.
- **QS Consumo (Química Suiza Consumo):** found by both the distributor and wholesaler research, a pharmacy grade consumer distributor of about 30 brands that became independent in December 2025 and is likely looking for new brands, with reach into pharmacies, supermarkets and bodegas.
- **G.W. Yichang & Cía S.A.:** Peru's largest chocolate importer under HS 180690 with more than 150 brands and Lima convenience and bodega reach, so it already runs the import licences and route Diablo needs and carries no sugar free chocolate line found in this run.

## Barriers to entry

- **Sanitary registration:** DIGESA registro sanitario, filed online through VUCE, valid five years. It is issued per product and manufacturer and each flavour needs its own registration, so a 117 SKU range is costly; start with a short hero range. The holder must be a Peruvian domiciled importer, which ties the brand to that partner.
- **Documents:** a certificate of free sale from the UK is required, and foreign documents must be under one year old and translated into Spanish.
- **Labelling:** the label must follow the Peruvian metrological labelling standard and show importer details, the registration number, batch and expiry date. Spanish back labels or stickers are the norm (exact language rule not verified in this run).
- **Octagon warnings (Ley 30021):** front of pack "alto en" warnings for sugar, sodium, saturated fat and trans fat. Since 2023 importers may permanently use hard to remove adhesives instead of printed octagons. Products high in saturated fat (likely for some chocolate and spreads) will carry a warning even when sugar free.
- **Sweeteners and claims:** no specific Peruvian sweetener warning was found in this run, and Peru is understood to follow Codex for additives and "sin azúcar" claims. Polyol laxative wording and permitted polyols by product type were not verified; check with the importer's regulatory team.
- **Import duty:** Peru's MFN tariff has three main levels (0%, 6% and 11%), with price bands on sugar and dairy. The UK Andean agreement gives zero preferential tariffs for most products if UK rules of origin are met; the confectionery line rate was not verified. IGV plus IPM add 18% on CIF plus duty.
- **Retail terms and concentration:** three supermarket groups control most modern grocery, and Indecopi has investigated them for price coordination. Listing fees, exhibition charges, payment terms and distributor margins could not be verified in this run; expect long payment terms and promotional contributions as is common in the region (unverified).
- **Competition:** imported sugar free lines such as Valor are already on shelf at Wong and Metro, per this run's retailer research.
