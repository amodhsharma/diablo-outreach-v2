## Market structure

Packaged confectionery in Argentina still moves mainly through the fragmented traditional trade. NielsenIQ puts the traditional channel (mainly almacenes) at 34% of mass consumption, chain supermarkets at 31%, kiosks and minimarkets at 15%, independent autoservicios at 14% and pharmacies at 5% (2025 data, reported April 2026). Late 2025 growth came from almacenes and independent autoservicios while chains fell. There are more than 194,700 independent points of sale nationally, so the real gatekeepers for an imported snack brand are the importer of record and the regional distributors and mayoristas (wholesalers and cash and carry) that serve kiosks, almacenes and autoservicios. Córdoba is a confectionery heartland: Arcor (Colonia Caroya) and Georgalos (Río Segundo) are local, and an industry estimate places 60% to 70% of national chocolate capacity in the province, so local brands are strong and well distributed. Chain supermarkets and pharmacy chains are the natural home for a premium imported sugar-free range, with health food shops (dietéticas) as a niche. The channel share figures are national; we found no Córdoba-only split.

## Closing analysis

- Bio-Salud S.R.L. (distributors, Tier 1 rank 1): already sells sugar-free and stevia lines into Córdoba dietéticas, the shelf that best suits Diablo; lead with chocolate and spreads because the Happy Food sugar-free alfajor overlaps with biscuits and sweets.
- EntreSano (retailers, Tier 1 rank 1): a seven-store Córdoba healthy supermarket chain that already runs a sugar-free category across around 400 brands, so a curated imported range fits and its 600 m2 Recta Martinolli store can take a branded block.
- La Granja del Centro (wholesalers, Tier 1 rank 1): a dietética wholesaler representing manufacturers that lists light and fit snacks but no sugar-free chocolate, giving Diablo a clear gap to fill across its trade customers.

## Barriers to entry

- Dominant local players: Arcor and Georgalos are based in Córdoba and have deep reach into kiosks and almacenes; imported products must justify a premium price against them.
- Shelf rules: the Ley de Góndolas (shelf share caps and SME quotas) was repealed by DNU 70/2023, so chains now negotiate shelf space freely. Listing fees, supplier payment terms and distributor margins could not be verified for Argentina in this run; treat them as unknown and ask partners.
- Front of pack labelling (Ley 27.642): products exceeding nutrient thresholds carry black octagon warnings, and any product containing sweeteners must carry a notice that it is not recommended for children. Products with any octagon cannot carry further nutrition claims, child appeal characters or promotional gifts. Whether specific Diablo SKUs trigger octagons (for example excess fats or calories) needs checking product by product.
- Sweeteners and claims: sweeteners and "sin azúcar" claims are governed by the Código Alimentario Argentino. The permitted list for steviol glycosides and polyols by product type, and whether a laxative warning is required for polyols, could not be verified in this run and should be confirmed with a regulatory adviser.
- Import procedure: Decreto 35/2025 created a simplified route for food from countries with high sanitary oversight, explicitly including the UK and EU: a sworn declaration and import notice on the TAD platform, plus a free sale certificate and proof of origin. INAL keeps post entry market controls. Spanish labelling is still expected in practice (unverified detail).
- Import duty: the exact Mercosur common external tariff rate for chocolate, sugar confectionery and biscuits could not be verified in this run.
- Trade agreements: the EU Mercosur agreement has applied provisionally from 1 May 2026, including Argentina, with some duties cut immediately and sensitive lines phased over up to 15 years. It does not cover goods of UK origin; whether Diablo goods shipped by B Healthy Ltd (UK) can qualify depends on EU preferential origin and is unverified. There is no UK Mercosur trade agreement that we found.
