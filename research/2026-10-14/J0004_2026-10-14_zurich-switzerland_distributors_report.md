## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly. Coop holds about 43% of food and food-related sales and Migros 37.4% (Nielsen, reported November 2025). Coop has now overtaken Migros. Lidl is expanding with 300 new shops planned, while Aldi is not expanding. Denner, Volg and Spar are smaller players. Private label takes around half of sales, and Migros alone runs about 250 own brands, so shelf space for imported branded confectionery is tight. The two leaders buy centrally and often import directly. That makes their national category buyers the real gatekeepers for mainstream shelf.

Distributors matter most outside the duopoly. They supply:
- independent and organic shops;
- pharmacies and drugstores (relevant for a sugar-free and diabetic angle);
- kiosk and convenience formats such as Coop Pronto and migrolino, both growing;
- foodservice;
- online, where Coop lists about 21,000 products and Migros 12,500. Migros is adding a second online distribution centre in Regensdorf (Zurich).

For Zurich, a national importer with German-speaking field sales is the realistic route, with the duopoly as a later listing goal. How much each channel adds up to for sugar-free confectionery is unverified.

## Closing analysis

- **R.C. Andreae AG (rca.ch):** the strongest confectionery-only specialist found. It calls itself the leading Swiss company for exclusive confectionery distribution. It reaches kiosks, forecourts and the organised trade. No sugar free range was found, so Diablo would fill a gap rather than compete.
- **Toga Food SA (togafood.ch):** runs import, sales and marketing in Switzerland for European brand owners. That matches an exporter like B Healthy entering without its own Swiss team. It scouts new brands at ISM and Anuga, so a trade show meeting is a natural opener.
- **Gustav Gerig AG (gerig.ch):** based in Zurich. It imports long-life brands for Swiss retail, with confectionery and health food already in scope. Its brand list is not public, so the first call should confirm there is no competing sugar free line. Conaxess Trade is a strong reach play but already carries NEOH and KETOFABRIK, which overlap with Diablo.

## Barriers to entry

- **Dominant players:** Coop and Migros hold about 80% between them and push private label hard (around 50% of sales). Expect a distributor to want exclusivity for Switzerland, since the market is small and national. Exclusivity norms, listing fees and distributor margins could not be verified for this report.
- **Duty:** Switzerland charges import duty on processed agricultural products such as chocolate, based on the sugar, milk and flour inside. The aim is to price those raw materials at Swiss levels. EU goods pay reduced rates under Protocol 2. UK origin goods should pay no more than EU rates. Exact rates per Diablo product are unverified and need checking in the Swiss tariff.
- **Rules of origin:** UK preference needs UK origin, proved with an EUR1 certificate or an origin declaration. Packing or labelling in the UK does not count. Goods made outside the UK need checking against the product rules.
- **New UK deal:** an enhanced UK-Switzerland trade deal was concluded in August 2026, but it is not yet in force. Reported gains cover dairy, meat and wine. Terms for confectionery are unpublished.
- **Sweeteners:** steviol glycosides (E 960a to E 960d) are permitted for certain foods since the 2023 additives ordinance revision. Whole stevia leaf counts as a novel food. Polyol permissions by product type are unverified against the Swiss annex.
- **Claims:** nutrition claims such as "zuckerfrei" must be listed in Annex 13 of the Swiss labelling ordinance (LIV) and meet its conditions. There is no approval route for new nutrition claims. The exact Swiss wording for "ohne Zuckerzusatz" is unverified.
- **Laxative warning:** a polyol laxative warning is expected, as in the EU, but the Swiss text was not verified.
- **Labelling:** the label must be in at least one official language (German, French or Italian), so German suits Zurich. The country of production must be shown unless the address or product name makes it clear.
- **Shelf life at entry:** no Swiss norm could be verified. Agree minimum remaining shelf life with each distributor.
