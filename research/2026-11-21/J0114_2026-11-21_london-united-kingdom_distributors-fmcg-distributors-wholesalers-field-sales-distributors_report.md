## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks reach London shoppers through two routes. The first is the big grocers (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Aldi, Lidl, Morrisons and Ocado online), which buy centrally into their own depots; Kantar and IGD panel data in 2026 show Lidl drawing level with Morrisons, so the discounters now matter as much as the traditional chains. The second is the large independent and convenience sector (symbol group stores, independents, forecourts, newsagents and ethnic grocers), which London has in unusual density. That sector is supplied mainly by a few delivered and cash and carry wholesalers (Booker, Bestway, Dhamecha, Parfetts, Unitas and Sugro members) and, for confectionery, by specialist sweet and snack wholesalers and van sales operators. The real gatekeepers are therefore the grocers' category buyers and the wholesalers' buying teams and buying groups, which decide which brands reach the symbol fascias. Specialist health, halal and kosher distributors give a narrower route into London's community stores. Diablo is itself UK based (B Healthy Ltd), so for London the task is winning listings, not importing. Share figures change monthly and were not checked in detail for this note.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentrated wholesale: a handful of wholesalers and buying groups control access to most independent and symbol stores; a listing usually needs promotional funding in their promotion cycles (amounts unverified).
- Grocer listings: central buying, range reviews at set times and supplier funded promotions; listing fees and margin terms are confidential and not verified here.
- Distributor terms: UK distributors and van sales operators typically expect a meaningful margin and credit terms; exact norms were not verified in this run.
- HFSS rules (England): location and, since October 2025, volume price promotion restrictions apply to in scope products in larger stores; sugar-free chocolate and biscuits may still be HFSS on fat, so each SKU needs a nutrient profile check.
- Sweeteners: GB assimilated Regulation 1333/2008 sets which sweeteners and polyols are permitted by food category; stevia (steviol glycosides) is permitted in named categories only.
- Laxative warning: products with more than 10% added polyols must carry "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects" (Regulation 1169/2011 Annex III as assimilated in GB).
- Claims: "sugar-free" and "no added sugars" must meet the GB nutrition and health claims conditions; Northern Ireland follows EU rules under the Windsor Framework.
- Labelling and duty: as a UK company Diablo faces no import duty for London; goods made in the EU need a GB importer address on pack and UK and EU origin rules apply (not checked per product).
