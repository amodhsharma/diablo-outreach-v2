## Market structure

Packaged confectionery in South Africa reaches shoppers through two very different routes. Formal retail is led by Shoprite (Checkers and Shoprite), Pick n Pay (including Boxer), Spar (a wholesaler supplying independently owned Spar stores from its own distribution centres) and Woolworths, all buying centrally. Alongside them sits a large independent and informal trade (independent supermarkets, spaza shops, tuck shops, forecourts and street traders) reported as a R268 billion force in FMCG in 2025. That trade buys from cash and carry and wholesale groups (Makro and Jumbo in the Massmart group, Shoprite Cash and Carry, independent cash and carry operators and buying groups) and from van sales and field sales distributors. Johannesburg and wider Gauteng are the hub for this wholesale and distribution network. For an imported premium brand the realistic gatekeepers are (1) a national importer or distributor that already holds listings with the chains and Spar distribution centres, (2) the central category buyers of the chains and (3) the large cash and carry groups for independent trade. This run found that most large Gauteng players are already in Diablo's pipeline (based on this run's channel research, not a market wide figure).

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: a few chains and Spar's distribution centres control formal shelf space; listing and promotional support are generally expected (specific listing fees unverified).
- Informal trade: reaching spaza and township shops needs cash and carry listings or van sales distributors, and is price driven; a premium imported sugar free range fits mainly the formal and upper income channel (judgement, not a verified figure).
- Distributor terms: importers typically ask for exclusivity for South Africa (often with neighbouring SACU countries) and marketing support; margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Sweeteners: use of sweeteners is governed by the Sweeteners in Foodstuffs regulations (R733 of 2012); steviol glycosides and polyols should be checked SKU by SKU against those regulations.
- Labelling and claims: current labelling rules are R146 (2010), which set conditions for nutrient claims; replacement labelling regulations have been published in draft (2023) and may change claim rules and add front of pack warnings. Exact conditions for "sugar free" and "no added sugar" and any polyol laxative statement were not confirmed in this run.
- Import labelling: labels in English with the South African importer's details and a country of origin statement; EU or UK labels usually need a local sticker (details to confirm with the importer).
- Shelf life at entry: sea freight and port clearance mean importers usually want generous remaining shelf life on arrival (exact norms unverified).
- Duty and trade agreement: the SACU and Mozambique UK Economic Partnership Agreement (SACUM UK EPA) carries over the EU SADC EPA terms for UK goods; whether every confectionery and biscuit line enters duty free under it was not confirmed in this run (check the SACUM preferential tariff document). VAT of 15% applies.
