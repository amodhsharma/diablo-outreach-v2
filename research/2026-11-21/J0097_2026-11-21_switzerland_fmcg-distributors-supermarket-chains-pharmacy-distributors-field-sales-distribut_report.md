## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in Switzerland reach shoppers mainly through the two cooperatives Coop and Migros, which press reports say now sit close together at the top of food retail, followed by Denner (Migros group), Aldi, Lidl and Volg. Both cooperatives buy centrally and import many brands directly, so they are the real gatekeepers for grocery shelf space. Around them sit a few large wholesalers and cash and carry groups (Transgourmet and Prodega for Coop, Aligro, Saviva for Migros), kiosk and convenience suppliers (Valora and its network) and brand importers that hold stock and run field sales to independents, petrol stations and kiosks. In pharmacies and drugstores Galenica (Amavita, Sun Store, Coop Vitality, wholesaler Galexis) dominates, with Benu and the wholesalers Voigt and Amedis behind it. Imported sugar-free ranges usually enter through a Swiss importer that registers articles, translates labels and sells into these central buyers. Exact market shares are not verified here. Online, Galaxus, coop.ch and migros.ch matter for niche dietary ranges.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Coop and Migros (with Denner) hold most grocery shelf space and own several of the big wholesalers; in health retail Galenica controls the largest chains and the main wholesale route. Listing fees and promotion budgets are usual but exact levels are unverified.
- Importer route: most chains expect a Swiss partner to hold stock, register articles, handle returns and fund promotions. Distributor margin and credit norms are not verified.
- Labelling: food must carry mandatory information in at least one official language (in practice German, French and Italian for national chains) and a Swiss or responsible importer address. Products made to EU rules can use the Cassis de Dijon route, which needs an FSVO authorisation for food.
- Sweeteners and polyols: Swiss additive rules broadly mirror EU lists (steviol glycosides, polyols); products with more than 10% added polyols need the laxative effect warning, as in the EU. Check product by product.
- Claims: "sugar free" and "no added sugars" follow the FSVO nutrition claims list, which matches EU conditions. Health claims need authorisation unless already on the Swiss list.
- Duty: Switzerland removed tariffs on industrial goods in 2024, but chocolate, biscuits and other processed farm products still carry an agricultural component duty unless UK origin qualifies under the UK Switzerland trade agreement; exact rates for each line are unverified.
- Shelf life: chains and wholesalers expect a long remaining shelf life at delivery; the exact minimum is not published and should be confirmed with each buyer.
Sources: [FSVO nutrition and health claims](https://www.blv.admin.ch/en/nutrition-and-health-claims), [SME portal: food labelling principles](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/kmu/en/home/concrete-know-how/sme-management/labeling/food-labeling.html), [FSVO Cassis de Dijon principle](https://www.blv.admin.ch/en/the-cassis-de-dijon-principle-food), [GOV.UK: trade with Switzerland](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/summary-of-the-uk-switzerland-trade-agreement), [SECO: free trade agreement United Kingdom](https://www.seco.admin.ch/en/free-trade-agreement-united-kingdom-uk)
