## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in Australia reach shelf through a highly concentrated grocery channel. Woolworths and Coles hold the largest share of grocery sales, with Aldi third (exact shares vary by source and are not verified here). Both majors buy centrally through state distribution centres, so the gatekeeper for Brisbane and Queensland is the national category buyer, not a local store. Independent supermarkets (IGA, Foodworks and others) are supplied mainly by Metcash, which is the dominant grocery wholesaler. Impulse and convenience outlets (petrol stations, newsagents, milk bars, pharmacies and vending) are served by field sales and convenience distributors. Metcash bought Confectionery Distributors (CDS, trading as The Distributors), a large confectionery and convenience field sales network, which the ACCC reviewed; this puts much of the independent route under one group. For an imported range, the realistic entry is an importer or distributor who holds the brand nationally, then sells into Metcash, the independent and convenience trade and, later, a major chain. Brisbane itself has few large independent wholesalers left outside these groups, and most of the big ones are already known to Diablo.

## Barriers to entry

- Channel concentration: Coles and Woolworths dominate national grocery and buy centrally; Metcash controls most independent grocery wholesale and, through The Distributors, a large share of convenience field sales.
- Supermarket terms: the Food and Grocery Code of Conduct became mandatory on 1 April 2025 and governs payments such as listing and promotional fees; it does not ban them, so expect listing, promotion and range review costs (amounts not verified).
- Distributor terms: importers usually ask for national exclusivity and margins that cover marketing support; typical margin and credit levels were not verified in this research.
- Sweeteners: steviol glycosides, erythritol, maltitol and other polyols are permitted additives under Schedule 15 of the Food Standards Code, but permission depends on food category, so each recipe should be checked against the schedule.
- Claims: "sugar free" and "no added sugar" claims are nutrient content claims under Standard 1.2.7 with set conditions (to be checked recipe by recipe; the exact thresholds were not verified here).
- Laxative warning: foods with added polyols above a set level must carry an advisory statement that excess consumption may have a laxative effect (threshold to be confirmed in the current Code).
- Labelling: the label must meet the Food Standards Code in English, including an Australian or New Zealand importer name and address, a nutrition information panel per 100 g and per serve, allergen declarations in the plain English format and country of origin labelling. UK labels will need a sticker or reprint.
- Shelf life: importers and retailers generally want a good share of shelf life left at entry; the exact norm was not verified.
- Import duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement came into force on 31 May 2023 and removed tariffs on UK origin goods, subject to the rules of origin; imported food also faces Imported Food Inspection Scheme checks and GST.
