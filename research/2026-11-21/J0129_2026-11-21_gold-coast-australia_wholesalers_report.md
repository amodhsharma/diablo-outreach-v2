## Market structure

Packaged confectionery in Australia reaches shelf mainly through Woolworths and Coles, which buy centrally and supply stores from their own distribution centres, followed by Aldi (direct import and private label heavy) and the independent sector. Gold Coast stores are supplied from South East Queensland distribution centres rather than from the city itself: Metcash, which supplies IGA and other independent supermarkets, runs Queensland grocery warehousing in the Brisbane region (Crestmead) and has field sales roles on the Gold Coast. Impulse channels (petrol and convenience, newsagents, pharmacies, tourist and resort shops, vending) are served by national wholesale and convenience distributors with Brisbane or Gold Coast depots, and Gold Coast based wholesalers are mostly foodservice suppliers to hospitality. For a new imported brand the realistic gatekeepers are (1) a national or Queensland importer or distributor that already holds supermarket and independent listings, (2) the Metcash buying office for Queensland independents and (3) the central category buyers at Woolworths and Coles. This run found that the large wholesalers serving the Gold Coast are already in Diablo's pipeline, so new partners there are few (based on this run's channel research, not a market wide figure). Online grocery is led by the same chains.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Woolworths and Coles dominate grocery; ranging reviews are national and central, so a Gold Coast entry usually means a national importer or a Metcash listing rather than city level selling.
- Grocery code: supplier dealings with the large retailers are governed by the Food and Grocery Code, now overseen by a Code Supervisor; promotional funding and listing support are commonly expected (exact fees unverified and negotiated case by case).
- Distributor terms: importers typically take exclusivity for Australia (often with New Zealand) and expect marketing support; specific margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- "No added sugar" claim (Schedule 4): no added sugar ingredient, and the food must contain no more than 10 g sugars per 100 g (solid). Products above that cannot carry the claim even with no sugar added.
- "Sugar free" (the "free" descriptor in Schedule 4) requires the food to meet the low sugar condition (no more than 5 g per 100 g for solids) plus the general conditions for "free" claims; check each SKU.
- "Unsweetened" cannot be used on any product containing intense sweeteners or polyols such as erythritol, maltitol, isomalt or xylitol.
- Polyol warning: products with added polyols at or above a set level need the "excess consumption may have a laxative effect" advisory statement under Standard 1.2.3 (threshold not confirmed in this run; check the current Standard).
- Sweeteners: stevia (steviol glycosides) and polyols are permitted additives in confectionery under Schedule 15; inulin is treated as a food ingredient. Each SKU's sweetener and level should be checked against Schedule 15.
- Sugar definition change: Amendment 233 changed the definition of sugars used for claims, with transition to 29 October 2028; labels must comply by then.
- Import labelling: labels in English with an Australian importer's name and address, country of origin statement, Australian allergen format (PEAL) and Nutrition Information Panel per 100 g and per serve; EU or UK labels need an Australian sticker or reprint.
- Shelf life at entry: long shipping and border inspection mean importers usually want generous remaining shelf life on arrival (exact norms unverified).
- Duty and trade agreement: the Australia UK Free Trade Agreement came into force on 31 May 2023 and removes Australian tariffs on UK goods, including confectionery, so B Healthy Ltd (UK) shipments of UK origin goods can enter duty free with proof of origin; 10% GST applies to confectionery and snacks.
