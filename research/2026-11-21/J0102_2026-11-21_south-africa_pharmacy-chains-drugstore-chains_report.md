## Market structure

South African grocery retail is concentrated in a handful of national groups (Shoprite, Pick n Pay, SPAR and Woolworths), which buy centrally into their own distribution centres. Health and beauty retail is a near duopoly of Clicks and Dis-Chem, both of which carry a healthy snacking and food aisle in their front shop and list suppliers through a central buying team. Clicks Group also owns the pharmaceutical wholesaler UPD, so the main pharmacy chains control much of their own supply chain. Smaller pharmacy banners such as Alpha Pharm and Link are networks of independent owners rather than chains with a central warehouse, so they are reached through wholesalers rather than one buying office. A large informal and traditional trade (spaza shops, wholesalers and cash and carry) carries a big share of everyday snacks, but a premium sugar free range is more likely to sell through modern retail, pharmacy and online. For an imported range the usual route is an importer or distributor that holds stock, handles labelling and sells into the chains. Figures for channel shares were not verified in this run.

## Barriers to entry

- Dominant players: Clicks and Dis-Chem lock up most of the pharmacy and health retail channel and both are already on Diablo's exclusion list, so few new big pharmacy or drugstore chains remain.
- Listing terms: the chains list centrally and expect suppliers to fund promotions and rebates; exact listing fees and margins were not verified in this run.
- Sweeteners: permitted sweeteners and their use are set by the Sweeteners in Foodstuffs regulations (R733 of 2012); stevia (steviol glycosides) should be checked against the permitted list and limits for each product type (unverified per category).
- Labelling and claims: R146 of 2010 governs labelling and claims such as "sugar free" and "no added sugar"; draft regulation R3337 of 2023 would add front of pack warning labels and tighten claims, but it had not been finalised at the time of the sources found (status unverified).
- Polyols: a laxative warning for products high in polyols is expected under the labelling rules; the exact wording and threshold were not verified.
- Import labelling: labels must be in English and meet South African rules, which usually means stickering or relabelling by the importer.
- Trade agreements: the SACUM UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives UK goods preferential access to the Southern African Customs Union; the duty rate for each confectionery and biscuit tariff line was not verified.
