## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in Switzerland reach shoppers mainly through the two cooperatives Migros and Coop, then Denner, Aldi, Lidl and kiosks (Valora). In the health channel the gatekeeper is Galenica: it owns the Amavita and Sun Store pharmacy chains, runs Coop Vitality jointly with Coop and supplies most pharmacies and drugstores through its wholesaler Galexis and its logistics arm Alloga. Benu (part of Phoenix, supplied by Amedis) is the second owned chain. Many other pharmacies and drugstores belong to groups of independent owners (TopPharm, Rotpunkt, pharmacieplus, Dropa) that negotiate listings but buy through the pharma wholesalers (Galexis, Voigt, Amedis) rather than a central food warehouse. For a sugar-free snack brand that means one listing decision at Galenica's central buying or at a wholesaler opens hundreds of outlets, while independent groups only follow once a product is in the wholesaler catalogue. Pharmacy share of free sale food is small and press reports describe pharmacies taking ground from traditional drugstores (unverified figures). Online, Galenica's own shops, Zur Rose and general marketplaces (Galaxus, Coop and Migros online) matter for niche dietary ranges.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Galenica (Amavita, Sun Store, Coop Vitality, Galexis) controls the largest owned chains and the main wholesale route, so one central buying team is the real door. Listing fees and promotion budgets are usual but exact levels are unverified.
- Wholesaler route: groups of independents buy through Galexis, Voigt or Amedis; a supplier usually needs a Swiss partner to hold stock, register articles and handle returns. Margin and credit norms not verified.
- Labelling: food must carry mandatory information in at least one official language (in practice German, French and Italian for national chains) and a Swiss or responsible importer address. Products made to EU rules can use the Cassis de Dijon route, which needs an FSVO authorisation for food.
- Sweeteners and polyols: Swiss additive rules broadly mirror EU lists (steviol glycosides, polyols); products with more than 10% added polyols need the "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects" warning, as in the EU. Treat product by product checks as needed.
- Claims: "sugar free" and "no added sugars" follow the FSVO nutrition claims list, which matches EU conditions (0.5 g per 100 g; no added sugars or sugary ingredients). Health claims need prior authorisation unless already on the Swiss list.
- Duty: Switzerland removed tariffs on industrial goods in 2024, but chocolate, biscuits and other processed farm products still carry an agricultural component duty unless UK origin qualifies under the UK Switzerland trade agreement; the enhanced agreement is still being negotiated, so exact rates for each line are unverified.
- Shelf life: chains and wholesalers typically expect a long remaining shelf life at delivery; the exact minimum is not published and should be confirmed with each buyer.
