## Market structure

In the United Kingdom packaged confectionery and snacks reach shoppers mainly through the big grocers (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Aldi, Morrisons, Lidl), which buy direct from brands or their UK agents. The second route is the impulse channel: tens of thousands of convenience stores, newsagents and forecourts supplied by delivered and cash and carry wholesalers (Booker, Bestway, Parfetts, Dhamecha and regional members of the Federation of Wholesale Distributors) and by symbol groups that tie independents to one supplier. Specialist confectionery wholesalers and brand importers sit between foreign brands and these wholesalers, holding stock and running field sales. Ethnic and halal retail is served by its own cash and carries, many in London, Birmingham, Leicester and Manchester. Wholesaler buyers and symbol group category managers are the real gatekeepers outside the grocers. Exact channel shares were not verified in this run (the search budget ran out).

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: most large wholesalers and symbol groups are already on Diablo's list; the open space is smaller importers and regional players. Listing fees, promotion funding and wholesaler margins apply but levels were not verified.
- HFSS rules (England): restrictions on location and volume price promotions of less healthy food apply to medium and large businesses; Wales has its own rules. Sugar-free products may still be in scope depending on the nutrient profiling score, so check each line.
- HFSS advertising: restrictions on TV and paid online advertising of less healthy food also apply; check whether brand advertising exemptions fit.
- Sweeteners and polyols: GB keeps the assimilated additives regulation (1333/2008) and the labelling rules of 1169/2011, including the laxative effect warning for products with more than 10% added polyols.
- Claims: "sugar free" and "no added sugars" follow the GB nutrition claims register (assimilated 1924/2006); reforms have been consulted on, outcome not verified here.
- Duty: Diablo's exporter is a UK company, so no import duty applies for UK sales; goods made outside the UK may face tariffs (not verified per line).
Sources: [GOV.UK: less healthy food advertising and promotions restrictions](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/less-healthy-food-or-drink-advertising-and-promotions-restrictions), [GOV.UK: HFSS promotions implementation guidance](https://gov.uk/government/publications/restricting-promotions-of-products-high-in-fat-sugar-or-salt-by-location-and-by-volume-price/restricting-promotions-of-products-high-in-fat-sugar-or-salt-by-location-and-by-volume-price-implementation-guidance), [Welsh Government HFSS guidance](https://www.gov.wales/restricting-promotion-high-fat-salt-and-sugar-foods-implementation-guidance-part-1-html), [legislation.gov.uk: Regulation 1169/2011 annexes](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/1169/annexes/2020-12-31), [GOV.UK: nutrition and health claims guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/nutrition-and-health-claims-guidance-to-compliance-with-regulation-ec-1924-2006-on-nutrition-and-health-claims-made-on-foods/nutrition-and-health-claims-guidance-to-compliance-with-regulation-ec-19242006)
