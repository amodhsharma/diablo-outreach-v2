## Market structure

Zurich is Switzerland's largest consumer market, but packaged confectionery and snacks reach its shelves through national structures rather than city ones. The cooperatives Coop and Migros (with Denner) buy centrally and press reports put them close together at the top of food retail, followed by Aldi, Lidl and Volg. They are the real gatekeepers and import many brands directly. Around them sit the large wholesale and cash and carry groups (Transgourmet and Prodega for Coop, Saviva for Migros, Aligro, TopCC), the kiosk and convenience network run by Valora and brand importers that hold stock and send field sales to independents, petrol stations and kiosks. Kiosk wholesale has narrowed since Lekkerland Schweiz, once Valora's supplier, was wound up in 2020. Most of the big Zurich wholesalers and distributors are already in Diablo's contact base, so new partners in the city are mostly small importers. Exact market shares are unverified here. ## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Coop and Migros (with Denner) hold most grocery shelf space and own several of the big wholesalers. Listing fees and promotion budgets are usual but exact levels are unverified.
- Importer route: chains expect a Swiss partner to hold stock, register articles, handle returns and fund promotions. Distributor margin and credit norms are not verified.
- Labelling: food must carry mandatory information in at least one official language (German for Zurich, all three for national chains) and a Swiss or responsible importer address. Products made to EU rules can use the Cassis de Dijon route, which needs an FSVO authorisation for food.
- Sweeteners and polyols: Swiss additive rules broadly mirror EU lists (steviol glycosides, polyols); products with more than 10% added polyols need the laxative effect warning, as in the EU. Check product by product.
- Claims: "sugar free" and "no added sugars" follow the FSVO nutrition claims list, which matches EU conditions.
- Duty: tariffs on industrial goods went in 2024, but chocolate, biscuits and other processed farm products still carry an agricultural component duty unless UK origin qualifies under the UK Switzerland trade agreement; exact rates are unverified.
- Shelf life: buyers expect a long remaining shelf life at delivery; the minimum is not published and should be confirmed with each buyer.
