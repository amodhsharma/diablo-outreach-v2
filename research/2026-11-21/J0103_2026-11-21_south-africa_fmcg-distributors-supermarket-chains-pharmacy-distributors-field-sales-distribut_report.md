## Market structure

South African grocery retail is concentrated in a handful of national groups (Shoprite, Pick n Pay, SPAR and Woolworths), which buy centrally into their own distribution centres. Health and beauty retail is a near duopoly of Clicks and Dis-Chem, and the main pharmacy wholesalers belong to the big retail groups (UPD to Clicks, for example), so the chains control much of their own supply. A large informal and traditional trade (spaza shops, independent supermarkets, wholesalers and cash and carry) carries a big share of everyday snacks and is served by wholesalers and field sales distributors with van and rep networks. A premium sugar free range is more likely to sell through modern retail, pharmacy and online first. For an imported range the usual route is an importer or distributor that holds stock, handles labelling and sells into the chains and the wholesale trade. Figures for channel shares were not verified in this run.

## Barriers to entry

- Dominant players: the big grocery groups and Clicks and Dis-Chem buy centrally and own much of their logistics, and the pharmacy wholesalers mostly belong to these groups, so independent distributors with national reach are few.
- Listing terms: the chains list centrally and expect suppliers to fund promotions and rebates; exact listing fees, distributor margins and credit terms were not verified in this run.
- Sweeteners: permitted sweeteners and their use are set by the Sweeteners in Foodstuffs regulations (R733 of 2012); stevia (steviol glycosides) should be checked against the permitted list and limits for each product type (unverified per category).
- Labelling and claims: R146 of 2010 governs labelling and claims such as "sugar free" and "no added sugar"; draft regulation R3337 of 2023 would add front of pack warning labels and tighten claims, but it had not been finalised at the time of the sources found (status unverified).
- Polyols: a laxative warning for products high in polyols is expected under the labelling rules; the exact wording and threshold were not verified.
- Import labelling: labels must be in English and meet South African rules, which usually means stickering or relabelling by the importer.
- Trade agreements: the SACUM UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives UK goods preferential access to the Southern African Customs Union; the duty rate for each confectionery and biscuit tariff line was not verified.
Sources: [Sweeteners in Foodstuffs R733 of 2012](https://www.health.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Sweeteners-in-Foodstuffs-R-733-of-2012.pdf), [Georgetown Global Food Laws: R146](https://globalfoodlaws.georgetown.edu/documents/no-r146/), [USDA FAS: South Africa issues new draft regulation on food labelling](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/south-africa-south-africa-issues-new-draft-regulation-food-labelling), [GOV.UK: Summary of the SACUM UK EPA](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/summary-of-the-sacum-uk-economic-partnership-agreement-epa)
