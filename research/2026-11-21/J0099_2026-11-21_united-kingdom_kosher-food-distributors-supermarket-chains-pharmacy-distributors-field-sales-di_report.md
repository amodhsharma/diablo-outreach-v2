## Market structure

The United Kingdom is Diablo's home market: the exporting entity, B Healthy Ltd, is a UK company, so product moves domestically rather than as an import. Most packaged confectionery and snacks reach shoppers through the big grocery multiples (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Aldi, Lidl, Morrisons and the Co-op), where buying is central and Tesco alone takes roughly one in three supermarket transactions according to Worldpanel data reported in 2026. Lidl overtook Morrisons as the fifth largest grocer in 2026. The second route is the independent and symbol convenience trade (Spar, Nisa, Costcutter and similar), served by delivered and cash and carry wholesalers and buying groups such as Unitas, which act as the gatekeepers for thousands of small shops. Pharmacy and health retail is concentrated in a few chains (Boots and Superdrug above all) supplied by full line pharmaceutical wholesalers. Most of the large wholesalers, chains and pharmacy distributors are already in Diablo's contact list, so this run found few new names. Online, Ocado and the multiples' own sites matter; marketplace channels were not researched in this job.

## Barriers to entry

- Supermarket listings: the Groceries Supply Code of Practice, enforced by the Groceries Code Adjudicator, limits when the largest retailers may ask for payments for listing or shelf position, but such payments are still allowed in some cases; actual fee levels were not verified.
- Gatekeepers: the multiples buy centrally and range reviews are infrequent; in convenience, the symbol groups and buying groups decide what their members can order. Typical distributor margins and credit terms were not verified in this run.
- HFSS rules: England restricts the placement and volume promotion of less healthy food in larger stores, with similar rules in Wales and Scotland; whether each Diablo product is in scope depends on its nutrient profile score and should be checked product by product.
- Claims: "sugar free" (no more than 0.5 g sugars per 100 g) and "with no added sugars" (no added mono or disaccharides or sugar-sweetened ingredients, with "contains naturally occurring sugars" where relevant) follow the retained Regulation 1924/2006 in Great Britain.
- Labelling: foods with more than 10% added polyols must carry "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects" (retained Regulation 1169/2011, Annex III); permitted sweeteners and polyols by food category follow the retained additives rules, not checked line by line here.
- Northern Ireland: goods moving from Great Britain to Northern Ireland fall under the Windsor Framework; the planned GB wide "Not for EU" labelling was later dropped, but the current NI retail movement rules should be checked before shipping there.
- Import duty: not applicable for UK made or UK held stock sold domestically; any goods brought in from the EU fall under the UK and EU Trade and Cooperation Agreement rules of origin (not checked in this run).
