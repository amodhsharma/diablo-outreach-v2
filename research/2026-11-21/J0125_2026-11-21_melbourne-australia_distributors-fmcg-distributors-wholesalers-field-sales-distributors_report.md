## Market structure

Packaged confectionery in Australia reaches shelf mainly through the big supermarket groups. Woolworths and Coles hold most of the grocery market, with ALDI a clear third and the independent sector (IGA, Foodworks and others) supplied largely through Metcash, which also runs Campbells cash and carry. Woolworths and Coles buy centrally into their own distribution centres, so a ranging decision by a national category buyer is the real gate. Petrol stations and convenience stores (often called servos) are an important impulse channel for confectionery and are supplied by national route to market groups and field sales distributors such as The Distributors and Accredited Distributors. Specialist importers and distributors (for example Stuart Alexander, DKSH and confectionery specialists such as All Fect) act as the brand's agent, handling import, warehousing, sales and merchandising across these channels. Health food shops, pharmacies and online sellers are smaller but more open routes for a sugar free range. In Melbourne most of these groups run Victorian depots, but buying decisions sit with national head offices, so a Melbourne partner usually means a national partner. Exact market shares were not verified in this run.

## Barriers to entry

- Channel concentration: Woolworths and Coles dominate and buy nationally; ranging reviews are infrequent and suppliers are commonly expected to fund promotions. Specific listing fee levels were not verified.
- Distributor lock up: the large national FMCG and confectionery distributors already carry competing international brands and tend to want national exclusivity; margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Claims: nutrition content claims such as "sugar free" and "no added sugar" are governed by FSANZ Standard 1.2.7, which sets conditions for each claim. The exact thresholds should be checked against the current text before printing labels.
- Laxative warning: foods containing polyols above set levels need an advisory statement that excessive consumption may have a laxative effect (FSANZ advisory statements). Thresholds should be confirmed for each product.
- Sweeteners: stevia (steviol glycosides) and polyols are permitted in Australia under the Food Standards Code, but permitted use by food category was not verified in this run.
- Labelling: imports must carry Australian compliant labels (nutrition information panel in Australian format, allergen declarations and country of origin rules). Relabelling of UK packs is likely to be needed.
- Shelf life: distributors and retailers usually expect a good share of shelf life left on arrival; the exact norm was not verified.
- Duty: the Australia UK Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removes tariffs on most UK goods, with UK government material citing confectionery as a beneficiary; confirm the rate for each HS code and UK origin rules with the importer.
