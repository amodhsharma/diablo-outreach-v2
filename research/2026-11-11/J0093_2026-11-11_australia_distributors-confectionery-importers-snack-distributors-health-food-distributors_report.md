## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: Woolworths and Coles together hold close to two thirds of grocery sales, with Aldi, the Metcash supplied independents (IGA, Foodland) and Costco making up most of the rest. Packaged confectionery and snacks reach these shelves either direct from large manufacturers (Mondelez, Mars, Nestle, Ferrero, Lindt) or, for imported and smaller brands, through national importer distributors that hold the supermarket listings, run merchandising teams and clear goods through customs. Those importer distributors are the real gatekeepers for a foreign brand: most of the large confectionery, specialty and British food importers already sit on Diablo's exclusion list, which suggests they have been approached before. A second route runs through convenience and route trade wholesalers (petrol and convenience, independent grocers), and a third through health food distributors that serve health food shops and pharmacies. Online, Amazon AU and the supermarkets' own sites matter, but range there still usually follows the bricks and mortar listing. Exact current market shares were not verified for this run.

## Barriers to entry

- Supermarket power: two chains decide most volume; suppliers face pressure on terms, own label substitution and delisting risk (Bain). Listing fees and promotional funding are common, but current levels were not verified.
- Importer distributors usually want exclusivity for Australia (often with New Zealand) and work on importer plus distributor margins; exact margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Dominant players: the multinational confectioners and a handful of national importers hold most shelf space and the merchandising reach into independents.
- Claims: FSANZ has gazetted a definition of added sugars for "no added sugar" claims, listing twelve sources (including glucose syrup, honey, malt and D-tagatose) with a four year transition plus two years stock in trade. Each Diablo recipe needs checking against it; whether inulin derived ingredients count was not verified.
- "Sugar free" claims fall under Schedule 4 of the Food Standards Code (low sugar thresholds); exact wording to be checked with a regulatory adviser.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted under Standard 1.3.1; foods with added polyols above set levels need the advisory statement that excess consumption may have a laxative effect (Standard 1.2.3). Steviol glycosides are permitted. Thresholds not re verified in this run.
- Labelling: Australian importer name and address, Australian country of origin labelling, Nutrition Information Panel per serve and per 100g, and Precautionary Allergen Labelling (PEAL format) are required; UK packs will need a sticker or local artwork.
- Import: goods pass Department of Agriculture biosecurity and the Imported Food Inspection Scheme; shelf life at entry is a commercial rather than legal requirement (importers typically ask for most of the life remaining, unverified).
- Duty: the Australia UK Free Trade Agreement (in force 31 May 2023) removed Australian tariffs on UK goods, so UK origin confectionery should enter duty free subject to rules of origin; GST of 10% still applies.
