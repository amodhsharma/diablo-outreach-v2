## Market structure

Packaged confectionery in South Africa reaches shoppers through two routes. The formal route runs through the national grocery chains (Shoprite and Checkers, Pick n Pay, SPAR, Woolworths and Massmart), which buy centrally or through their own distribution centres, and through the Clicks and Dis-Chem health and pharmacy chains. The informal route runs through independent wholesalers and cash and carries that supply about 150,000 spaza shops, a market Trade Intelligence put at about R197 billion at retail and R259 billion at wholesale in 2024; Shoprite's Usave and Cash & Carry formats compete hard here. Large suppliers such as Tiger Brands are now going direct to informal traders. Gqeberha in the Eastern Cape is served mainly by the national chains' regional distribution and by local independent wholesalers, cash and carries, SPAR franchisees, florists, hampers and gift shops; we found no figures specific to the city (unverified). Seasonal and gift shops in Gqeberha are mostly small owner-run florists and hamper businesses, so the real gatekeepers for an imported sugar-free range are the national chain buyers, the health chains and the importers and distributors that supply independents.

## Barriers to entry

- Chain listings: national chains buy centrally and typically expect listing support, promotional spend and rebates; we could not verify current fee levels, so treat them as unverified.
- Dominant players: Shoprite, Pick n Pay, SPAR and Woolworths dominate formal grocery; local brand owners such as Tiger Brands and AVI hold strong biscuit and confectionery shelf space.
- Distributor terms: independent wholesalers and importers usually work on credit terms and margins we could not verify for this category (unverified).
- Sweeteners: Regulation R.733 of 2012 allows only sweeteners listed in the Codex GSFA; steviol glycosides must be labelled "Steviol Glycosides" or "Steviol Extract" and non-nutritive sweeteners named with "non-nutritive sweetener".
- Laxative warning: foods with more than 50 g/kg of polyols must state "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect".
- Labelling and claims: R.146 is the labelling regulation in force; the draft R.3337 (April 2023) would add front-of-pack warnings for added sugar and some sweeteners and limit claims on warned products, but was still not final in 2026. Exact sugar-free and no-added-sugar claim conditions should be confirmed under R.146 (unverified here).
- Import duty: the SACUM-UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives UK goods gradual tariff liberalisation with EUR1 proof of origin and cumulation with EU materials, but sensitive agricultural lines can face quotas or safeguards; confirm the duty for each HS code before quoting.
- Shelf life and import labelling: products must carry English labelling to R.146; specific minimum shelf life at entry rules were not verified.
