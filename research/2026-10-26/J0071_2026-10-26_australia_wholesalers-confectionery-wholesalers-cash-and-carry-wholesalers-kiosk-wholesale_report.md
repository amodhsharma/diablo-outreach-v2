## Market structure

Australian grocery is a tight oligopoly. Woolworths and Coles hold about 67% of national supermarket sales, Aldi 9% and Metcash-supplied independents (IGA and similar) 7% (ACCC). The chains buy largely direct, so for an overseas brand the real gatekeeper is the category buyer, reached through a local importer or distributor that holds stock, owns compliance and funds promotions. Metcash is the pivot of the wholesale route. It supplies IGA independents and, through its foodservice and convenience division (Campbells, C-Store Distribution, Superior Foods), convenience banners including roughly 600 Ampol Foodary sites, BP, FoodWorks, Friendly Grocer and 7-Eleven in Western Australia. Campbells is the trade wholesale arm; its exact cash and carry footprint is unverified. Below Metcash, regional and specialist confectionery wholesalers serve newsagents, kiosks, pharmacies, cinemas and corner shops. This tier is fragmented and I could not verify a reliable list of leaders. Online, the majors' own sites dominate grocery delivery. Quick commerce is thin: Milkrun closed in April 2023 and Woolworths bought the brand for its own rapid delivery service. Amazon Australia and health retailers are plausible secondary routes, but their share for confectionery is unverified.

## Barriers to entry

- **Dominant players:** Coles and Woolworths together control about two thirds of supermarket sales. Metcash controls most independent and convenience wholesale, so losing one Metcash listing closes much of the non-major channel.
- **Retailer fees and terms:** Suppliers report being required to fund promotional rebates and use retailer-specified advertising and transport, and say retailers change terms over time (ACCC). Specific listing fee levels are unverified.
- **Food and Grocery Code:** Mandatory since 1 April 2025 for grocery retailers and wholesalers with revenue over A$5bn (Aldi, Coles, Metcash, Woolworths). It requires written supply agreements and good faith dealing, with penalties up to A$10m or more. This helps suppliers but does not remove fees.
- **Exclusivity, distributor margin and credit:** Australian importers commonly expect national exclusivity, but typical margin and payment terms could not be verified. Confirm with each partner.
- **Quick commerce:** Little independent quick commerce remains after Milkrun's 2023 closure. Fees on Uber Eats and DoorDash grocery are unverified.
- **Sweeteners:** FSANZ has approved steviol glycosides as a food ingredient since 2008. Permitted maximum levels by product type sit in Schedule 15 of the Food Standards Code and were not verified here. Check each SKU, especially syrups and spreads.
- **Laxative warning:** Standard 1.2.3 requires "excess consumption may have a laxative effect" at 10 g/100 g or more of lactitol, maltitol, maltitol syrup, mannitol or xylitol, or 25 g/100 g or more of erythritol, isomalt, polydextrose or sorbitol. Mixed combinations trigger it at 10 g/100 g. I found no such rule for inulin.
- **No added sugar:** Amendment 224 (December 2023) bans the claim where a food contains any added sugar ingredient (including honey, malt and glucose syrup) or exceeds 10 g sugars/100 g for solids or 7.5 g/100 mL for liquids. There is a 48 month transition from 13 December 2023. Fruit-based spreads and high-sugar chocolate may fail the total sugars cap.
- **Sugar free:** The Code sets no threshold. The ACCC requires sugar to be completely absent under consumer law, so "sugar free" on products with trace or naturally present sugars is a risk. This is a secondary source; confirm.
- **Import labelling:** Country of origin rules apply to imports. Confectionery and biscuits are non-priority foods, so a plain statement such as "Made in the UK" suffices. Australian importer details, English labelling and Imported Food Inspection Scheme checks were not verified here. Assume UK labels need relabelling or stickering.
- **Shelf life at entry:** No published rule found. Minimum remaining shelf life is set commercially by each importer or retailer and is unverified.
- **Duty and trade agreements:** The Australia-UK FTA entered into force on 31 May 2023. It removed tariffs on 98% of UK imports at once, with the rest gone within six years, so B Healthy Ltd (UK) shipments should enter duty free with a Declaration of Origin, if the goods meet UK rules of origin. The EU-Australia FTA was concluded in March 2026 but is not yet in force. Goods from any EU plant would still pay Australian general duty (rate unverified). GST at 10% is likely to apply to confectionery (unverified).
