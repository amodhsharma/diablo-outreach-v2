## Market structure

Warning: the web search budget for this run was used up by the Channel helpers before the Location research could start, so this section is written from background knowledge only and is unverified. Check it before relying on it.

South African grocery is concentrated in a few national chains (Shoprite Checkers, Pick n Pay, SPAR, Woolworths) that buy centrally and mostly list imported brands through a local importer or distributor rather than direct from a foreign exporter. SPAR runs through regional distribution centres, including one serving KwaZulu-Natal, which makes its KZN guild a possible regional gatekeeper (unverified). Health and specialty ranges also reach shoppers through Dis-Chem and Clicks, independent health shops and online stores such as Takealot and Checkers Sixty60. Durban is the country's main container port, so many importers clear goods there before moving them inland to Gauteng. The real gatekeepers for a foreign sugar-free brand are likely the importer or distributor who holds the listing and the chain category buyers.

## Barriers to entry

- Listing fees, rebates and promotional contributions with the national chains are common; amounts are not verified here.
- Distributor margin and credit terms for imported confectionery are not verified here.
- Labelling falls under the Foodstuffs, Cosmetics and Disinfectants Act regulations (R146); a newer labelling regulation has been in draft for some years and its status is unverified.
- Sweetener and polyol permissions and sugar-free claim rules need checking against the current South African regulations; not verified in this run.
- A laxative warning for polyols may be required; unverified.
- Products with added sugar may fall under the Health Promotion Levy (sugar tax), which as far as known applies to sugary beverages, not confectionery; unverified.
- UK goods may enter under the SACUM-UK Economic Partnership Agreement and EU goods under the SADC-EU EPA; duty rates for confectionery are not verified here.
