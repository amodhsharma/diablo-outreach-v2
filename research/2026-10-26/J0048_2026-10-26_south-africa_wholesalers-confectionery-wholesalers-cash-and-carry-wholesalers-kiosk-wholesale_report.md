## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in South Africa reach shoppers through two routes. The first is a few national grocery chains (Shoprite and Checkers, Pick n Pay, Spar and Woolworths), which buy centrally or through their own distribution centres and decide which brands get shelf space; Spar's independent store owners, supplied from regional distribution centres, are a second layer of gatekeeper. The second route, the one these wholesale Channels serve, is the independent trade: cash and carry wholesalers and buying groups supply independent grocers, forecourt shops, kiosks and the large informal spaza (tuck shop) trade in the townships, often with van sales. Spaza shops have had to register with their local municipality since late 2024 after food safety incidents, which may push owners towards buying from established, traceable wholesalers (unverified). For an imported niche brand like Diablo, the realistic gatekeeper in this route is a wholesaler or importer that already supplies independents and can handle registration and labelling. Shares of each route were not verified; this run reused the market notes from job J0047 (South Africa, same day) because the run's web search budget was used up by the Channel research.

## Barriers to entry

- Chain power: the Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry looked at rebates, fees and supplier terms charged by the large chains and found these can shut out small suppliers. Exact listing fee and rebate levels were not verified.
- Wholesaler margin, credit and exclusivity norms were not verified; expect importers and wholesalers to ask for exclusivity and marketing support (unverified).
- Sweeteners: only sweeteners listed in the Codex GSFA may be used, at GSFA maximum levels (R733 of 2012). Whether each Diablo polyol is allowed in each food category should be checked against the GSFA category entries.
- Sweetener labelling: non nutritive sweeteners must be named in the ingredients followed by "(non-nutritive sweetener)"; stevia must be labelled "Steviol Glycosides" or "Steviol Extract".
- Laxative warning: products with more than 50 g/kg of polyols must state "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect" (R733 of 2012).
- Claims and labelling: R146 of 2010 governs labels and claims. Draft R3337 (April 2023) would add front of pack warnings, possibly for any product with non nutritive sweeteners. Whether it has been finalised is unverified; it is a real risk for a sweetener led range.
- Sugar free and no added sugar claim conditions, shelf life at entry and import permit rules were not verified.
- Trade: the SACU and Mozambique (SACUM) UK Economic Partnership Agreement, applied from 1 January 2021, carries over the SADC EU EPA tariff commitments for UK goods, with some sensitive products excluded. The duty on UK chocolate and sugar confectionery was not verified.
- Informal trade: spaza shops must now be registered with their municipality, so wholesalers may check registration before supplying (unverified).
