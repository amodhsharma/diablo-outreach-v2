## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: the four largest operators held 83.3% of supermarket revenue in 2021, with Woolworths (37.4%) and Coles (28.4%) ahead of Aldi (10.5%) and Metcash (7%). The big chains run their own national and regional distribution centres, so a central category buyer is the gatekeeper for most mainstream shelf space. Metcash is the largest grocery wholesaler and supplies the independent IGA, Supa IGA and IGA Express banners, which makes it the main route into independent supermarkets and part of the convenience channel. 7-Eleven is described as the leading convenience operator; petrol forecourt chains and travel retail buy centrally or through specialist convenience wholesalers (not verified in this run). Specialist channels such as sports nutrition, keto, diabetic and free from shops are fragmented and largely independent or online, so an importer or specialist distributor holding stock locally is the realistic first step before the major chains. Online grocery data was not gathered in this run (the search limit was reached).

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Woolworths and Coles together hold more than 65% of grocery, so mainstream listing depends on two central buyers; listing fees and promotional terms were not verified in this run.
- No added sugar claims: under FSANZ P1062 (Amendment 224, December 2023) a no added sugar claim is not allowed if the food contains an added sugar as defined (including honey, glucose syrup and maltodextrin) or exceeds 10 g total sugars per 100 g for solid food. A 48 month transition runs from 13 December 2023. Check each SKU, in particular any using maltodextrin.
- Sugar free claim conditions were not verified in this run.
- Polyols: the Food Standards Code requires an advisory statement on a laxative effect for foods with added polyols or polydextrose above set levels (unverified in this run: commonly cited as 10 g per 100 g). Maltitol based SKUs will need it on the label.
- Sweeteners: stevia and inulin based ranges are generally permitted, but each sweetener must be checked against the Code schedules by product type (unverified in this run).
- Import labelling: Australian labelling (nutrition information panel and country of origin labelling) is needed at entry; details were not verified in this run.
- Duty: the Australia UK FTA (signed December 2021; entry into force in May 2023 not verified here) removes tariffs on almost all UK goods, with confectionery and biscuits phased out within five years. This favours shipping from B Healthy Ltd (UK).
- Shelf life at entry, distributor margins and credit terms were not verified in this run.
