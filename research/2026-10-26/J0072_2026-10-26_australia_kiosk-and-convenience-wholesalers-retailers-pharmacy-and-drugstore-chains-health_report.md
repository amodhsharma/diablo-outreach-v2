## Market structure

Written without live searches: the run's shared web search budget was used up by the Channel helpers before this section was researched, so every point below is from general knowledge and is unverified for this run.

Grocery is highly concentrated. Woolworths and Coles hold the majority of supermarket sales, with Aldi a strong third and Metcash supplying the independent IGA and Foodland stores through its own warehouses. A listing with one of the two majors is the biggest single gatekeeper and is usually won through a local importer or broker. Convenience and petrol (7-Eleven, Ampol Foodary, EG and others) is served by route trade wholesalers and convenience distributors, with Metcash Campbells and specialist confectionery wholesalers important for independents. Pharmacy runs through banner groups such as Chemist Warehouse, Priceline and TerryWhite Chemmart, supplied by pharmacy wholesalers; their sugar free lolly bays are a real niche. Health food is fragmented: independent organic grocers and small chains buy from specialist health food distributors. Online, Amazon.com.au, eBay and specialist sweets and hamper sites matter, and the majors run large online grocery operations.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: two supermarket groups dominate, so a major listing usually needs a local importer, promotional funding and range review timing (unverified detail).
- Trade terms: listing fees, promotional contributions and long payment terms are common with the majors; exact norms not verified this run.
- Sweeteners: steviol glycosides and polyols are permitted under the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code, but by food type; check each product against Standard 1.3.1 and Schedule 15 (unverified for each SKU).
- Laxative warning: products with added polyols above set levels must carry an "excess consumption may have a laxative effect" style advisory statement (unverified wording).
- Claims: "sugar free" and "no added sugar" are nutrition content claims under Standard 1.2.7 with set conditions; health star rating is voluntary but expected by major retailers (unverified detail).
- Import labelling: English labels with an Australian importer name and address, country of origin labelling and an Australian style nutrition information panel; UK packs will usually need stickering or relabelling.
- Biosecurity: dairy containing products such as milk chocolate may need import permits or conditions from the Department of Agriculture (unverified for this range).
- Duty: the Australia UK Free Trade Agreement in force since 2023 removes most tariffs on UK goods; confirm rules of origin for B Healthy Ltd products (unverified this run).
