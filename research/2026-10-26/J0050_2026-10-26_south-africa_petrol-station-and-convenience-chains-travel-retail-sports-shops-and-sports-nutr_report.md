## Market structure

Packaged confectionery in South Africa reaches shelf mainly through the national supermarket groups (Shoprite and Checkers, Pick n Pay, Spar, Woolworths and the Massmart stores), which buy centrally into their own distribution centres and are the main gatekeepers for any new brand. A second, very large route runs through wholesale and cash and carry to spaza shops and independent grocers: one estimate puts the wholesale supply market at about R259 billion and the spaza sector at about R197 billion, with the big retailers now competing for that trade too. Forecourt shops are mostly run with supermarket partners under the fuel brands, so forecourt listings usually follow the partner retailer's buying. Pharmacies and drugstores (Clicks, Dis-Chem) and health and free from specialists carry better-for-you snacks, and e-commerce is growing from a small base. Local majors (Tiger Brands, Nestle, Mondelez through Cadbury) dominate mainstream chocolate and sweets. Channel share figures could not be verified in this run.

## Barriers to entry

- Buyer power: the Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry found the national chains' buyer power drives a rebate structure that discriminates against wholesalers and small retailers; a new supplier should expect rebate and promotion demands.
- Exclusive mall leases long kept specialist and challenger stores out of malls; the Inquiry asked for these to be phased out, which helps specialist health shops.
- Listing fees, distribution centre fees, payment terms and distributor margins: not verified in this run; confirm with any importer before pricing.
- Labelling: R146 (in force since 2012) is the law today; the draft R3337 regulations, not yet in force as of mid 2026, would add Chile style front of pack warnings for high sugar, sodium or saturated fat and a warning for products with non-nutritive sweeteners. Whether stevia counts as an "artificial sweetener" under the draft is unclear and should be checked.
- Sugar free and no added sugar claim rules, permitted polyols by product type and any laxative warning: not verified in this run; check R146 and the sweetener regulations before shipping.
- Trade: the SACUM-UK EPA carries over the SADC-EU EPA terms, so UK origin goods may get preferential duty; the exact rate for chocolate and sweets was not verified in this run.
- Shelf life at entry and import labelling specifics: not verified in this run.
