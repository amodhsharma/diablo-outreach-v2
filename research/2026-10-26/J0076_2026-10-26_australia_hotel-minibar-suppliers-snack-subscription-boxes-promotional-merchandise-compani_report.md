## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated and centrally distributed. Woolworths and Coles held about two thirds of grocery value in 2021, with ALDI third and Metcash (IGA banners) fourth; the four largest held about 83% of industry revenue. The major chains run their own national and regional distribution centres, so imported confectionery normally reaches them through a local importer or distributor that holds the listing, ranging and promotional funding. Metcash is the largest grocery wholesaler and the main route into independent grocers through its IGA network; it also signed a supply agreement with Ampol for petrol station convenience (terms not checked). Independent grocers and convenience stores pool buying through banner groups. The three channels in this job are niche: hotel minibar suppliers, snack subscription boxes and promotional merchandise companies buy small volumes, usually from local importers and wholesalers rather than directly from overseas brands, and many of the larger players are already on Diablo's exclusion list. Market share figures above are 2021 and may have moved since; ALDI is reported to be gaining.

## Closing analysis

- Sense2 first: it has the broadest promotional confectionery range in Australia, with biscuits, cookies and energy bar categories that match Diablo's catalogue, and dispatches nationwide from its own Sydney warehouse.
- Just In Case Direct second: a specialist hotel minibar wholesaler with over 250 lines of branded chocolate, confectionery and snacks, and a Healthy Products section that shows hotels already ask for better-for-you lines.
- Good Things Australia third: a dedicated branded confectionery category for large corporate clients plus in-house warehousing and pick-and-pack, which suits Diablo's chocolate, sweets and gift boxes.

## Barriers to entry

- Supermarket gatekeepers: Woolworths, Coles and ALDI decide ranging centrally; a local importer with a listing record is usually needed. Listing fees, promotional funding and margin norms were not verified in this run.
- Mandatory Food and Grocery Code of Conduct (in force 1 April 2025) covers retailers and wholesalers with over AUD 5 billion grocery revenue: written supply agreements, good faith dealing and no retaliation against suppliers.
- Sugar claims: "no added sugar" conditions were tightened by FSANZ Amendment 224 (13 Dec 2023): no added sugars (including honey, malt and concentrated fruit juice) and total sugars no more than 10 g/100 g for solids; a 48 month transition runs to late 2027. The exact "sugar free" threshold was not confirmed in this run (thought to be 0.5 g sugars per 100 g; unverified).
- Laxative advisory: Standard 1.2.3 requires a statement that excess consumption may have a laxative effect when lactitol, maltitol, mannitol or xylitol reach 10 g/100 g, or polydextrose, erythritol, isomalt or sorbitol reach 25 g/100 g. Relevant for any Diablo lines using polyols.
- Sweeteners: stevia (steviol glycosides) and inulin are permitted in Australia; product by product permissions should be checked against the Food Standards Code before export (not verified per product here).
- Labelling: Australian importer name and address, country of origin labelling and Australian nutrition information panel format are required; UK labels will normally need a sticker or relabel (details not verified in this run).
- Duty: the Australia UK Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023. Tariffs on confectionery and biscuits were to be phased out over five years rather than removed at once, so some residual duty may apply until 2028 (current rate not verified). UK rules of origin must be met.
- Shelf life at entry: no fixed legal minimum found; importers and retailers typically ask for long remaining life (unverified).
