## Market structure

Formal grocery in South Africa is concentrated in a few groups. Shoprite (Shoprite, Checkers, Usave) is the clear leader and keeps gaining share, while Pick n Pay, Spar and Woolworths Food follow. Spar runs as a wholesaler supplying independently owned stores, so its distribution centres and the store owners are both gatekeepers. Woolworths sells mostly its own label, which limits room for imported brands. Health and wellness ranges also sell through pharmacy and health chains such as Clicks and Dis-Chem (not verified in this run). Quick commerce matters in cities: Checkers Sixty60 is the largest app, with Woolworths Dash and Pick n Pay asap! behind it. Online, Takealot is the main general marketplace (not verified in this run). Imported confectionery usually enters through a South African importer and distributor who holds the stock, handles labelling compliance and sells into chain head offices, wholesalers and independents. For the channels in this job (hotel minibar suppliers, snack subscription boxes and promotional merchandise companies), the helpers found the channels thin: few companies stock third party branded snacks and many make their own or custom products, so an importer distributor is likely the real gatekeeper even for these routes.

## Barriers to entry

- Dominant players: Shoprite and Pick n Pay control a large share of formal grocery and Woolworths favours own label; listing fees, rebates and margin norms for chains were not verified in this run.
- Distributor terms: margins, credit terms and exclusivity norms for South African importers were not verified; expect the importer to ask for national exclusivity.
- Sweeteners: permitted sweeteners and their uses are set by the Regulations relating to the Use of Sweeteners in Foodstuffs (R733 of 2012); check that stevia (steviol glycosides) and each polyol is permitted for each product type before export.
- Claims: labelling and nutrition claims fall under the Foodstuffs, Cosmetics and Disinfectants Act and R146 (2010); new draft regulations (R3337, 2023) would tighten claims and add front of pack warnings. The exact conditions for "sugar free" and "no added sugar" were not verified in this run.
- Laxative warning: a warning for polyol content is expected under the labelling rules, but the exact wording and threshold were not verified.
- Import labelling: labels must be in English and meet R146 (importer details, date marking, ingredients and allergens); stickering by the importer is common but unverified as permitted for every item.
- Sugar tax: the Health Promotion Levy applies to sugary beverages only, not confectionery, and sugar-free products are favoured by the wider policy.
- Duty: the SACUM-UK Economic Partnership Agreement, in force since 2021, gives preferential access for UK goods; the rate for chocolate and sugar confectionery under it was not verified. EU goods use the SADC-EU EPA.
- Shelf life at entry: no fixed national minimum was found; importers and chains set their own.
