## Market structure

South African grocery retail is concentrated in a few national chains (Shoprite Checkers, Pick n Pay, Spar, Woolworths, Massmart and Clicks were long described as the "Big Six"), and these chains buy largely through central listing and distribution, so a national buyer is the real gatekeeper for mainstream shelf space. Independent wholesalers and cash and carry outlets supply most of the informal trade (spaza shops), which Trade Intelligence sizes at about R456 billion combined with wholesale supply to informal traders. For seasonal and gift shops in Cape Town the route is different and much shorter: hamper makers, gift boutiques, tourist and waterfront shops and Christmas or Easter pop ups usually buy direct from brand owners or from local importers and specialist distributors, and often in small quantities ahead of peak seasons. Online, Takealot and health stores such as Faithful to Nature list imported sweets and chocolate and can act as a showcase. Unverified: the share of imported sugar free confectionery sold through gift and seasonal outlets, which no source we found measures. A local importer holding stock in the Western Cape is likely to be needed to serve these small, seasonal buyers.

## Barriers to entry

- Retailer terms: the Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry examined rebates and other supplier charges by the large chains and long exclusive lease clauses in shopping centres; exact listing fee levels were not verified.
- Seasonality: gift and seasonal shops buy in short windows (Christmas, Easter, Valentine's), so stock must land well before peak; shelf life at entry needs to cover the season.
- Sweeteners: only sweeteners in the Codex GSFA list are permitted (R733 of 2012); steviol glycosides must be labelled "Steviol Glycosides" or "Steviol Extract", and non nutritive sweeteners named with "(non nutritive sweetener)".
- Laxative warning: foods with more than 50 g/kg of polyols must state "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect" (250 g/kg for sugar free chewing gum).
- Claims and labelling: R146 of 2010 still governs labelling and claims; the draft R3337 would add front of pack warnings for high sugar and for products with artificial sweeteners, but was not in force at the time of checking. Exact rules for "no added sugar" claims under R146 were not verified.
- Import labelling: imported food must comply with South African labelling law (often meaning a local sticker or relabel) and non compliant consignments can be held by Port Health.
- Duty: the SACU and Mozambique UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives UK goods some reduced duties into SACU; the exact rate for chocolate and confectionery from the UK was not verified.
