## Market structure

Johannesburg and the wider Gauteng province are South Africa's largest consumer market, and product reaches shelf mainly through the national grocery groups. Shoprite (Shoprite, Checkers, Usave) leads, followed by Pick n Pay, Spar and Woolworths Food. Spar is a wholesaler supplying independently owned stores, so both its distribution centres and the store owners act as gatekeepers. Woolworths leans on own label. Health and wellness ranges also sell through the pharmacy chains Dis-Chem and Clicks, which are strong in Gauteng. Quick commerce is well developed in Johannesburg: Checkers Sixty60 is the largest app, with Pick n Pay asap!, Woolworths Dash and Spar2U competing. Away from the chains, office pantry suppliers, vending operators, sports nutrition shops and corporate hamper companies buy from local importers and wholesalers rather than direct from foreign brands (inferred from the helpers' findings, not verified as a rule). An importer distributor holding stock in Gauteng, compliant with local labelling, is therefore the practical gatekeeper for a new imported brand. The helpers found these niche channels thin in Johannesburg, partly because many candidate websites would not open and the search budget ran out.

## Barriers to entry

- Retailer terms: the Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry examined rebates and other supplier charges that disadvantage small suppliers; current listing fee levels were not verified.
- Dominant players: the large chains control most formal grocery and Woolworths favours own label, so national listings are hard for a new brand.
- Distributor terms: importer margins, credit terms and exclusivity norms were not verified in this run; expect requests for national exclusivity.
- Sweeteners: the Regulations relating to the Use of Sweeteners in Foodstuffs (R733 of 2012) set permitted sweeteners by product type; check stevia and each polyol before export (not verified per product).
- Claims and warnings: labelling, nutrition claims and any polyol laxative warning fall under the Foodstuffs, Cosmetics and Disinfectants Act and R146; draft regulations would tighten claims. Exact sugar-free and no-added-sugar conditions were not verified.
- Import labelling: English labels meeting R146 are required; whether importer stickering is accepted for every item was not verified.
- Sugar tax: the Health Promotion Levy covers sugary beverages only, not confectionery.
- Duty: the SACUM-UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives UK goods preferential access; the rate for chocolate and sugar confectionery was not verified.
- Shelf life at entry: no national minimum was found; importers and chains set their own.
