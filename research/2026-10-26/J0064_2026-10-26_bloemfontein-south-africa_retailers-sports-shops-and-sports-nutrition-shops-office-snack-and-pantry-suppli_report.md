## Market structure

Packaged confectionery in South Africa reaches shoppers through two routes. The formal route runs through the national grocery chains (Shoprite and Checkers, Pick n Pay, SPAR, Woolworths and Massmart), which buy centrally or through their own distribution centres, and through the Clicks and Dis-Chem health and pharmacy chains. The informal route runs through independent wholesalers and cash and carries that supply about 150,000 spaza shops, a market Trade Intelligence put at about R197 billion at retail and R259 billion at wholesale in 2024; Shoprite's Usave and Cash & Carry formats compete hard here, and large suppliers such as Tiger Brands now go direct to informal traders. Bloemfontein, the Free State's main city, is served mainly by the national chains and by regional wholesalers and cash and carries; we found no figures specific to the city (unverified, as this run's search budget ran out before city-level research). The real gatekeepers for an imported sugar-free range are the national chain buyers, the health and pharmacy chains and the importers and distributors that supply independent shops.

## Barriers to entry

- Chain listings: national chains buy centrally and typically expect listing support, promotional spend and rebates; current fee levels were not verified.
- Dominant players: Shoprite, Pick n Pay, SPAR and Woolworths dominate formal grocery; local brand owners such as Tiger Brands and AVI hold strong biscuit and confectionery shelf space.
- Distributor terms: margins and credit terms for this category were not verified.
- Sweeteners: Regulation R.733 of 2012 allows only sweeteners listed in the Codex GSFA; steviol glycosides must be labelled "Steviol Glycosides" or "Steviol Extract" and non-nutritive sweeteners named with "non-nutritive sweetener".
- Laxative warning: foods with more than 50 g/kg of polyols must state "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect".
- Labelling and claims: R.146 is the labelling regulation in force, including its claims section; the draft R.3337 (April 2023) would add front-of-pack warnings for added sugar and some sweeteners and limit claims on warned products, but was still not final in 2026. Exact sugar-free and no-added-sugar claim conditions under R.146 were not verified here.
- Import duty: the SACUM-UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives UK goods gradual tariff liberalisation with EUR1 proof of origin and cumulation with EU materials, but sensitive agricultural lines can face quotas or safeguards; confirm the duty for each HS code.
- Shelf life at entry: no specific rule was verified.
