## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in South Africa reach shoppers through three routes. The formal grocery chains (Shoprite Checkers, Pick n Pay, SPAR, Woolworths and Boxer) buy centrally or through regional distribution centres and are the main gatekeepers for national listings. The formal independent and wholesale trade (cash and carry wholesalers, buying groups and unlisted retailers) is put at about R259 billion, roughly a third of the FMCG market, and it supplies the informal trade. Spaza shops and other informal outlets number about 150,000 and were estimated at R197 billion in 2023; they are supplied mainly by independent wholesalers, with chains such as Shoprite (Usave) and brand owners such as Tiger Brands now pushing into the channel. Pharmacy and health retail (Clicks, Dis-Chem and independent health shops) is the natural home for a sugar free range. Online, Takealot and the retailers' own apps (Checkers Sixty60, Pick n Pay asap) lead; seller access through them was not verified in this run. For a new importer the practical first gatekeeper is a national importer and distributor with chain listings or a pharmacy and health shop buyer.

## Barriers to entry

- Sweeteners: R733 of 2012 permits only sweeteners referred to in the Codex GSFA, at GSFA maximum levels and food categories. Steviol glycosides must be labelled "Steviol Glycosides" or "Steviol Extract"; the permitted use per product type was not confirmed in this run.
- Polyol warning: foods with polyols above 50 g/kg must state "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect" (chewing gum above 250 g/kg). This will apply to most of the chocolate and sweets range.
- Labelling: R146 of 2010 is still the law; sugar free and other nutrient content claims must meet compositional criteria and match the nutrition table, backed by laboratory analysis. The exact sugar free and no added sugar thresholds were not verified here. A local sticker or relabel is usually needed for UK packs (unverified detail).
- Draft R3337: proposed front of pack warnings for high sugar, sodium or saturated fat and limits on claims for products carrying them. Not yet in force; monitor, as it could favour a sugar free range.
- Trade terms: listing fees, rebates and long payment terms at the big chains are reported in the trade but no figures were verified in this run. Distributor margin and credit norms were not verified.
- Duty: the SACU and Mozambique to UK Economic Partnership Agreement (in force 1 January 2021) phases down duties on UK goods entering SACU; the rate for confectionery under it was not confirmed, so check the SARS tariff book for the exact HS codes.
- Dominant players: Mondelez (Cadbury), Nestle, Tiger Brands and Beacon (Mondelez) hold the confectionery shelf and invest heavily in informal trade support. Shelf life and import permit rules at entry were not verified.
