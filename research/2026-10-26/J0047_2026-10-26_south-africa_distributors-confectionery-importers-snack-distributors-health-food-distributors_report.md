## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in South Africa reach shoppers mainly through a few national grocery chains (Shoprite and Checkers, Pick n Pay, Spar and Woolworths), which buy centrally or through their own distribution centres and decide which brands get shelf space. Spar works through independent retailers supplied from regional distribution centres, so its buyers and its larger store owners are both gatekeepers. Beside the chains, cash and carry wholesalers and buying groups supply independent grocers, forecourt shops and the large informal (spaza) trade, which is a separate route reached through wholesalers and van sales rather than central listings. Health and free from products also sell through pharmacy and health chains and online health shops, which often buy from specialist health food distributors. For an imported niche brand like Diablo, the realistic gatekeeper is an importer or distributor that already holds chain listings and handles registration, labelling and logistics. Shares of each route were not verified in this run; treat this as a structural outline.

## Barriers to entry

- Chain power: the Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry looked at rebates, fees and supplier terms charged by the large chains and found these can shut out small suppliers. Exact listing fee and rebate levels were not verified in this run.
- Distributor margin, credit and exclusivity norms were not verified in this run; expect importers to ask for national exclusivity and marketing support (unverified).
- Sweeteners: only sweeteners listed in the Codex GSFA may be used, at GSFA maximum levels (R733 of 2012). Whether each Diablo polyol is allowed in each food category should be checked against the GSFA category entries.
- Sweetener labelling: non nutritive sweeteners must be named in the ingredients followed by "(non-nutritive sweetener)"; stevia must be labelled "Steviol Glycosides" or "Steviol Extract".
- Laxative warning: products with more than 50 g/kg of polyols must state "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect" (R733 of 2012).
- Claims and labelling: R146 of 2010 governs labels and claims today. Draft R3337 (published April 2023) would add black and white front of pack warnings, possibly for any product with non nutritive sweeteners, and limit cartoon characters on pack. Whether R3337 has since been finalised is unverified; it is a real risk for a sweetener led range.
- Sugar free and no added sugar claim conditions under R146 were not verified in this run.
- Trade: the SACU and Mozambique (SACUM) UK Economic Partnership Agreement, applied from 1 January 2021, carries over the SADC EU EPA tariff commitments for UK goods, with some sensitive products excluded. The duty rate on chocolate and sugar confectionery from the UK was not verified.
- Shelf life at entry and import permit rules were not verified in this run.
