## Market structure

Packaged confectionery in South Africa reaches shelf mostly through five grocery groups: Shoprite (Checkers), Pick n Pay, Spar, Massmart (Makro) and Woolworths, which together are reported to hold about 80% of formal retail sales, with Shoprite pulling ahead in 2026. These chains buy centrally (largely from Gauteng and Cape Town head offices), so their category buyers are the real gatekeepers, and an importer usually reaches them through a local importer or distributor that holds stock and services stores. In Gqeberha that means most chain listings are decided outside the city; local buyers matter mainly for independent retailers, Spar stores (which are independently owned), sports and cycle shops, office pantry suppliers and hamper companies, which tend to buy from national importers and Eastern Cape wholesalers rather than direct from overseas. Traditional general dealers and spaza shops still matter outside the big cities and are served through wholesalers and buying groups. This run's web search budget ran out early, so Gqeberha specific facts (mall hubs, the size of the local wholesale base) are unverified.

## Barriers to entry

- Retailer fees: the Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry found national chains demand rebates from suppliers to fund display, new stores, shelf access and category management. Exact listing fee levels are unverified.
- Dominant players: the five big groups and long term exclusive mall leases (which the Commission asked malls to phase out) favour chains over independent shops, which get smaller rebates through wholesalers and buying groups.
- Distributor margin and credit norms: not verified in this run.
- Labelling today: R146 of 2010 remains the law. Draft R3337 (comments closed 2023) is still not in force as of mid 2026.
- Draft R3337 risk: it would require a front of pack warning on any product containing artificial (non nutritive) sweeteners and on products above 10 g sugar per 100 g, covering about a quarter of the front of pack, plus limits on marketing to children. Whether stevia is caught by the final rule is unverified.
- Sweeteners and polyols by product type, sugar free and no added sugar claim conditions and any polyol laxative warning: not verified in this run; check with a South African regulatory consultant before shipping.
- Import labelling: labels must meet R146, so UK or EU packs are likely to need a local sticker or relabel. Shelf life at entry: not verified.
- Import duty: the SACU and Mozambique to UK Economic Partnership Agreement has applied since 1 January 2021, so UK origin goods may get preferential tariffs; the confectionery rate and rules of origin are unverified.
