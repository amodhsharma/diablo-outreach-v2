## Market structure

Note: the shared web search budget for this run was used up by the Channel helpers, so this section reuses the sourced findings from today's earlier Australia jobs (J0071 and J0073) and adds no new searches.

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: the four largest operators held 83.3% of supermarket revenue in 2021, with Woolworths (37.4%) and Coles (28.4%) ahead of Aldi (10.5%) and Metcash (7%). The big chains buy centrally through their own distribution centres, so a central category buyer is the gatekeeper for mainstream shelf space. Metcash supplies the independent IGA banners and is the main route into independent supermarkets. The channels in this job sit outside that core: vegan shops, kosher shops (concentrated in Melbourne and Sydney) and halal grocers are small, largely independent or online, and usually buy from specialist importers and wholesalers rather than the majors (unverified in this run). Corporate gifting and hamper companies buy direct from brands or wholesalers and peak before Christmas (unverified). A local importer or specialist distributor holding stock is the realistic first step.

## Barriers to entry

- **Dominant players:** Coles and Woolworths together control about two thirds of supermarket sales; Metcash controls most independent wholesale.
- **Food and Grocery Code:** mandatory since 1 April 2025 for grocery retailers and wholesalers with revenue over A$5bn (Aldi, Coles, Metcash, Woolworths), requiring written supply agreements and good faith dealing. Listing fee levels are unverified.
- **Exclusivity, margin and credit:** importers commonly expect national exclusivity; typical margins and payment terms are unverified.
- **Certification:** kosher and halal channels will expect recognised certification on each SKU (unverified which certifiers are accepted in Australia).
- **Sweeteners:** FSANZ has approved steviol glycosides since 2008; maximum levels by product type sit in Schedule 15 of the Food Standards Code (not verified per SKU).
- **Laxative warning:** Standard 1.2.3 requires an advisory at 10 g/100 g or more of lactitol, maltitol, maltitol syrup, mannitol or xylitol, or 25 g/100 g or more of erythritol, isomalt, polydextrose or sorbitol.
- **No added sugar:** Amendment 224 (December 2023) bans the claim where a food contains any added sugar ingredient or exceeds 10 g sugars/100 g (solids); 48 month transition from 13 December 2023.
- **Sugar free:** no Code threshold; the ACCC expects sugar to be absent (secondary source, confirm).
- **Import labelling:** country of origin labelling applies; UK packs will likely need stickering or relabelling (details unverified).
- **Shelf life at entry:** no published rule found; set commercially (unverified).
- **Duty:** the Australia UK FTA entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed tariffs on most UK goods, so B Healthy Ltd (UK) shipments should enter duty free if they meet UK rules of origin. The EU Australia FTA is not yet in force.
