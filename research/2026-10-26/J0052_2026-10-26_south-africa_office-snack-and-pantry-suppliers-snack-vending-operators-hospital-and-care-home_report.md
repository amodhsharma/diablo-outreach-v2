## Market structure

Formal grocery in South Africa is concentrated in a few listed groups: Shoprite (Checkers, Shoprite, Usave), Pick n Pay (with Boxer), SPAR (a wholesaler supplying independent stores) and Woolworths Food, with Clicks and Dis-Chem adding a health aisle. Their central buying offices are the main gatekeepers. The informal route is large: Trade Intelligence puts wholesale supply to informal traders at about R259 billion and the spaza sector at about R197 billion, served mostly by independent wholesalers and Shoprite's cash and carry stores.
The channels in this job sit outside that core. Office pantry suppliers, vending operators, hospital shops, gift shops and venue caterers usually buy from local wholesalers or snack distributors rather than importing themselves, so for Diablo they are second tier customers reached through an importer (a judgement, not a sourced fact). Hospital shops are largely pharmacy front shops run by the pharmacy chains, and vending is a fragmented field of regional operators (both from this run's channel research, unverified at market level).

## Barriers to entry

- Retailer power: the Competition Commission's grocery inquiry found national chains demand supplier rebates for merchandising, shelf space and store openings. Listing fee levels are not public (unverified).
- Dominant players: Shoprite leads supply to informal trade; independents and buying groups get weaker terms than chains.
- Distributor margin and credit norms: not verified for this range; expect importers to ask for exclusivity and marketing support (unverified).
- Sweeteners: only sweeteners in the Codex GSFA are permitted (R733 of 2012). Steviol glycosides must be labelled as "Steviol Glycosides" or "Steviol Extract", with "non-nutritive sweetener" in the ingredient list.
- Laxative warning: foods with more than 50 g/kg of polyols must state "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect".
- Claims: R146 of 2010 is still the law and allows only prescribed claim wording; "no added sugar" is barred on any food with added sugars. Draft R3337 (comment period April to July 2023) is still not in force.
- Draft R3337 risk: it would require a front of pack warning on products containing artificial sweeteners, covering about 25% of the front, and products in vending machines would have to show it clearly. Whether stevia counts as "artificial" under the final text is unverified.
- Import labelling: English, a South African importer named on pack, local allergen list and metric quantity; Port Health can detain non-compliant stock, so overseas packs usually need over-labels.
- Shelf life at entry: no specific rule found (unverified).
- Duty: the SACUM-UK EPA (concluded 2019) rolls over the SADC-EU EPA terms, including tariffs, quotas and rules of origin. Rates for chocolate and confectionery headings were not verified.
- Sugar tax: the Health Promotion Levy applies only to beverages; no extension to confectionery was found.
