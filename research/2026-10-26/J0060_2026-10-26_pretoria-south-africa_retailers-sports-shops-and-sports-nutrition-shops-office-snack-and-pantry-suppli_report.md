## Market structure

Packaged confectionery in South Africa reaches shelf through two routes. The formal route runs through a few national grocery groups (Shoprite and Checkers, Pick n Pay and Boxer, SPAR with its independent owner-run stores, Woolworths and Clicks and Dis-Chem on the health shelf), whose central buyers are the real gatekeepers and who usually buy through a local importer or distributor rather than straight from a foreign brand. Shoprite is the clear leader: one estimate puts it at about 26% of formal grocery against about 16% for Pick n Pay (2021 figures, not current). The second route is wholesale and cash and carry (Makro, Jumbo and independent cash and carries in Gauteng) feeding spaza shops, independent grocers and sweet shops. Pretoria sits inside Gauteng, the country's main distribution hub, so most national distributors, vending operators and office pantry suppliers serve it from Johannesburg area depots (not verified per company in this run). Corporate gifting, office snacks and vending are fragmented and mostly run by small Gauteng firms. Online grocery is growing, led by the chains' own apps (share unverified).

## Barriers to entry

- Supplier rebates: the Competition Commission's grocery retail market inquiry (2019) found that national chains demand rebates for merchandising, store openings, shelf space and category management, which entrench the big chains. Exact listing fee levels were not verified.
- Chain listing normally needs a local importer or distributor that carries stock, handles invoicing and credit terms and merchandises in store; distributor margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Sweeteners: regulation R.733 (2012) permits only sweeteners listed in the Codex GSFA; steviol glycosides must be labelled "Steviol Glycosides" or "Steviol Extract" and non-nutritive sweeteners flagged in the ingredient list. Whether each polyol is permitted for each product type was not confirmed here.
- Laxative warning: foods with more than 50 g/kg of polyols must state "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect" (250 g/kg for sugar-free chewing gum).
- Claims and labelling: the current rules are R.146 (2010). Draft R.3337 (April 2023), with front-of-pack warnings for added sugar and some sweeteners and limits on claims, was still a draft in July 2026 with no timeline. Sugar-free and no-added-sugar claim conditions under R.146 were not checked in detail.
- Health Promotion Levy: South Africa's sugar tax applies to sugary drinks, not confectionery, so it does not hit Diablo's range today.
- Trade: the UK-SACUM EPA (agreed 2019) carries over the SADC-EU EPA terms for UK and South Africa trade. The actual duty on UK sugar confectionery and chocolate entering South Africa was not verified.
- Shelf life at entry: chains usually want a good share of shelf life left on arrival; the exact requirement was not verified.
