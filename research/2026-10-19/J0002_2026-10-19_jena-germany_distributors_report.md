## Market structure

German grocery is highly concentrated. Edeka, Schwarz Group (Lidl and Kaufland), Rewe and Aldi hold about 85% of food retail, with 2024 revenues of roughly 61, 55, 40 and 36 billion euros. Discounters are the largest confectionery channel, with about 44.6% of confectionery sales in NielsenIQ's 2023 monitor. Jena sits in the area of EDEKA Nordbayern-Sachsen-Thüringen (Rottendorf), the Edeka regional company for Thuringia. Local store listings show Rewe (about seven stores), Edeka and E center, Kaufland, Globus, tegut, nah und gut and SPAR Express, plus the discounters. Tegut is being split between Edeka and Rewe, pending cartel office approval (April 2026). Drugstores dm and Rossmann are a growing gate for health-led food: food is about 17% of their revenue and grew 13.8%, with suppliers reporting strong demand for health-oriented products. Online is small (about 4.9% of FMCG sales in 2025); quick commerce is concentrated in large cities and no service in Jena was confirmed. The real gatekeepers are the central and regional buyers of the big four and the drugstore chains, so an importer that already holds listings with them is the practical route in.

## Closing analysis

- **Crevel Europe GmbH (creveleurope.com):** its whole model is importing foreign branded sweets and snacks (Tajin, Herr's, Van Holten's) and building them with customers such as EDEKA, REWE, METRO and TEDi, so it can take Diablo into the chains that serve Jena.
- **tabacon TABAKWAREN GmbH & Co. KG (tabacon.de):** a family owned Thuringian wholesaler near Gera supplying over 2,700 shops, kiosks and petrol stations, already adding sweets to a mostly tobacco range, so sugar free lines are a clear add-on and a decision maker is reachable.
- **Lekkerland Deutschland (lekkerland.de):** the leading supplier of sweets and snacks to petrol stations and kiosks in Central Germany, including around Jena, with a newcomer products section; listing is central and slower, so run it in parallel with the two above.

## Barriers to entry

- **Buyer power:** four groups control about 85% of food retail; the cartel office reviews deals for regional effects, but suppliers carry most of the pressure.
- **Listing fees and promotion funds:** common in German retail but no verified figures were found in this run; treat any quoted fee as something to confirm with the importer.
- **Trading terms law:** Germany's unfair trading practices law (AgrarOLkG) caps buyer payment terms at 60 days for non-perishable food and restricts pushing the cost of returning unsold goods onto the supplier; it protects suppliers up to 350 million euros group turnover.
- **Distributor margin, credit and exclusivity norms:** not verified in this run.
- **Market headwind:** in 2025 the average chocolate pack cost about 14% more and packs sold fell 5.8%; growth came from sports bars, waffles and fruit gums (NIQ, BDSI).
- **Quick commerce and online:** Flink, Rewe via Lieferando (40+ cities) and Wolt serve mainly large cities; online is about 4.9% of FMCG, so it is a secondary route for Jena.
- **Claims:** "sugars-free" means no more than 0.5 g sugars per 100 g; "no added sugars" means no added mono or disaccharides or sweetening foods, plus "contains naturally occurring sugars" where relevant (Regulation 1924/2006).
- **Sweeteners and polyols:** polyols are permitted mainly in energy-reduced or no-added-sugar foods (Regulation 1333/2008 Annex II); packs need "with sweetener(s)" and, above 10% added polyols, the laxative warning ("kann bei übermäßigem Verzehr abführend wirken").
- **Sweetener review:** EFSA kept the laxative warning for erythritol (ADI 0.5 g per kg body weight per day) and is re-evaluating maltitol, sorbitol, isomalt, lactitol and xylitol in 2026; steviol glycoside status was not covered.
- **Diabetic positioning:** Germany removed the dietary food rules for diabetic products (production allowed until 2012), so avoid "für Diabetiker" claims; current wording limits were not confirmed.
- **Import labelling:** the importer must register with the local food authority and carries full legal responsibility; labels must be in German and name the EU importer when the maker is outside the EU.
- **Shelf life at entry:** not verified in this run.
- **Duty:** UK-origin goods meeting the Trade and Cooperation Agreement rules of origin enter at zero tariff with a statement on origin; non-originating chocolate (heading 1806) can face up to 8.3% plus a possible sugar and milk fat component (check TARIC). Stock already in free circulation in the EU moves to Germany duty free.
