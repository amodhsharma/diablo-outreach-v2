## Market structure

German grocery is one of the most concentrated in Europe. The Bundeskartellamt puts the four leading groups (Edeka, Rewe, Aldi and the Schwarz Group with Lidl and Kaufland) at more than 85% of food retail revenue, and EHI data cited by FoodNavigator gives Aldi and Lidl alone over 45%. Cologne is home to the Rewe Group, which passed 100 billion euros turnover in 2025 and runs Rewe and Penny; its 1,620 independent Rewe merchants hold some local listing freedom. Drugstores are a second gatekeeper for better-for-you sweets: dm leads and Rossmann had 2,342 German stores and an 18.8% share of drugstore goods sold through grocery in 2025. Central buying desks are therefore the real gatekeepers; a foreign brand typically enters through an importer or specialist wholesaler that already holds listings with these chains, health food stores, pharmacies, kiosks and petrol station shops. Convenience, kiosk and forecourt supply runs largely through Lekkerland and regional Süßwaren wholesalers. Cologne also hosts ISM (31 January to 3 February 2027), the leading sweets and snacks trade fair and the natural place to meet German buyers and importers. Online (Amazon.de and drugstore webshops) matters but its confectionery share is unverified.

## Barriers to entry

- Retailer power: with four groups above 85% of revenue, the chains dictate terms. Listing fees and promotional contributions are common practice but no published figures were found (unverified).
- Supplier terms: the BGH (2018, KVR 3/17) found Edeka's retroactive best-price and "partnership fee" demands on suppliers partly abusive, showing how hard German retailers press suppliers on conditions.
- Distributor margins, credit terms and exclusivity norms: no reliable published figures found; treat any rule of thumb as unverified and confirm in negotiation.
- Polyols: foods with more than 10% added polyols must carry "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects"; any food with sweeteners must state "with sweetener(s)" (in German on pack).
- Permitted use: under Regulation 1333/2008 polyols and intense sweeteners such as steviol glycosides are allowed in confectionery mainly when it is energy-reduced or has no added sugars; check each SKU against Annex II.
- Claims: under Regulation 1924/2006 "sugar-free" means no more than 0.5 g sugars per 100 g; "no added sugars" means no added mono- or disaccharides or sweetening foods, and must add "contains naturally occurring sugars" where these are present.
- Import labelling: full German language labelling under Regulation 1169/2011 with an EU or EU-based food business operator named; UK-only packs will need relabelling or new artwork.
- Border controls: shelf-stable composite products (chocolate, biscuits, sweets) from GB are exempt from border control post checks if conditions are met, but the EU importer must complete a private attestation (form 8352).
- Duty: UK-originating goods enter tariff-free under the UK EU Trade and Cooperation Agreement, but only if rules of origin are met; non-originating confectionery faces the EU third-country tariff (chocolate lines quoted at up to about 8.3% plus a possible agricultural component, unverified, check TARIC).
- Shelf life at entry: German importers and chains usually expect a minimum remaining shelf life on delivery, but no standard figure was verified.
