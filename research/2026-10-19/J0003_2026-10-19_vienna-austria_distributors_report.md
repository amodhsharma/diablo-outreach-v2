## Market structure

Austrian grocery is one of the most concentrated in Europe. NielsenIQ figures for 2025 put SPAR at 36.3%, REWE Group (Billa, Billa Plus, Penny, ADEG) at 32.4% and the discounters Hofer and Lidl at 25.7% combined, leaving Markant (Unimarkt, Nah&Frisch), MPreis and independents with under 6%. The competition authority (BWB) put the big four at about 91% in 2023. The real gatekeepers are therefore the central buying desks of SPAR and REWE, plus the drugstore chains dm and BIPA, which are an important outlet for better-for-you snacks (their exact snack share is unverified). For a foreign brand, the usual route is an Austrian importer or distributor that already holds listings and field sales coverage with these buyers. Foodservice and independents are served through cash and carry and wholesale (Metro, which has taken over REWE's AGM, and family wholesalers such as Kastner). Online grocery is small and shrinking: Interspar, MPreis and Unimarkt left home delivery in 2024 and 2025 and Billa ended its own delivery in January 2026, leaving Gurkerl and Alfies in the Vienna area plus Foodora quick commerce.
Sources: [ESM Magazine: top 5 supermarket chains in Austria](https://www.esmmagazine.com/retail/top-5-supermarket-retail-chains-in-austria-235638), [vienna.at: BWB report on food prices](https://www.vienna.at/bericht-der-bundeswettbewerbsbehorde-zu-lebensmittelpreisen/8386885), [vienna.at: Billa leaves online delivery](https://www.vienna.at/nach-mpreis-und-interspar-auch-billa-zieht-sich-aus-dem-onlinegeschaeft-zurueck/9957077), [Der Standard: wholesale concentration](https://www.derstandard.at/story/2000135324012/groll-ueber-riesen-im-lebensmittelgrosshandel)

## Barriers to entry

- Channel lock up: SPAR, REWE and the discounters control over 90% of grocery, so one buying decision at SPAR or REWE decides national reach; strong private labels compete on shelf.
- Listing and promotion fees: under the Austrian UTP law (FWBG), listing fees, promotion cost sharing and unpaid returns are allowed only if clearly agreed in advance; payments unrelated to sales are banned. Actual fee levels are not public and are unverified.
- Payment terms: the FWBG caps payment at 60 days for non-perishable food (30 days for perishables); distributor margin and credit norms for Austria were not verified.
- Online and quick commerce: small after Billa, Interspar and MPreis withdrew; Gurkerl, Alfies and Foodora remain in Vienna. Their supplier fees were not verified.
- Sweeteners and polyols: under Regulation (EC) 1333/2008 polyols are mostly permitted only in energy-reduced or no-added-sugar foods; steviol glycosides (E960) are permitted by food category, which should be checked product by product.
- Labelling: products with sweeteners must say "with sweetener(s)"; more than 10% added polyols requires "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects" (Regulation (EU) 1169/2011). Mandatory information must be in German for Austria.
- Claims: "no added sugars" requires no added mono or disaccharides or other ingredients used for sweetness; "sugar free" requires no more than 0.5 g sugars per 100 g (Regulation (EC) 1924/2006).
- Import duty: UK goods enter tariff free under the EU UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement only if they meet rules of origin; otherwise full EU duty applies. Customs declarations and an EU based food business operator on the label are needed. Shelf life expectations at entry were not verified.
