## Market structure

German grocery is highly concentrated: Edeka, Rewe, Aldi and the Schwarz Group (Lidl and Kaufland) together hold about 90% of the market, which leaves few independent buyers. Discounters are gaining, reaching about 47% of grocery store visits in September 2025, while Edeka and Rewe have stagnated. For an imported brand the real gatekeepers are therefore the central and regional buying offices of these four groups. Edeka and Rewe also run independent merchant stores that can list some lines locally, but we could not verify how much listing power those Berlin stores hold. Drugstores (dm, Rossmann, Müller) are a second gatekeeper: they are growing their food ranges, sweets are a strong impulse category for them and they lean towards organic, vegan and better-for-you lines, which suits a sugar-free range. In Berlin, quick commerce is a real channel: Flink, based in Berlin, runs local delivery hubs and confirmed EBITDA profitability in March 2026. Importers and wholesalers are the practical way in for a UK brand, as they handle customs, German labelling and listing talks with these chains. Health food stores, kiosks (Spätis) and international food shops are reachable through smaller wholesalers; their size is unverified.

## Closing analysis

- Genuport Trade GmbH first: a family-owned specialist importer of foreign confectionery and snack brands into German retail, already carrying British brands and fitness lines with no direct sugar-free competitor.
- Uplegger Food Company GmbH second: a long-standing importer that has built British brands such as Cathedral City and Gu in German retail, though its chilled focus and whether it buys and holds stock need checking.
- Terra Naturkost Handels KG third: a Berlin-based organic and natural food wholesaler confirmed by two sources, giving direct reach into Berlin's health food stores where a stevia-sweetened range fits; Lekkerland follows for the Späti and kiosk channel but its buying is central within the REWE Group.

## Barriers to entry

- Channel lock-up: four retail groups hold about 90% of grocery, so a listing with one central buyer decides most volume.
- Listing costs: suppliers pay listing fees (Listungsgebühren), shelf rent and contributions to advertising, promotions and store refits; exact amounts are not public and unverified.
- Exclusivity: retailers may restrict a listed product from being sold through other channels; check any exclusivity clause before signing.
- Distributor margins and credit terms: no verified German figures found; treat any quoted margin as a negotiation point.
- Sweeteners: steviol glycosides (E 960) are allowed in hard and soft confectionery, liquorice and nougat when energy-reduced or with no added sugar (Regulation 2017/335); chocolate permissions sit in a separate Annex II category and should be checked per product (unverified here).
- Polyols: generally limited to energy-reduced or no-added-sugar foods; any food with polyols must say "with sweetener(s)" (in German) and over 10% added polyols must carry the laxative warning.
- Claims: "no added sugars" means no added mono- or disaccharides or sweetening ingredients containing them; "sugar-free" means no more than 0.5 g sugars per 100 g (Regulation 1924/2006).
- Labelling: all mandatory information must be in German; shelf life on entry is set by each buyer and was not verified.
- Duty: under the UK EU Trade and Cooperation Agreement goods meeting rules of origin enter tariff free; goods that fail them pay full EU tariffs, so origin paperwork for B Healthy Ltd matters.
