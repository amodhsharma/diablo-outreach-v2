## Market structure

French grocery is national and very concentrated: six groups hold most of the market (Kantar, Feb 2025: E.Leclerc 24.4%, Carrefour 22.3%, Les Mousquetaires 17.0%, Coopérative U 12.4%, Auchan 8.9%, Lidl 7.9%). Listings are agreed at their head office or purchasing centrals, not in Lyon, so a Lyon based partner matters mainly for the region's independents, convenience, specialist and foodservice outlets. Online grocery (drive and home delivery) took 10.6% and proximity stores 7.9% of spend in early 2025. Traditional outlets are still about a fifth of food retail. For sugar-free lines, two further gatekeepers count: the organic and health chains (La Vie Claire runs 387 stores from a logistics platform at Montagny, south of Lyon) and pharmacies, supplied by six wholesalers holding more than 96% of that channel (among them CERP Rhin-Rhône Méditerranée). An importer that holds stock and has its own field sales is usually needed to reach independents, health shops and pharmacies; whether a given chain buys direct or via an importer is unverified and varies by chain.

## Barriers to entry

- Annual negotiations: supplier and retailer contracts must be signed by 1 March each year (EGalim rules); a failed negotiation can end the relationship after one month of mediation, and late contracts risk fines of up to 1 million euros.
- Logistics penalties retailers charge suppliers are capped at 2% of the value ordered (EGalim 3); listing fees and back margins are common but their level for this category is unverified.
- Payment terms: late payment interest applies after the agreed date; B2B terms of up to 60 days can be negotiated.
- Concentration: six groups control most shelf space, so a new brand usually enters through an importer, a regional independent network or the specialist channels before national grocery.
- Sweeteners: polyols and steviol glycosides are allowed in confectionery only under Regulation (EC) 1333/2008 (generally in energy-reduced or no-added-sugar products); each product's sweeteners should be checked against its food category.
- Labelling: products with polyols must say "avec édulcorant(s)"; above 10% added polyols they must carry "une consommation excessive peut avoir des effets laxatifs"; all labels must be in French.
- Claims: "sans sucres" only at 0.5 g of sugars or less per 100 g; "sans sucres ajoutés" only with no added mono- or disaccharides, and "contient des sucres naturellement présents" where relevant.
- Duty: goods that meet the UK and EU Trade and Cooperation Agreement rules of origin enter duty free with a statement on origin; goods made outside the UK (for example in the EU or elsewhere and shipped through the UK) may not qualify and then pay the EU third-country tariff, which for some chocolate lines is around 8.3% plus a possible extra amount for sugar and milk content (unverified per product; check TARIC).
