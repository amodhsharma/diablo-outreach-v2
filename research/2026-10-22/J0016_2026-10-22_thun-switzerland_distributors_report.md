## Market structure

Thun is a German speaking town of the canton of Bern at the gateway to the Bernese Oberland, so it has no separate route to market: product reaches its shelves through national Swiss channels. Grocery is highly concentrated: Nielsen figures reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% and Migros at 37.4% of food and food related sales, and both run central buying, logistics and large private label ranges, so they are the main gatekeepers for any branded snack. Discounters (Aldi, Lidl, Denner) and the Volg village network, supplied centrally by fenaco, add further central buyers. Convenience is led by Valora (k kiosk and avec), with Migrolino and Coop Pronto at petrol stations; these buy centrally or through a small group of kiosk and confectionery wholesalers. Tourist traffic into the Oberland adds kiosk, station and hotel demand, but we found no verified Thun figures. For a foreign brand the practical first step is a national Swiss importer or distributor that already holds kiosk, independent grocer, health food or online listings. We found no new Thun based confectionery distributor.

## Barriers to entry

- Retail concentration: Coop and Migros together hold about 80% of food sales on the November 2025 Nielsen figures, with heavy private label, so shelf space for a new branded range is scarce; listing fees and terms at the big chains are not published and are unverified.
- Price pressure: Swiss shelf prices sit well above neighbouring countries, so the price needs a clear case; Thun is far from the border, so cross border shopping matters less than in border cantons (unverified for Thun).
- Exclusivity and margins: we found no published Swiss norm for distributor margins, credit terms or exclusivity; expect partners to ask for national exclusivity (unverified).
- Import duty: chocolate and other processed agricultural goods pay an industrial element plus variable components that offset raw material price gaps, so duty depends on the recipe; no flat rate could be verified.
- Trade agreement: the UK and Switzerland trade agreement is in force and UK processed agricultural goods face tariffs no higher than EU goods, but only with proof of UK preferential origin; goods only packed or labelled in the UK do not qualify.
- Claims: "sugar free" and similar nutrition claims are allowed only as listed in Annex 13 of the FDHA Ordinance on Information on Foodstuffs; the exact Swiss wording for "no added sugars" is likely, but unverified, to mirror the EU conditions.
- Sweeteners and polyols: Swiss additive rules are understood to follow the EU list by product category (steviol glycosides and polyols permitted), and the EU warning "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect" applies above 10% polyols; the Swiss text was not verified.
- Labelling: German labels suit Thun; Cassis de Dijon lets food lawfully sold in the EU enter, but a product not fully meeting Swiss rules needs an FSVO authorisation; whether UK made goods qualify is unverified.
- Shelf life at entry: no Swiss specific rule found; importers' minimum remaining shelf life requirements are unverified.
Sources: [GOV.UK: UK and Switzerland trade agreement](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/summary-of-the-uk-switzerland-trade-agreement), [SECO: Processed agricultural products](https://www.seco.admin.ch/en/processed-agricultural-products), [FSVO: Nutrition and health claims](https://www.blv.admin.ch/en/nutrition-and-health-claims), [FSVO: Cassis de Dijon principle for food](https://www.blv.admin.ch/en/the-cassis-de-dijon-principle-food)
