## Market structure

Packaged confectionery reaches Lausanne shoppers mainly through the national grocery chains, which buy centrally. Coop and Migros dominate food retail: a Nielsen Retail Monitor comparison reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% and Migros at 37.4% of food and near food sales. Lausanne's Migros stores are run by the regional cooperative Migros Vaud (based in Ecublens), which reported net turnover of about CHF 1.13 billion for 2024, slightly down on 2023; national ranges are set centrally, so a regional listing route for confectionery is unverified. Below the two leaders sit the discounters (Denner, Aldi, Lidl), drugstore and pharmacy chains with a food shelf, organic and health food shops supplied by specialist wholesalers, kiosks and convenience formats, and online grocery and snack shops. For a foreign brand the real gatekeepers are the central category buyers of Coop and Migros and the importers and wholesalers that supply independents and kiosks.

## Closing analysis

- Bodysport first: it already sells diet bars, protein treats, spreads and 0 kcal syrups and sauces from other brands, the widest overlap with Diablo's bars, spreads and syrups among the Lausanne retailers found.
- Sparposten.ch second: its sweets section carries sugar-free Santiveri Maria biscuits and Juanola sweets, so sugar-free biscuits and sweets already sell there.
- Transa Backpacking AG third: its outdoor food section of 121 items carries bars and snacks from several third-party brands, a route for Diablo's bars. No company reached tier 1 and the Channel ran out of web searches, so coverage is partial.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Coop and Migros together hold about 80% of food sales, so a brand outside them depends on discounters, specialists and kiosks. Coop has bought jointly with other European retailers through buying alliances (Coopernic from 2006), which strengthens its terms with brands.
- Listing fees and margin norms: no verified figures found for Swiss listing fees, retailer margins or distributor credit terms; assume central listing negotiations and promotional contributions (unverified).
- Labelling: mandatory information in at least one official language (French for Lausanne), minimum 1.2 mm font, the name and address of the responsible business (the head office need not be in Switzerland) and the country of production.
- Claims: nutrition claims such as "zuckerfrei" are governed by the FDHA Ordinance on Food Information (LIV, Article 29 and Annex 13); the claim must be listed there and its conditions met. The exact Swiss wording for "no added sugar" was not checked here.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted additives are set by the Swiss Additives Ordinance (ZuV), revised in 2024; it broadly follows EU additive rules, but the exact uses for steviol glycosides, polyols and the laxative warning were not verified in this run.
- Duty: under the UK Switzerland trade agreement UK processed agricultural products face no higher tariffs than EU ones, provided UK rules of origin are met (EUR1 or origin declaration). Switzerland still charges variable agricultural components on processed goods such as chocolate, so duty is not zero.
- Shelf life at entry: no Swiss specific rule found; retailer minimums are set by each buyer (unverified).
