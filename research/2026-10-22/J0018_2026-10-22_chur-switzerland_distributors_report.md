## Market structure

Swiss grocery is highly concentrated: Migros and Coop together hold around 70% of grocery retail, with Aldi, Lidl, Denner and Volg making up most of the rest, and private label is reported at around 50% of sales. Migros and Coop buy centrally at national level, so a Chur listing in either is decided in their head offices, not in Graubünden. Outside the two cooperatives, packaged sweets and snacks reach kiosks, petrol station shops, independent grocers, health food shops and vending through national importers and kiosk wholesalers (for example the Valora network for kiosks), most of them based in the Zurich area or Romandie and delivering to Graubünden. No Chur or Graubünden based confectionery distributor that fits Diablo was found in this run: the regional wholesalers found supply restaurants, hotels and hospitals. For a Chur entry, the real gatekeepers are therefore the national importers and wholesalers already researched in earlier Swiss jobs, plus the central buyers at Migros, Coop and the discounters.Sources: [Lloyds Bank Trade: Switzerland distribution](https://www.lloydsbanktrade.com/en/market-potential/switzerland/distribution), [m&k: Promarca lobbies for open shelving](https://www.markt-kom.com/en/?p=31449), [Valora: sales growth 2023](https://www.valora.com/en/info/valora-news/valora-with-strong-sales-growth-in-2023-thanks-to-expansion-of-food-business-id2149)

## Barriers to entry

- Channel lock-up: Migros and Coop (about 70% combined) buy centrally and lean heavily on private label (about 50% of sales), which narrows room for a new branded range. Listing fee levels for Swiss chains were not found and are unverified.
- Distributor terms: margin, exclusivity and credit norms for Swiss confectionery importers were not found in public sources; expect to negotiate them case by case (unverified).
- Trade agreement: UK and Swiss goods trade runs under the Switzerland-UK trade agreement, which carries over the Swiss-EU free trade and agriculture provisions, including preferences for processed agricultural products. Rules of origin apply and UK inputs cannot be cumulated with EU inputs.
- Import duty: Switzerland abolished tariffs on industrial goods from 1 January 2024, but processed food such as chocolate and biscuits falls under agricultural tariff rules; the exact duty per product must be checked in the Swiss tariff (Tares). Not verified for Diablo's lines.
- Cassis de Dijon: foods lawfully sold in the EU can enter Switzerland more easily, but foods that do not fully comply with Swiss rules need FSVO authorisation; the page covers EU products only and how it treats UK made goods is unverified.
- Importer responsibility: under self-monitoring, the Swiss importer must ensure goods comply and all required consumer labelling is shown; language rules (Swiss practice is at least one official language, here German for Chur) were not confirmed in this run.
- Sweeteners and claims: Swiss food law is broadly aligned with EU rules on additives and nutrition claims, including the polyol laxative warning ("excessive consumption may produce laxative effects") and the sugar-free and no-added-sugar claim conditions; the Swiss texts were not checked in this run, so treat exact thresholds as unverified.
- Shelf life at entry: no published Swiss norm was found (unverified).
