## Market structure

Swiss grocery is highly concentrated. Nielsen data reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% and Migros at 37.4% of food and near food sales, with Denner, Aldi, Lidl and Volg sharing most of the rest; earlier sources report private label at around half of sales. Migros and Coop buy branded confectionery centrally, so a Fribourg listing is decided at national level. Migros runs Fribourg through its Neuchâtel-Fribourg regional cooperative (head office in Marin-Epagnier), which may buy some regional lines, but whether it lists imported brands independently is unverified. Fribourg is bilingual (French and German) and sits between the Romandie and Bern trade areas, so it is mostly served by importers and wholesalers based around Lausanne, Geneva, Bern and Zurich rather than by local firms. Outside the big chains, packaged sweets and snacks reach kiosks and convenience stores (largely the Valora network), petrol station shops, independent grocers, health food shops and vending through these national importers and cash and carry wholesalers such as Aligro and Prodega. In this run no new Fribourg based distributor fitting Diablo was found: the national players that serve Fribourg are already known from earlier Swiss jobs.

## Barriers to entry

- Channel lock-up: Coop and Migros together hold about 80% of food and near food sales (Nielsen, 2025) and lean heavily on private label, leaving little shelf room for a new branded range. Listing fee levels were not found and are unverified.
- Distributor terms: margin, exclusivity and credit norms for Swiss confectionery importers were not found in public sources; expect to negotiate case by case (unverified).
- Trade agreement: UK and Swiss goods trade runs under the UK-Switzerland trade agreement, which carries over most existing preferences; the UK food industry says trade in most products is already tariff free but processed products struggle with strict rules of origin. A new UK-Switzerland agreement was published in April 2025 (TS No.20/2025); its effect on confectionery duty was not checked.
- Import duty: Switzerland abolished tariffs on industrial goods from 1 January 2024, but processed food such as chocolate and biscuits stays under agricultural tariff rules; check the exact duty per line in the Swiss tariff (Tares). Not verified for Diablo's lines.
- Cassis de Dijon: foods lawfully sold in the EU can enter more easily, but foods that do not meet Swiss rules need FSVO authorisation; the FSVO page covers EU products only, so how it treats UK made goods is unverified.
- Labelling language: Swiss law reportedly allows mandatory food information in just one national language or English (April 2026 report), but French and German labels are commercially important in bilingual Fribourg. Importer self-monitoring makes the Swiss importer responsible for compliance.
- Sweeteners and claims: Swiss food law is broadly aligned with EU rules on additives and nutrition claims, including the polyol laxative warning and the sugar-free and no-added-sugar claim conditions; the Swiss texts were not checked in this run, so treat exact thresholds as unverified.
- Shelf life at entry: no published Swiss norm was found (unverified).
