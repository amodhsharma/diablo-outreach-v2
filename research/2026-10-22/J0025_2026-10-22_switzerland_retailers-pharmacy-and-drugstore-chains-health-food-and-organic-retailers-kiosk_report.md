## Market structure

Swiss grocery is held by two cooperatives: Coop has about 43% and Migros about 37% of food and related sales (Nielsen, reported November 2025), and about half of their sales are own label, so national supermarket shelf space is decided at their head offices. Denner, Volg, Aldi and Lidl make up most of the rest, and discounters are growing. Pharmacy and drugstore chains, health food and organic shops, kiosks and station shops, and online shops are the channels outside the big two where an imported sugar free brand can realistically start. Kiosk, convenience and many independent shelves are reached through national importers and wholesalers that hold stock, most of which are already in Diablo's pipeline from earlier Swiss jobs. Online took a growing share of retail (around 11.8% in 2020; current figures unverified), with Galaxus and other large Swiss shops acting as gatekeepers for online orders. ## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Coop and Migros hold most grocery sales and push own label; listings with them are national and slow. Listing fees, retailer fees and their size are unverified.
- Distributor margin, credit and exclusivity norms in Switzerland are unverified; treat any figure as unconfirmed.
- UK trade agreement: the UK and Switzerland trade agreement is in force; UK processed agricultural goods pay no higher tariffs than EU goods. Preferential origin needs a EUR1 or origin declaration.
- Import duty: chocolate, biscuits and similar processed farm products pay an industrial element plus variable agricultural amounts; exact rates for Diablo's HS codes are unverified.
- Market access: under the Cassis de Dijon principle many foods lawfully sold in the EU can be sold in Switzerland; foods that do not fully meet Swiss rules need an FSVO general authorisation.
- Sweeteners and polyols: steviol glycosides (E 960a to E 960d) are allowed in certain foods under the additives ordinance (ZuV), which sets permitted sweeteners by food category; each product should be checked against it.
- Claims and warnings: sugar free and no added sugar claim conditions and the polyol laxative warning are understood to mirror EU rules, but this is unverified against the Swiss text.
- Labelling: mandatory information in at least one official language (German, French or Italian), plus the name and address of the manufacturer, importer or seller and the country of production; national chains usually expect German and French (unverified).
- Shelf life at entry: no Swiss specific rule found; set by buyer terms (unverified).
