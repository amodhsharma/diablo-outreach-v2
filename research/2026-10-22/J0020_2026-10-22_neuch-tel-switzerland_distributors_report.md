## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly: Nielsen data reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% and Migros at 37.4% of food and near-food sales, with discounters (Aldi, Lidl, Denner) and convenience making up most of the rest. Both cooperatives buy centrally in German-speaking Switzerland, so a listing for Neuchâtel shelves is decided at national head office level, not in the canton; regional Migros cooperatives (Migros Neuchâtel-Fribourg) add some local listings (unverified for this category). Convenience is the second gate: Valora runs k kiosk and avec, including more than 170 petrol station shops, and Coop Pronto and migrolino run their own formats. Independent grocers, kiosks and health food shops are reached through national wholesalers and confectionery distributors, almost all based outside the canton, which deliver into Neuchâtel. Neuchâtel is a small, French-speaking market with no wholesaler of its own for branded sweets found in this run; its local food wholesalers mainly serve restaurants. Online, Galaxus, Coop.ch and Migros Online lead (unverified share figures).

## Barriers to entry

- Coop and Migros control about 80% of food sales and push their own brands; a national distributor is needed to reach the rest of the trade. Listing fees and margin norms are not published (unverified).
- Duty on EU confectionery: under Protocol 2 Switzerland levies a tariff equal to the Swiss and EU price gap on the farm ingredients (milk, flour and so on); products whose only farm ingredient is sugar pay none. How this applies to UK goods is unverified.
- UK trade: the UK and Switzerland concluded an enhanced free trade agreement in July 2026, expected in force in 2027; until then the 2021 continuity agreement applies. Its terms for processed food are unverified.
- Claims: "sugar free" and other nutrition claims must match the conditions in Annex 13 of the FDHA Ordinance on Information on Foodstuffs; "no added sugar" conditions were not confirmed in this run but are believed to follow the EU wording (unverified).
- Sweeteners and polyols: Swiss additive rules broadly follow EU Regulation 1333/2008, so steviol glycosides are permitted by product category; the polyol laxative warning (for more than 10% added polyols) is believed to apply as in the EU (unverified).
- Labelling: Switzerland asks for more origin detail than the EU, giving the country of production. EU labelled foods that do not fully meet Swiss rules can be sold under the Cassis de Dijon principle only after an FSVO authorisation; whether UK labelled goods qualify is not stated by the FSVO.
- Import control: importers must run their own checks that goods meet Swiss law; shelf life norms at entry were not found (unverified).
