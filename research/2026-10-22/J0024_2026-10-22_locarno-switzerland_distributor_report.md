## Market structure

Locarno is a small Italian speaking town in Ticino, served by the national Swiss grocery system rather than a separate regional one. Migros and Coop together hold roughly 70% of Swiss grocery retail, and about half of sales are their own private label, so the two cooperatives are the main gatekeepers for supermarket shelf space. Migros runs in Ticino through its own regional cooperative (Migros Ticino). Denner, Volg, Aldi and Lidl make up most of the rest. Branded confectionery outside the big two reaches kiosks, petrol station shops, independent grocers and vending largely through national and Ticino based wholesalers and importers, most of which are already in Diablo's pipeline. Online took a growing share of retail (around 11.8% in 2020); current figures are unverified. Ticino retail is also exposed to shopping across the border in Italy: Migros reported a sharp fall in Ticino supermarket sales in 2022 as cross border shopping returned after the pandemic. For a new brand the realistic route is a national importer or confectionery wholesaler that also covers Ticino, not a Locarno only partner.

## Barriers to entry

- Retail concentration: Migros and Coop control about 70% of grocery and push private label (about 50% of sales), limiting space for imported brands. Listing fees and their size were not verified.
- Distributor margin, credit and exclusivity norms in Switzerland could not be verified in this run; treat any figure as unconfirmed.
- Local market size: Locarno and Ticino are small and lose spend to Italian shops across the border, so partners will usually want national or at least Ticino wide coverage.
- UK trade agreement: the UK and Switzerland trade agreement is in force; UK processed agricultural goods are charged no higher tariffs than EU goods. Preferential origin needs a EUR1 or origin declaration, and relabelling or repacking EU goods in the UK does not confer UK origin.
- Duty on chocolate and sugar: Switzerland applies agricultural component duties on processed foods; with the EU a "double zero" arrangement removes duty on sugar in processed products. Exact rates for Diablo's HS codes were not verified.
- Sweeteners and polyols: Switzerland's additives ordinance (ZuV) sets permitted sweeteners by food category and broadly follows EU lists; whether every sweetener in Diablo's range is approved for each Swiss category was not verified.
- Claims and warnings: Swiss "sugar free" and "no added sugar" claim conditions and the polyol laxative warning are understood to mirror EU rules, but this was not confirmed against the Swiss text in this run.
- Labelling: Swiss labelling is largely aligned with EU rules under the FDHA ordinance on food information; the manufacturer or importer is responsible. Language rules (at least one official language) and Swiss importer details were not confirmed here; for Ticino, Italian labels are expected in practice.
- Shelf life at entry: no Swiss specific rule found; set by buyer terms (unverified).
