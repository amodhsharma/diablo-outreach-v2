## Market structure

Packaged confectionery reaches Bern shoppers mainly through the national grocery chains, which buy centrally. Coop and Migros dominate food retail: a Nielsen Retail Monitor comparison reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% and Migros at 37.4% of food and near food sales, with Lidl planning 300 more stores. Migros works through regional cooperatives, and Bern is served by Migros Aare (cantons of Bern, Solothurn and Aargau, about 12,000 staff and CHF 3.4 billion turnover in 2018), so some local listings may pass through it, though national ranges are set centrally (unverified for confectionery). Below the two leaders sit the discounters (Denner, Aldi, Lidl), drugstore and pharmacy chains with a food shelf, organic and health food shops supplied by specialist wholesalers, kiosks and convenience formats, and online grocery and snack shops. For a foreign brand the real gatekeepers are the central category buyers of Coop and Migros and the importers and wholesalers that already supply independents and kiosks.

## Closing analysis

- Only one Bern retailer passed every rule, so approach LOLA first: two organic neighbourhood shops in Bern plus an online shop, already selling other brands' chocolate, wafer bars, crisps and spreads (iChoc, Love Raw, Rapunzel).
- Its vegan and organic focus suits Diablo's vegan muesli bars, wafer, cookie, peanut butters and jams, and no direct sugar-free competitor was seen on its shelf.
- It is small (tier 3), so treat it as a local test listing rather than a route to wide reach in Bern.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Coop and Migros together hold about 80% of food sales, so a brand outside them depends on discounters, specialists and kiosks. Coop has bought jointly with other European retailers through buying alliances (Coopernic from 2006), which strengthens its terms with brands.
- Listing fees and margin norms: no verified figures found for Swiss listing fees, retailer margins or distributor credit terms; assume central listing negotiations and promotional contributions (unverified).
- Labelling: mandatory information in at least one official language (German for Bern), minimum 1.2 mm font, the name and address of the responsible business (the head office need not be in Switzerland) and the country of production.
- Claims: nutrition claims such as "zuckerfrei" are governed by the FDHA Ordinance on Food Information (LIV, Article 29 and Annex 13); the claim must be listed there and its conditions met. The exact Swiss wording for "no added sugar" was not checked here.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted additives are set by the Swiss Additives Ordinance (ZuV), revised in 2024; it broadly follows EU additive rules, but the exact uses for steviol glycosides, polyols and the laxative warning were not verified in this run.
- Duty: under the UK Switzerland trade agreement UK processed agricultural products face no higher tariffs than EU ones, provided UK rules of origin are met (EUR1 or origin declaration). Switzerland still charges variable agricultural components on processed goods such as chocolate to offset raw material price gaps, so duty is not zero.
- Shelf life at entry: no Swiss specific rule found; retailer minimums are set by each buyer (unverified).
