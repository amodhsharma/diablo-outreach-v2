## Market structure

German grocery is one of Europe's most concentrated markets. Edeka, the Schwarz Group (Lidl and Kaufland), Rewe and Aldi hold about 85% of food retail between them; in 2024 Edeka took 61.4 billion euros, Schwarz 55.2 billion, Rewe 40.1 billion and Aldi 35.8 billion. Their national and regional buying offices are the real gatekeepers for mainstream shelf. Edeka and Rewe also buy through regional companies; Thuringia, including Jena, falls under EDEKA Nordbayern-Sachsen-Thüringen (not verified in this run). Drugstores (dm and Rossmann, which has a store in Jena centre) are a strong route for better-for-you and sugar free snacks, alongside Reformhaus and organic stores supplied by organic wholesalers. Impulse and convenience outlets (kiosks, petrol stations) are reached through national impulse wholesalers such as Lekkerland. A city of about 110,000, Jena has no confectionery importer of its own; it is served from Erfurt, the Leipzig area and national players, so a national importer with regional field sales is the realistic route.

## Barriers to entry

- **Dominant players:** the big four hold about 85 to 90% of grocery, so there is little room to sell around them; their private label also competes hard with branded confectionery.
- **Listing fees and terms:** chains charge listing fees before first delivery, shelf rent for good placement and contributions to promotions and store openings, and may restrict selling the listed product elsewhere. The source is from 2017 and no longer updated, so current amounts are unverified.
- **Distributor margin and exclusivity:** norms could not be verified in this run; expect a German importer to ask for national exclusivity.
- **Claims:** under Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006, "sugar free" (zuckerfrei) needs no more than 0.5 g sugars per 100 g, and "no added sugars" (ohne Zuckerzusatz) needs no added mono- or disaccharides or sugary ingredients; naturally present sugars must then be declared.
- **Sweeteners and polyols:** polyols are allowed at quantum satis in foods with no added sugars or reduced energy; above 10% added polyols the label must say "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects", in German. Steviol glycosides (E 960) are permitted in the EU; levels per product type were not checked against the annex in this run.
- **Labelling:** German-language labelling is needed, including "with sweetener(s)" (mit Süßungsmittel(n)).
- **Duty and origin:** UK to EU trade is tariff free only for goods that meet the Trade and Cooperation Agreement's rules of origin; confectionery not sufficiently processed in the UK pays EU duty. Supplying from stock already held in the EU through Diablo's Prague entity avoids the UK origin question (to confirm with the logistics team).
- **Shelf life at entry:** no German norm could be verified; agree minimum remaining shelf life with each partner.
