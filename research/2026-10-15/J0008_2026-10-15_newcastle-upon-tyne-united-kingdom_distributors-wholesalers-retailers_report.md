## Market structure

Packaged confectionery reaches UK shoppers mainly through the national grocery multiples (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Aldi, Lidl, Co-op, Waitrose, Iceland) and the discounters B&M and Home Bargains. These retailers buy direct from brand owners or a UK importer and sit under the Groceries Supply Code of Practice. The second route is the independent and symbol convenience sector (SPAR, Nisa, Londis, Costcutter), supplied by cash and carry chains such as Booker and Bestway and by delivered impulse wholesalers. Kitwave, headquartered in North Shields on Tyneside, is a delivered wholesaler of impulse products, groceries and chilled and frozen food to independent convenience and foodservice outlets, with low minimum orders. Small single-depot specialists serving roughly a 50 mile radius compete with it and are often acquired by it. Health food stores and specialist outlets are reached through natural food wholesalers. Online, Amazon is a designated GSCOP retailer. The real gatekeepers are therefore the central buyers of the multiples and the category teams of the large wholesalers and symbol groups; Newcastle has little separate buying power beyond regional names such as Fenwick. Category shares by channel were not verified.

## Closing analysis

- Epicurium first: a Consett healthy snacks distributor 15 miles from Newcastle whose whole proposition is better-for-you indulgence, with a director-led structure that makes a decision maker reachable.
- Hancocks second: the UK's specialist confectionery cash and carry already runs a sugar-free category and has a Newcastle depot, so Diablo's chocolate and biscuit range fills a clear gap for Tyneside retailers.
- Holland & Barrett third: it already lists Diablo, so the ask is a wider range and Newcastle store distribution rather than a new listing; it carries the competitor Free'ist, so the pitch must be differentiated.

## Barriers to entry

- Groceries Supply Code of Practice restricts listing fees and marketing charges at retailers with UK grocery turnover above £1 billion, but suppliers still report "pay to stay" pressure; exact listing fee levels were not verified.
- Wholesaler margins and credit terms for confectionery were not verified in this run; expect to agree them per partner.
- HFSS rules in England: confectionery is category 4. Stores of qualifying businesses (50 or more staff, 185.8 m² or larger) cannot place HFSS products within 2 m of checkouts, at aisle ends or near entrances (since 1 October 2022), and volume promotions such as BOGOF have been banned since 1 October 2025.
- Sugar free does not mean exempt: HFSS status comes from the nutrient profiling score (4 or more points), so a high fat sugar free chocolate may still be HFSS. Each SKU needs scoring (unverified for the Diablo range).
- TV (before 21:00) and paid online advertising of less healthy food are restricted from 5 January 2026.
- Labelling: "with sweetener(s)" must accompany the name; more than 10% added polyols requires "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects". Aspartame warnings do not apply to this range.
- Claims: "sugar free" only at 0.5 g of sugars or less per 100 g; "no added sugars" conditions were not re-checked this run.
- Import: as the exporter is a UK entity (B Healthy Ltd), goods made in the EU enter the UK at zero tariff under the UK and EU Trade and Cooperation Agreement only with a statement on origin or importer's knowledge; non-originating goods pay UK Global Tariff rates (rate for these lines unverified).
