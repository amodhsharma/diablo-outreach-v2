## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in Durban reach shoppers mainly through the national grocery chains (Shoprite and Checkers, Pick n Pay, Spar and Woolworths), which buy centrally and supply stores in KwaZulu-Natal from their own regional distribution centres. Spar works through independently owned stores fed by regional distribution centres, so both the KwaZulu-Natal distribution centre and the larger store owners decide ranging. Durban has South Africa's busiest container port, so many importers and logistics firms keep warehouses in and around the city and clear imported food there before it moves inland to Gauteng. Beside the chains, cash and carry wholesalers, buying groups and van sales distributors supply independent grocers, forecourt shops and the large informal (spaza) trade across the province. Health and free from products also sell through pharmacy and health chains and online shops. For an imported niche range, the realistic gatekeeper is an importer or distributor that already holds chain listings and handles labelling, registration and logistics; many such firms are based in Johannesburg rather than Durban. The share of each route in Durban was not verified in this run.

## Barriers to entry

- Chain power: the Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry found that suppliers face rebates, listing and promotional costs set by the large chains, which weigh most on small suppliers. Exact fee levels were not verified.
- Exclusive lease agreements long kept rival retailers out of malls; the inquiry asked chains to end them, which slowly opens space for independents and specialist shops.
- Distributor margin and credit terms for imported confectionery were not verified in this run; expect the distributor to ask for marketing support and to hold stock in rand.
- Sweeteners: permitted sweeteners and their use in foodstuffs are set by the Sweeteners in Foodstuffs regulations (R733 of 2012). Stevia and polyol use for each product type must be checked against that list before shipping.
- Labelling: the draft labelling and advertising regulations (R3337 of 2023) propose front of pack warning labels and tighter rules on nutrient and health claims, including sugar free and no added sugar claims. Their final form and start date were still delayed at last report, so this is unverified.
- Laxative warning: a polyol warning statement is expected under the labelling rules, but the exact wording required in South Africa was not verified in this run.
- Sugar tax: the Health Promotion Levy applies to sugary beverages only, not to confectionery; it raises consumer interest in lower sugar products.
- Duty: the SACUM and UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives preferential tariffs on many UK goods entering South Africa; the exact duty for chocolate and sugar confectionery under it was not verified.
