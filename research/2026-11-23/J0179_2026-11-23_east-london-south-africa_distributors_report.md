## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in East London (Buffalo City) reach shoppers mainly through the national grocery chains (Shoprite and Checkers, Pick n Pay, Spar and Woolworths), which buy centrally and feed Eastern Cape stores from regional distribution centres. Spar works through independently owned stores fed by regional distribution centres, so the distribution centre and the larger store owners both decide ranging. Beside the chains, cash and carry wholesalers and van sales distributors supply independent grocers, forecourt shops and the large township spaza trade in Mdantsane and the former Ciskei and Transkei areas; provincial officials closed a number of spaza shops and wholesalers in early 2026 over food safety and compliance. East London has a port, but it is small and mainly handles vehicles and bulk cargo; most imported packaged food still arrives through Durban, Gqeberha or Cape Town and reaches East London via a national importer. For an imported niche range the real gatekeeper is a national importer or distributor that already holds chain listings and handles labelling and logistics. The share of each route in East London was not verified.

## Barriers to entry

- Chain power: the Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry found that suppliers face rebates, listing and promotional costs set by the large chains, which weigh most on small suppliers. Exact fee levels were not verified.
- Independent and informal trade is large but fragmented and under tighter compliance checks since 2026, so reaching it needs a distributor with van sales or cash and carry customers; margin and credit norms for imported confectionery were not verified.
- Sweeteners: permitted sweeteners and their use in foodstuffs are set by the Sweeteners in Foodstuffs regulations (R733 of 2012). Stevia and polyol use for each product type must be checked against that list before shipping.
- Labelling: the draft labelling and advertising regulations (R3337 of 2023) propose front of pack warning labels and tighter rules on nutrient claims, including sugar free and no added sugar. Their final form and start date were not verified.
- Laxative warning: a polyol warning statement is expected under the labelling rules; the exact wording required was not verified.
- Sugar tax: the Health Promotion Levy applies to sugary beverages only, not to confectionery.
- Duty: the SACUM and UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives preferential tariffs on many UK goods; the exact duty for chocolate and sugar confectionery under it was not verified.
