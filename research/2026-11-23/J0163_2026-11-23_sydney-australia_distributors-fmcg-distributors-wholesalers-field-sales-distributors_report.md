## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in Sydney reach shelf mainly through the two national supermarket chains, Woolworths and Coles, which buy centrally and take stock into their own distribution centres. Aldi is the third chain buyer, largely private label. Independent supermarkets (IGA, Foodworks and others) are supplied mainly through Metcash, the dominant grocery wholesaler. Convenience, petrol, newsagent and small grocery outlets are served by confectionery and convenience distributors, many grouped in buying networks such as The Distributors, and by national brand houses such as Stuart Alexander that sell imported brands to all channels. Pharmacy chains and health food stores are a secondary route for sugar free ranges. Online, the grocery chains' own sites and Amazon Australia matter most. The real gatekeepers are the central category buyers at Woolworths, Coles and Metcash, and the handful of national importers and distributors that already hold most international confectionery principals. Note: this run could not verify current market shares or the latest buying arrangements because the web search budget for the run was used up; treat this section as a summary to confirm.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Woolworths and Coles hold most grocery sales, so a national listing usually needs a strong distributor or importer with an existing buyer relationship.
- Distributor channel locked up: most national confectionery and convenience distributors serving Sydney already appear on Diablo's exclusion list, so few new partners are left.
- Listing and promotional costs: chains commonly expect promotional funding and trade terms; exact listing fees and distributor margins were not verified in this run.
- Labelling: Food Standards Code rules apply, including the nutrition information panel in Australian format, an Australian importer's or supplier's name and address, and country of origin labelling where it applies to the product type (unverified for each SKU).
- Sweeteners and claims: stevia (steviol glycosides) and polyols are permitted food additives under the Code; "sugar free" and "no added sugar" are nutrition content claims with set conditions, and foods with high polyol content need an advisory statement about laxative effects (thresholds not verified in this run).
- Imported food controls: the Department of Agriculture's Imported Food Inspection Scheme may inspect and test consignments at the border; shelf life at entry expectations were not verified.
- Trade: the Australia UK Free Trade Agreement is in force and removes most tariffs on UK goods; the duty treatment for each product line should be confirmed against the tariff schedule.
