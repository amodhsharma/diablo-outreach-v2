## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in Australia reach shelf mainly through the grocery majors. Woolworths and Coles hold most of the supermarket market, with Aldi a strong third and Metcash supplying the independent IGA network as the main wholesale gatekeeper (shares not re-verified this run). Woolworths and Coles buy centrally into their own distribution centres and are growing their own brand ranges, which squeezes shelf space for imported brands. A newcomer therefore usually needs a local importer or distributor that already holds supplier accounts with the majors and Metcash. Convenience, petrol and route trade is served by specialist wholesalers such as the members of The Distributors group. Health food shops, pharmacy chains, independent grocers and online grocery are the likeliest first channels for a sugar free range. Nearly all of the large national confectionery distributors are already on Diablo's contact list, so new partners in this market are mostly mid sized specialist importers. Amazon Australia and the grocers' own online shops matter for online orders (unverified this run).

## Barriers to entry

- Grocery majors expect trading terms and promotional support from suppliers; exact listing fees are not public (unverified).
- Local distributors commonly ask for exclusivity by channel or nationally; margin and credit norms were not verified this run.
- Sweeteners such as steviol glycosides and polyols must be permitted for each food category under the Food Standards Code; check product by product.
- Foods with added polyols above set levels must carry an advisory statement that excess consumption may have a laxative effect (Standard 1.2.3).
- "Sugar free" and "no added sugar" are nutrition content claims with conditions set by FSANZ; UK pack wording must be checked against them.
- Imports need Australian labelling, including an Australian importer name and address and Country of Origin Labelling; shelf life at entry is usually set by the importer (unverified).
- The Australia UK Free Trade Agreement removed tariffs on UK confectionery and most other UK goods from entry into force, subject to rules of origin.
