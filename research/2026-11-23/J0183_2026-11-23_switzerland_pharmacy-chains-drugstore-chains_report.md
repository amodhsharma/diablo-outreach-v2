## Market structure

Swiss food retail is a duopoly: Coop holds about 43% and Migros 37.4% of food and near food sales (Nielsen), and both also control health channels. Pharmacy is the gatekeeper channel for a health positioned range. Of roughly 1,830 pharmacies, about two thirds are independent. Galenica dominates the chains with Amavita (196) and Sun Store (84) and owns 51% of Coop Vitality (86), roughly 55% of chain outlets. Galenica also owns Galexis, the main pharmacy wholesaler, Alloga (pre-wholesale logistics) and Winconcept, which markets to independent partner pharmacies, so one listing conversation can reach most of the channel. Other chains are BENU (102), Dr. Bähler DROPA (73, pharmacies and drugstores) and Migros owned Medbase (49) plus the former Zur Rose online pharmacy. Independents buy through groups such as TopPharm and Dr. Gurtner. Drugstores are smaller: about 339 drugstore companies, mostly independent, while Müller runs about 90 Swiss stores helped by Migros sites and Rossmann is entering with a real food aisle including sweets. dm is absent, selling Balea only via Manor. Online, Migros owned Galaxus is likely the key marketplace (unverified). A Swiss importer holding stock and acting as legal importer is the usual entry route.

## Barriers to entry

- Channel concentration: Galenica controls the largest pharmacy chains, the main wholesaler and the independent marketing platform; being declined there closes much of pharmacy at once.
- Migros politics: Migros reportedly steers its sites towards Müller and away from Rossmann, and owns Medbase and Galaxus, so channel choices can be read as taking sides.
- Exclusivity: importers commonly expect national exclusivity; not verified for this category in this run.
- Listing fees, promotional fees, distributor margins and credit terms: no verified Swiss figures found in this run; treat any quoted norm as unverified and ask partners directly.
- Quick commerce: not a material channel for this range based on what was found; unverified.
- Labelling language: mandatory information must appear in at least one official language (German, French or Italian); multilingual DE/FR/IT packs are the practical norm for national listing.
- Country of production must be stated on pack; for processed foods a region such as "EU" can be used. Name and address of the responsible party, including the Swiss importer, is required.
- Importer liability: the Swiss importer is responsible for compliance through self monitoring; cantonal chemists carry out official controls.
- Sweeteners: governed by the Swiss Additives Ordinance (ZuV), which the market generally treats as aligned with EU rules on steviol glycosides and polyols by food category; check each SKU against the ZuV annexes (unverified per product).
- Laxative warning: the ZuV requires "may have a laxative effect if consumed in excess" for polyol based sweetener preparations; the equivalent rule for foods with over 10% polyols is believed to mirror the EU but was not verified in this run.
- Claims: nutrition claims must be on the FoodIO Annex 13 list; health claims on Annex 14 or FSVO authorised. "Suitable for diabetics" claims are banned. "No added sugar" is defined in the ZuV as no added mono or disaccharides; the exact "sugar free" threshold is believed to mirror the EU 0.5 g per 100 g but was not verified.
- Shelf life at entry: no regulatory minimum found; retailer minimums not verified.
- Import duty: chocolate, biscuits and similar processed agricultural products still carry duties with a variable agricultural component that offsets Swiss raw material prices; the 2024 abolition of industrial tariffs does not remove this. Exact rates per tariff line not checked.
- UK trade: the UK Switzerland trade agreement is in force and UK processed agricultural goods are charged no more than EU rates, subject to rules of origin (EUR1 or origin declaration, EU materials can count). UK origin must be proved to qualify.
