## Market structure

Durban is South Africa's main container port, so many national importers clear goods there, but buying decisions for formal retail are made centrally. Shoprite and Checkers, Pick n Pay, SPAR (through its KwaZulu-Natal distribution centre), Woolworths and Boxer buy into their own distribution centres, so a Durban partner must be able to supply those DCs or sit inside a national distributor. KwaZulu-Natal also has a large independent and informal trade (spaza shops, township supermarkets and independent grocers) served by cash and carry wholesalers, buying groups and field sales distributors with van sales; national estimates put independent trade at well over R200 billion a year (figure unverified). This run's search budget ran out after one Durban search, so local gatekeepers are not named here and this section rests mostly on the national picture (unverified for Durban specifically). Online grocery is led by the retailers' own apps (Checkers Sixty60, Pick n Pay asap!) and Takealot.

## Barriers to entry

- Listing and trade terms: national chains charge listing fees, rebates and marketing contributions; the Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry (2019) flagged supplier rebates and exclusive mall leases. Exact fee levels are not public.
- Distributor terms: importers usually want national exclusivity and expect margin to cover warehousing, van sales and retailer credit (norms unverified for this category).
- Dominant players: Mondelez (Cadbury), Nestle, Mars, Beacon (Tiger Brands) and Ferrero hold most confectionery shelf space.
- Sweeteners: permitted sweeteners and limits are set by R733 of 2012 under the Foodstuffs, Cosmetics and Disinfectants Act; check steviol glycosides and polyols against the product tables.
- Claims and labels: R146 (2010) governs labelling and sugar-free and no-added-sugar claims; labels in English with a South African importer named. Polyol laxative warning wording and threshold unverified. Draft regulations R3337 (2023) propose front-of-pack warnings (final status unverified).
- Duty: the SACU and Mozambique and UK EPA gives preferential access for UK origin goods; the rate for chocolate and sugar confectionery is unverified.
- Shelf life: a large share of remaining shelf life at entry is usually expected (norm unverified).
