## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in Melbourne reach shelf mainly through the two national supermarket chains, Woolworths and Coles, which buy centrally and dominate grocery; Aldi and the Metcash supplied independents (IGA, Foodworks) follow. Their combined share is widely reported to be high, but this run could not confirm a current figure. Imported and niche brands usually enter through a specialist importer or distributor that holds stock in a Melbourne or Sydney warehouse, clears imports and sells into supermarket head offices, independents, pharmacy, health food and convenience. Route trade (petrol, convenience, milk bars, vending) is served by convenience distributors and wholesale groups with field sales teams. The real gatekeepers are the central buyers at the large chains and the handful of national importers and convenience distributors that already hold most international confectionery agencies; this run's research found that nearly all such Melbourne companies were already known to Diablo. The supermarket side is overseen by the Food and Grocery Code and its supervisor. Market share figures in this section are unverified for this run.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentrated grocery: the large chains buy centrally, so a national listing usually needs a distributor with supermarket relationships; promotional funding and listing terms apply but exact fees were not verified in this run.
- Distributors often ask for exclusivity for Australia (sometimes with New Zealand); margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Many international confectionery agencies are already held by established importers and convenience distributors, which limits the open partners in Melbourne.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted by the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code by food type; products with added polyols above set levels need an advisory statement that excess consumption may have a laxative effect. Exact limits for each product were not checked.
- Claims: "sugar free" and "no added sugar" are nutrition content claims with set conditions under the Code; each SKU's label should be checked against them before export.
- Labelling: Australian labels need an Australian importer or supplier name and address, country of origin labelling and allergen declarations in the Code's format, so UK packs usually need over-stickering or a local label.
- Imported food: shipments are subject to biosecurity clearance and the Imported Food Inspection Scheme; shelf life at entry expectations were not verified.
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement (in force since 31 May 2023) removed tariffs on most UK goods, so UK made confectionery should enter duty free if it meets the rules of origin; this should be confirmed for each tariff line.
