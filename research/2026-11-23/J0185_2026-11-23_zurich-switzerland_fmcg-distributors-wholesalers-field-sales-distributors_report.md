## Market structure

Swiss grocery is one of Europe's most concentrated markets. Coop and Migros together hold most of food retail, and Coop overtook Migros in its core food business in 2025; Denner (owned by Migros), Aldi, Lidl, Volg and Spar follow. All buy centrally, so in Zurich, as across the country, the chain buyers are the main gatekeepers for shelf space. Beside the chains, a small group of national wholesalers and kiosk and convenience distributors supply independent grocers, kiosks, petrol station shops, vending operators and the cash and carry trade, and most of them already sit on Diablo's exclusion list from earlier Swiss research. Many international confectionery brands sell through their own Swiss subsidiaries, leaving fewer independent importers than in other markets of this size. Online grocery runs mainly through the chains' own platforms. The share of each route for imported sugar free confectionery in Zurich was not verified.

## Barriers to entry

- Chain concentration: Coop and Migros between them control most shelf space and buy centrally, so a listing decision by a few buyers decides national reach. Listing fee levels were not verified.
- Fewer independent importers: many confectionery brand owners run their own Swiss subsidiaries, and the main kiosk and convenience wholesalers are few; distributor margin and exclusivity norms were not verified.
- Sweeteners and additives: permitted sweeteners, including steviol glycosides and polyols, are set by the Additives Ordinance (ZuV), which largely mirrors EU rules; use for each product type must be checked before shipping.
- Claims: nutrition claims such as sugar free and no added sugar are governed by the Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office rules on nutrition and health claims, broadly aligned with the EU conditions.
- Labelling: labels must be in at least one Swiss official language, and foods with more than 10 per cent added polyols carry the excessive consumption laxative warning as in the EU; exact Swiss wording was not verified.
- Duty: the UK and Switzerland trade agreement continues the earlier EU based terms; processed agricultural products such as chocolate and biscuits can still face an agricultural component on import. The exact rate for Diablo's range was not verified.
Sources: [Food labelling: legal requirements (FSVO)](https://www.blv.admin.ch/en/legal-requirements-food-labelling), [Nutrition and health claims (FSVO)](https://www.blv.admin.ch/en/nutrition-and-health-claims), [Additives Ordinance (ZuV)](https://lawbrary.ch/law/817_022_31/ZuV/v2024.02/de), [Food labeling: the main principles](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/kmu/en/home/concrete-know-how/sme-management/labeling/food-labeling.html), [Summary of the UK Switzerland trade agreement](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/summary-of-the-uk-switzerland-trade-agreement?lang=en-gb), [Processed agricultural products](https://www.europa.eda.admin.ch/en/processed-agricultural-products)
