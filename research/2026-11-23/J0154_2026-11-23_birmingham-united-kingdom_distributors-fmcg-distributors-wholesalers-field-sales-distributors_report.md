## Market structure

In the United Kingdom packaged confectionery reaches shelf through two routes. The big grocery multiples (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Aldi, Lidl) buy centrally and are reached directly or through a UK broker. The convenience and independent trade, which matters most in Birmingham, is supplied by delivered and cash and carry wholesalers and the symbol groups they run (for example Bestway, Booker, Parfetts and Blakemore), who are the real gatekeepers for small shops. Wholesale sector growth in 2025 was led by these same groups, according to trade press. Birmingham and the wider West Midlands are a major wholesale hub serving a large base of independent and ethnic convenience stores, so the channels researched here are in principle the right ones. In this run every sizeable Birmingham wholesaler and distributor the helpers found was already on Diablo's exclusion list (already known to Diablo), so no new companies came out of the city. Online, Amazon and specialist health sites carry sugar free lines; their share for this range was not verified in this run. The location research stopped early because the run's shared search budget was used up, so this section rests on fewer sources than usual.

## Barriers to entry

- Symbol group and wholesaler listings: wholesalers typically ask for promotional support and a place in their promotional guides; the size of these fees was not verified in this run.
- Grocery multiples: the Groceries Supply Code of Practice, policed by the Groceries Code Adjudicator, limits listing payments by the big supermarkets to specific circumstances; buyers still expect promotional funding.
- Dominant players: a few national wholesale groups and their symbol fascias control most of the independent convenience trade, so a listing with one of them is the main door.
- HFSS rules in England restrict where high fat, sugar or salt products can be placed in larger stores; whether each Diablo line is HFSS depends on its nutrient profile score and was not checked here (unverified).
- Sweeteners and polyols: GB retained law on additives permits steviol glycosides and polyols by food category; products with more than 10% added polyols must carry "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects" (retained EU rule, unverified for any change since).
- Claims: "sugars free" and "no added sugars" follow the retained nutrition and health claims rules (0.5 g sugars per 100 g for sugar free).
- Import and labelling: Diablo exports from a UK entity (B Healthy Ltd), so no import duty applies within GB; labels need a UK food business address and English labelling.
