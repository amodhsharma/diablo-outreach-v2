## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly. Coop holds about 43% and Migros 37.4% of food and near-food sales (Nielsen data reported in November 2025), so their central category buyers are the main gatekeepers. Both negotiate through international buying alliances (Coop via Agecore and Vasco, Migros via Epic Partners) and run strong own label ranges. Migros also owns Denner and migrolino; Coop owns Transgourmet, whose Prodega cash and carry and delivery arm dominates foodservice and independent wholesale after absorbing several rivals in 2024. Aldi and Lidl are expanding discounters (shares unverified). Independents and village shops are reached through SPAR Handels AG, Volg and Aligro. Pharmacy and drugstore is a real second channel for a sugar free range: 1,830 pharmacies, about a third in chains (Amavita and Sun Store owned by Galenica, Coop Vitality, BENU, DROPA), supplied mainly by Galenica's Galexis, which also serves drugstores. Online food was only about 3.8% of the market in 2021 and is led by the retailers' own sites (Migros Online, Coop.ch) plus Farmy; quick commerce is marginal. Whether Galaxus, the leading marketplace, lists third party ambient food is unverified. The practical route is a Swiss distributor selling into Coop, Migros, Galexis and independents.

## Barriers to entry

- Duopoly buying power: Coop and Migros together hold roughly 80% of food retail; annual autumn negotiations cover prices, promotion costs and advertising, and failed talks end in delivery stops or delisting.
- Listing and visibility fees: brands pay for listings, shelf stoppers, screens and magazine adverts; national prime location promotions can cost six figures in CHF. Exact listing fee levels are unverified.
- Own label pressure: both majors run deep own label ranges and use parallel imports or substitute products when suppliers resist terms.
- Wholesale lock up: Transgourmet (Coop) dominates delivery wholesale and foodservice; Galenica owns both the leading pharmacy wholesaler (Galexis) and the largest pharmacy chains.
- Distributor margins and credit terms: wholesaler EBITDA is indicatively about 2%, but typical distributor mark ups, exclusivity norms and payment terms in this category were not verified.
- Quick commerce fees: the channel is marginal; no fee data verified.
- Sugar free claim: permitted only at no more than 0.5 g sugars per 100 g or 100 ml (FoodIO Annex 13, 18.1).
- No added sugar claim: no added mono or disaccharides or other foods used for sweetness; if sugars occur naturally, the label must say "contains naturally occurring sugars" (Annex 13, 19.1 and 19.2).
- Laxative warning: foods with more than 10% added polyols must carry "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects" (FoodIO Annex 2, Part B, 2.4).
- Sweeteners: steviol glycosides E 960a to E 960d are permitted since the 2023 additives ordinance revision, in many categories only for energy reduced or no added sugar products. Polyol and stevia limits per product type follow the Additives Ordinance annexes and should be checked line by line (not verified for each SKU).
- Labelling and import: importers carry legal responsibility for compliance through self monitoring; country of production must be shown. The rule that mandatory text must appear in at least one official language (German, French or Italian) is widely cited but was not verified in the source text. EU compliant goods can use the Cassis de Dijon route, but foods not fully meeting Swiss rules need an FSVO general authorisation, and whether this covers UK origin goods is unverified.
- Shelf life at entry: retailer minimum remaining shelf life requirements were not verified.
- Import duty: Switzerland levies duties on processed agricultural products such as chocolate with an industrial element plus a variable element that lifts raw material costs to Swiss price levels. Under the UK Switzerland trade agreement UK exporters pay no more than EU exporters on these goods, subject to rules of origin (EUR.1 or origin declaration). Exact rates per tariff line were not verified.
- Import VAT: applies at the reduced food rate; the current rate should be confirmed with the customs office (BAZG).
