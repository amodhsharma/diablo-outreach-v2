## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in Bloemfontein reach shoppers mainly through the national grocery chains (Shoprite and Checkers, Pick n Pay, Spar and Woolworths), which buy centrally and feed Free State stores from regional distribution centres, most of them in Gauteng. Shoprite's Riverfields distribution centre near Johannesburg, opened in 2025 to serve store growth, shows how far central buying has gone. Spar works through independently owned stores fed by regional distribution centres, so the distribution centre and the larger store owners both decide ranging. Beside the chains, cash and carry wholesalers, buying groups and van sales distributors supply independent grocers, forecourt shops and the township spaza trade in Mangaung; University of the Free State research has looked at how chain entry into Bloemfontein townships hit spaza shops. Bloemfontein is inland with no port, so imported goods arrive through Durban or Cape Town and usually via a Gauteng or coastal importer. For an imported niche range the real gatekeeper is a national importer or distributor that already holds chain listings and handles labelling and logistics. The share of each route in Bloemfontein was not verified.

## Barriers to entry

- Chain power: the Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry found that suppliers face rebates, listing and promotional costs set by the large chains, which weigh most on small suppliers. Exact fee levels were not verified.
- Independent and informal trade is large but fragmented, so reaching it needs a distributor with van sales or cash and carry customers; margin and credit norms for imported confectionery were not verified.
- Sweeteners: permitted sweeteners and their use in foodstuffs are set by the Sweeteners in Foodstuffs regulations (R733 of 2012). Stevia and polyol use for each product type must be checked against that list before shipping.
- Labelling: the draft labelling and advertising regulations (R3337 of 2023) propose front of pack warning labels and tighter rules on nutrient claims, including sugar free and no added sugar. Their final form and start date in late 2026 were not verified.
- Laxative warning: a polyol warning statement is expected under the labelling rules; the exact wording required was not verified.
- Sugar tax: the Health Promotion Levy applies to sugary beverages only, not to confectionery.
- Logistics: Bloemfontein is inland, so landed cost includes port handling in Durban or Cape Town and road freight.
- Duty: the SACUM and UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives preferential tariffs on many UK goods; the exact duty for chocolate and sugar confectionery under it was not verified.
