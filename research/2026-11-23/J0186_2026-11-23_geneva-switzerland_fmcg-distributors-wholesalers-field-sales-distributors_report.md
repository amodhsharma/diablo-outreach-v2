## Market structure

Swiss grocery is highly concentrated: Migros and Coop hold the large majority of food retail and buy centrally, so a listing in Geneva is a national decision taken by head office category buyers, not a city one (exact 2026 shares unverified). Migros Genève is a regional cooperative with its own management, but national ranging still runs through the Migros group (degree of local buying freedom unverified). Discounters Aldi Suisse, Lidl Schweiz and Denner form the second layer, with Manor food halls in the city centre. Geneva is French speaking and loses a notable share of grocery spending to shops across the border in France, which keeps local prices and ranges under pressure. The independent, convenience and kiosk trade is served by a handful of national wholesalers and cash and carry groups such as Aligro, Prodega/Transgourmet and kiosk suppliers, most of which Diablo has already researched. Imported confectionery usually enters through a Swiss importer that holds stock, handles Swiss labelling and sells into retail head offices. Online grocery is led by the retailers' own platforms and Galaxus (third party marketplace room unverified).

## Barriers to entry

- Retail concentration: Migros and Coop control most shelf space, favour own brands and negotiate listing and promotion terms nationally (amounts unverified).
- Saturated partner pool: the Swiss distributors, wholesalers and kiosk suppliers that serve Geneva are already on Diablo's contact list; Lekkerland Schweiz closed in 2020.
- Cross-border price pressure: Geneva shoppers compare against French prices, which limits room for premium pricing (size of effect unverified).
- Distributor terms: importers expect exclusivity for Switzerland and margins that cover labelling, warehousing and listing costs (typical figures unverified).
- Import duty: chocolate and biscuits carry an agricultural component; the UK Switzerland trade agreement gives preference for originating goods, but check each tariff line.
- Labelling: Swiss rules closely mirror EU Regulation 1169/2011; at least one national language is needed (French expected in Geneva) and more than 10% added polyols requires the laxative effect warning.
- Claims and sweeteners: "sugar free" and "no added sugars" follow rules equivalent to the EU claims annex; permitted sweeteners and polyols follow the Swiss additives ordinance, largely aligned with EU lists (check product by product).
- Shelf life: importers and retailers expect substantial remaining shelf life on arrival (exact norms unverified).
