## Market structure

Lucerne is served by the same national route to market as the rest of German speaking Switzerland; we found no evidence of a distinct Lucerne channel. Grocery is a duopoly: Nielsen data reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% of food and near food and Migros at 37.4%, so the two cooperatives decide most shelf space. Denner (Migros owned), Lidl, Aldi and Volg (fenaco) make up most of the rest, and Lidl plans further store openings. Private label is reported at about half of grocery sales, which narrows room for foreign brands in the big chains. Most Swiss wholesalers act as importers, holding stock, financing and handling quality control, and often hold exclusive regional or national rights to the brands they import. The real gatekeepers are therefore the central buying teams at Coop and Migros and the importer that holds the brand. Below them sit cash and carry and foodservice wholesale, convenience (Migrolino, Coop Pronto, kiosks), drugstores and pharmacies, health food shops and online stores such as Galaxus. Exact channel shares outside the two leaders are unverified.

## Barriers to entry

- Exclusivity: importers commonly expect exclusive regional or national rights; plan on one national partner rather than several regional ones.
- Retailer concentration: Coop and Migros together hold roughly 70% to 80% of food sales and lean heavily on private label. Listing fees and promotional terms are not published; treat them as unverified and expect negotiation.
- Distributor margin and credit norms: we found no reliable published figures for Swiss importer margins or payment terms (unverified).
- Duty: imports from the EU and UK of processed agricultural goods such as chocolate and biscuits pay a variable agricultural component that lifts raw material cost (sugar, milk, flour) towards Swiss prices. Sugar free recipes may attract a lower component, but this is unverified and needs a check in the Tares tariff by HS code.
- UK trade: the Switzerland UK agreement rolls over the Swiss EU market access terms, including preferences for processed agricultural products. An enhanced UK Switzerland deal was concluded in July 2026 but is not expected in force until 2027. Rules of origin apply: B Healthy Ltd needs UK origin proof, and EU inputs only count under the PEM rules.
- Cassis de Dijon: EU legal foods that do not fully meet Swiss rules can need an FSVO general ruling before sale. Whether this route covers goods shipped from the UK is unverified.
- Sweeteners: steviol glycosides (E 960) are permitted under the Swiss additives ordinance (ZuV) since the 2023 revision, for listed food categories. Polyol limits by product type and the laxative warning for products over 10% polyols are believed to mirror EU rules, but we did not confirm the Swiss text.
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar claims are believed to follow the EU conditions (0.5 g sugars per 100 g for sugar free); unverified against the Swiss ordinance.
- Labelling: labels must carry the mandatory details in at least one official language (German for Lucerne). One source says English alone may be accepted; sources conflict, so plan German labels. Country of production must be shown.
- Shelf life at entry: no published norm found; unverified.
