## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: Woolworths and Coles hold most supermarket sales, a position examined by the ACCC supermarkets inquiry, with Aldi third and Metcash supplying independent IGA and Friendly Grocer stores. The Gold Coast follows this national pattern; local shares were not verified in this run.

The channels in this job sit mostly outside the big chains. Sports nutrition shops buy from national supplement distributors. Office pantry suppliers and vending operators buy from wholesalers and cash and carry, and Gold Coast vending is spread across small local operators. Hamper and corporate gift companies buy large volumes ahead of Christmas. Many national wholesalers serve the Gold Coast from Brisbane or Sydney (unverified for each one). In each case the gatekeeper for an overseas brand is the Australian importer who holds stock and carries compliant labelling.

## Barriers to entry

- Importer as gatekeeper: most buyers in these channels will not import direct, so an Australian importer or wholesaler is usually needed; margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Dominant players: Woolworths and Coles control much of mainstream grocery; Metcash independents are the main alternative route to supermarket shelf.
- Seasonality: hamper and gifting buyers commit well ahead of Christmas; exact buying windows are unverified.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted sweeteners are set by the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code; each Diablo sweetener should be checked for its product type before export (not checked line by line here).
- Laxative warning: Standard 1.2.3 requires an advisory statement on foods containing polyols above set levels.
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar are regulated nutrition content claims; FSANZ approved new no added sugar conditions in 2023 (P1062) with a transition period. Whether every Diablo product meets them is unverified.
- Labelling: Australian format nutrition panel, allergen and country of origin labelling are needed, usually applied by the importer.
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed most tariffs on UK goods; the duty line for each product is unverified.
