## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: Woolworths and Coles hold most supermarket sales, a position examined by the ACCC supermarkets inquiry, with Aldi third and Metcash supplying independent IGA stores. Seasonal and gift shops sit outside that system. In Perth they are mostly small independents: gift and homewares boutiques, lolly and British sweet shops, gourmet food stores, Christmas and seasonal pop ups, and hamper makers that also sell from a shop or online. They buy from Australian importers, gift wholesalers and agents rather than direct from overseas brands, and many of those suppliers are based in the eastern states. Much of this buying happens at trade shows such as Reed Gift Fairs in Sydney and Melbourne, where wholesalers show seasonal ranges to independent retailers well ahead of Christmas and Easter. Perth's distance from the eastern states adds freight time and cost, so a supplier holding stock in Western Australia, or a wholesaler with reliable WA delivery, matters to these shops. The gatekeeper is the Australian importer or gift wholesaler who holds the brand, carries compliant labelling and attends the gift fairs. Order sizes per shop are small and how many Perth gift shops carry sugar free lines is unverified.

## Barriers to entry

- Importer as gatekeeper: small gift shops will not import direct, so an Australian importer or gift wholesaler is needed; margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Seasonality: gift buyers commit to Christmas and Easter ranges months ahead, often at trade fairs; exact buying windows are unverified.
- Distance: many national gift wholesalers serve Perth from the eastern states, adding freight time and cost.
- Dominant players: no single chain locks up the gift channel in Perth; it is fragmented across independents (share data unverified).
- Sweeteners and polyols: steviol glycosides are permitted under the Food Standards Code after FSANZ application A1037 and later amendments; each Diablo sweetener and polyol should be checked against its product type before export (not checked line by line here).
- Laxative warning: Standard 1.2.3 requires an advisory statement on foods containing polyols above set levels, to the effect that excess consumption may have a laxative effect.
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar are regulated nutrition content claims; FSANZ approved new no added sugar claim conditions in 2023 (P1062) with a transition period. Whether every Diablo product meets them is unverified.
- Labelling: Australian format nutrition panel, allergen and country of origin labelling are needed, usually applied by the importer.
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed most tariffs on UK goods; the duty line for each product is unverified.
- Shelf life: seasonal stock that does not sell is often held to the next season, so long remaining shelf life on arrival matters; no fixed rule was verified.
