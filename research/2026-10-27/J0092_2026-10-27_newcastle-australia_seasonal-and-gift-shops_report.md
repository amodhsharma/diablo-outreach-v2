## Market structure

Australian grocery is concentrated: Woolworths and Coles hold most supermarket sales, Aldi is third and Metcash supplies independent IGA and Friendly Grocer stores. Newcastle and the Hunter follow this national pattern; local shares were not verified in this run.

Seasonal and gift shops sit outside the big chains. They are mostly owner-run: lolly and pick and mix shops, tourist and heritage gift shops (for example in Morpeth and the Hunter Valley wine country), hamper sellers and online gift shops. They buy branded confectionery from Australian importers and gift wholesalers, often met at the Reed Gift Fairs in Sydney and Melbourne, rather than importing themselves. Buying peaks before Christmas, Easter and Valentine's Day, so orders for seasonal gift boxes and advent calendars are placed months ahead. Many national gift and confectionery wholesalers are likely to serve Newcastle from Sydney (unverified for each one). The gatekeeper for an overseas brand is the Australian importer who holds stock and carries compliant labelling, and most of the larger hamper and lolly players serving Newcastle were already covered by earlier jobs.

## Barriers to entry

- Trade agreement: the Australia UK Free Trade Agreement came into force on 31 May 2023 and covers the elimination of tariffs on UK goods, with confectionery and biscuits named among imports set to get cheaper. The exact pre-FTA rate for this range was not verified; goods must meet UK rules of origin to qualify.
- Sweeteners: steviol glycosides are permitted under the Food Standards Code (FSANZ has approved further steviol glycoside production methods); the exact permission for each product type was not checked here and should be confirmed per SKU.
- Polyols: foods with added polyols above a set level must carry a laxative effect advisory statement under Standard 1.2.3; the threshold was not verified in this run.
- No added sugar claims: Amendment No. 224 (13 December 2023) tightened them: no claim if the food contains an added sugar (such as honey) or more than 10 g sugars per 100 g. A 48 month transition runs from December 2023. Sugar free claim conditions were not checked here.
- Country of origin: confectionery, biscuits and snack food are non-priority foods, so imports need only a text origin statement (for example "Made in the UK"), not the kangaroo mark.
- Labels must be in English with an Australian importer or supplier name and address (standard Code requirement; not re-verified in this run).
- Channel norms: gift shops order small quantities at gift fairs and expect wholesalers to hold stock; seasonal lines need lead times of several months. Listing fees, margins and credit terms for this channel were not verified.
