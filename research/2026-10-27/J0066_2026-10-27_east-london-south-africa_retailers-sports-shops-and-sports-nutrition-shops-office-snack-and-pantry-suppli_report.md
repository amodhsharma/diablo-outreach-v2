## Market structure

East London (Buffalo City) is a secondary market fed by national chains through regional distribution, so listings are won at head office or regional buying level, not in store. Shoprite Checkers is taking share, Pick n Pay owns roughly two thirds of discounter Boxer (in East London since 1995) and Spar acts as a wholesaler to owner run stores, which gives individual Spar owners more freedom to range niche lines. Chocolate is concentrated: Mondelez, Nestlé and Lindt hold about 53% of retail value, Tiger Brands leads sugar confectionery and the top three hold nearly three quarters of the market. Below the chains, independent wholesalers, cash and carry outlets and spaza shops move impulse lines. The better for you shopper is reached through pharmacy chains Dis-Chem and Clicks and through health specialists Wellness Warehouse and Faithful to Nature, the latter mainly online for East London (local store presence unverified). Online gatekeepers are Checkers Sixty60 (sales up 58% in its last year), Pick n Pay asap! and Takealot with Mr D. Sports nutrition, office pantry and gifting channels in East London look fragmented; no dominant local player was found (unverified).

## Barriers to entry

- **Rebates:** the Competition Commission's grocery inquiry found the dominant grocery model is built on supplier rebates for merchandising, store openings, shelf space and category management; budget for these to list nationally. Specific listing fee levels not verified.
- **Exclusivity:** long term exclusive mall leases have kept specialist stores out; the inquiry recommended phasing them out. Distributor exclusivity norms not verified.
- **Marketplace and quick commerce fees:** Takealot charges success and fulfilment fees (fulfilment fees adjusted from 1 April 2026); rates for this range not verified.
- **Distributor margin and credit terms:** no reliable figures found; treat as unverified and test with partners.
- **Dominant players:** Mondelez, Nestlé and Tiger Brands hold nearly three quarters of confectionery, with strong control of shelf space and impulse fixtures.
- **Sweeteners (R.733 of 2012):** only sweeteners listed in the Codex GSFA are permitted, at GSFA maximum levels per food category (check each category for stevia and polyols); non nutritive sweeteners are banned in foods for infants and young children.
- **Sweetener labelling:** steviol glycosides must be declared as "Steviol Glycosides" or "Steviol Extract" and non nutritive sweeteners identified as such in the ingredient list.
- **Laxative warning:** required where polyols exceed 50 g/kg of the final product (250 g/kg for sugar free gum). Exact wording not verified.
- **Claims (R.146):** "sugar free" allows no more than 0.5 g mono and disaccharides per 100 g; "no added sugar" is barred if the food contains added sugars; "reduced" claims need at least a 25% difference. Treatment of polyols under these claims not verified.
- **Draft R.3337:** still a draft as of July 2026; it would add front of pack warnings, including on certain products with artificial sweeteners, and restrict claims on warned products. A packaging risk to watch.
- **Import labelling:** packs must meet South African R.146 rules above; whether UK packs can be over stickered was not verified.
- **Shelf life at entry:** retailer minimum remaining life norms not verified.
- **Import duty:** chocolate can reportedly face up to 37%, with some European chocolate entering duty free (2022 opinion piece, unverified); check exact rates per tariff line with SARS.
- **UK trade agreement:** the SACUM UK EPA keeps bilateral preferential rates but excludes domestically sensitive products; goods need UK origin (EUR1 or origin declaration), and EU made goods simply packed or labelled in the UK do not qualify.
