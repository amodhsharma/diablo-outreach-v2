## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated. Woolworths and Coles together are reported to hold between 70 and 90 per cent of grocery in some areas (Metcash figure, 2024), with Aldi third and Metcash supplying independent IGA and Foodworks stores as wholesaler. Big chain shelf space is reached through national range reviews, usually via an Australian importer or broker rather than direct from an overseas brand.

The channels in this job sit mostly outside that system. Health food and independent grocers in Melbourne buy from specialist importers and health food wholesalers. Sports nutrition is led by chains such as Chemist Warehouse and Nutrition Warehouse, which range protein snacks alongside supplements, plus independent supplement stores. Office pantry suppliers and vending operators buy from wholesalers and cash and carry, and vending is fragmented across many local operators. Corporate gifting and hamper companies buy large seasonal volumes for Christmas. In each case the gatekeeper is the local importer who holds the brand and carries compliance labelling. Searches for this section were cut short by the run's search limit, so channel detail is lighter than usual.

## Barriers to entry

- Dominant players: Woolworths and Coles control most grocery shelf space; Chemist Warehouse is a powerful gatekeeper in sports nutrition. Listing fees and promotional terms there are not verified.
- Importer margins, credit terms and exclusivity norms are not verified; expect a local importer to ask for Australian exclusivity.
- Sweeteners: steviol glycosides, inulin and polyols are permitted under the Food Standards Code subject to each food category's additive permissions (check per SKU, not verified here).
- Laxative advisory statement: required where lactitol, maltitol, maltitol syrup, mannitol or xylitol reach 10 g/100 g or more, or erythritol, isomalt, polydextrose or sorbitol reach 25 g/100 g or more (Standard 1.2.3).
- No added sugar claims: tightened under FSANZ proposal P1062; not allowed if the food contains added sugars (including honey, malt, maltodextrin or concentrated fruit juice) or more than 10 g/100 g sugars in solid food. Transition end date unverified.
- Labelling: English label meeting the Food Standards Code, Australian importer name and address and country of origin statement; the Imported Food Inspection Scheme can hold and test consignments.
- Shelf life at entry: no legal minimum found; importer expectations unverified.
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed tariffs on almost all UK goods, so UK origin goods should enter duty free if they meet the rules of origin. GST of 10 per cent applies.
