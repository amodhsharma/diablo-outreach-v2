## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: Woolworths and Coles hold most supermarket sales, a position examined by the ACCC supermarkets inquiry, with Aldi third and Metcash supplying independent IGA stores. Aldi entered Western Australia only in 2016, so in Perth independent IGA stores kept a larger role than in some eastern cities for longer; their current share is not verified. Big chain shelf space is reached through national range reviews, usually via an Australian importer or broker rather than direct from an overseas brand.

The channels in this job sit mostly outside that system. Perth health food shops, bulk food stores and independent grocers buy from specialist importers and health food wholesalers, many based in the eastern states. Sports nutrition is led by national chains and independent supplement stores. Office pantry suppliers and vending operators buy from wholesalers and cash and carry, and Perth vending is spread across many small local operators. Hamper and corporate gifting companies buy large seasonal volumes for Christmas. Perth's distance from the eastern states adds freight time and cost, so a partner holding stock in Western Australia matters. In each case the gatekeeper is the local importer who holds the brand and carries compliant labelling.

## Barriers to entry

- Importer as gatekeeper: most buyers in these channels will not import direct, so an Australian importer or wholesaler is usually needed; margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Distance: many national wholesalers serve Perth from the eastern states, adding freight time and cost; a Perth or WA stockist shortens lead times.
- Dominant players: Woolworths and Coles control mainstream grocery; sports nutrition and vending have national leaders but remain partly fragmented.
- Seasonality: hamper and gifting buyers commit well ahead of Christmas; exact buying windows are unverified.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted sweeteners are set by the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code; each Diablo sweetener should be checked for its product type before export (not checked line by line here).
- Laxative warning: Standard 1.2.3 requires an advisory statement on foods containing polyols above set levels, to the effect that excess consumption may have a laxative effect.
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar are regulated nutrition content claims; FSANZ approved new no added sugar claim conditions in 2023 (P1062) with a transition period. Whether every Diablo product meets them is unverified.
- Labelling: Australian format nutrition panel, allergen and country of origin labelling are needed, usually applied by the importer.
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed most tariffs on UK goods; the duty line for each product is unverified.
- Shelf life: importers usually want long remaining shelf life on arrival; no fixed rule was verified.
