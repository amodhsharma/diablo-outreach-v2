## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: Woolworths and Coles are reported to hold a very large share of supermarket sales, with Aldi third and Metcash supplying independent IGA stores as wholesaler. Big chain shelf space is reached through national range reviews, usually via an Australian importer or broker rather than direct from an overseas brand.

Seasonal and gift shops in Melbourne sit outside that system. The channel is mostly independent gift and homewares shops, lolly and confectionery shops, and a large online gift hamper sector (consumer and corporate) that buys heavily for Christmas and Easter. Hamper businesses source branded chocolate, biscuits and sweets from local importers and gourmet food wholesalers, and some also sell to the trade. Bricks and mortar gift shops typically buy at trade fairs and from wholesale reps. The real gatekeeper is the Australian importer or wholesaler who holds the brand, carries compliant labelling and supplies seasonal stock on time. How much of this channel buys direct from overseas brands is unverified.

## Closing analysis

- **I Love This Shop** first: a Victorian chain of party and lolly shops whose online shop lists over 900 branded sweets and chocolates, the largest branded confectionery shelf found in this Channel, so Diablo's sweets and chocolate fit an existing shelf.
- **Gottis** second: it already imports European branded sweets (BUBS, Ahlgrens) into Melbourne and supplies pick and mix stands to other shops, so it knows how to bring in a European confectionery brand and pass it on.
- **Spotlight** third: Melbourne headquartered with 146 stores listed and a Christmas and party confectionery range from other brands, which gives national seasonal reach if a listing is won.

## Barriers to entry

- Seasonality: gift and hamper buyers commit months ahead of Christmas and Easter, so a brand missing the buying window waits a year. Exact buying calendars are unverified.
- Importer as gatekeeper: most small shops will not import direct, so an Australian importer or wholesaler is usually needed; their margin and credit terms were not verified in this run.
- Dominant players: Woolworths and Coles control mainstream grocery, but they matter little for this channel.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted additives and sweeteners are set by the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code; each Diablo sweetener should be checked against the Code for its product type before export (not checked line by line here).
- Laxative warning: Standard 1.2.3 requires an advisory statement on foods containing polyols above set levels, to the effect that excess consumption may have a laxative effect.
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar are regulated nutrition content claims. FSANZ approved changes to the no added sugar claim conditions in 2023 (P1062) with a transition period; whether every Diablo product meets the new conditions is unverified.
- Labelling: products need Australian compliant labels (nutrition information panel in Australian format, allergen labelling and country of origin rules), usually applied by the importer.
- Duty and trade agreement: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed most tariffs on UK goods; the exact duty line for each Diablo product is unverified.
- Shelf life: importers usually want long remaining shelf life on arrival given shipping time; no fixed rule was verified.
