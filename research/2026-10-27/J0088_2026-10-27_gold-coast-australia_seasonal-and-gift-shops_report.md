## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: Woolworths and Coles hold most supermarket sales, a position examined by the ACCC supermarkets inquiry, with Aldi third and Metcash supplying independent stores. The Gold Coast follows the same pattern as the rest of Queensland.

Seasonal and gift shops sit outside that system. Independent gift shops, lolly shops, hamper makers and seasonal pop up retailers mostly buy from Australian importers, wholesalers and brand agents, many of whom show their ranges at the Reed Gift Fairs in Melbourne and Sydney. Most of these suppliers serve the Gold Coast from Brisbane or the southern states. The Gold Coast is a major tourist city that reported record visitor spending in 2024, so souvenir, sweet and gift shops in tourist precincts and shopping centres matter more here than in most cities; the share of confectionery sold through them is not verified. Hamper and corporate gift companies buy ahead of Christmas, and Easter is the other big confectionery peak. The gatekeeper for an overseas brand is the Australian importer or gift wholesaler who holds stock and carries compliant labelling; few Gold Coast gift shops are likely to import direct (not verified shop by shop).

## Barriers to entry

- Importer as gatekeeper: gift and lolly shops rarely import direct, so an Australian importer, gift wholesaler or brand agent is usually needed; margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Trade fair calendar: gift buyers source at the Reed Gift Fairs in Melbourne and Sydney, so a brand without a local agent showing there is harder to find.
- Seasonality: Christmas, Easter and the tourist peaks drive gift buying, with orders placed well ahead; exact buying windows are unverified.
- Many of the Gold Coast's lolly and hamper shops were already researched in earlier jobs, so few new names remain in this channel.
- Dominant players: Woolworths and Coles control mainstream grocery, but this channel is fragmented among independents.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted sweeteners are set by the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code; each Diablo sweetener should be checked for its product type before export (not checked line by line here).
- Laxative warning: Standard 1.2.3 requires an advisory statement on foods containing polyols above set levels.
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar are regulated nutrition content claims; FSANZ approved new no added sugar conditions in 2023 (P1062) with a transition period. Whether every Diablo product meets them is unverified.
- Labelling: Australian format nutrition panel, allergen and country of origin labelling are needed, usually applied by the importer.
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed most tariffs on UK goods; the duty line for each product is unverified.
