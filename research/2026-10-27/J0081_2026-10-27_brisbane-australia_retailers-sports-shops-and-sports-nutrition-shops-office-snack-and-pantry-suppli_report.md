## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: Woolworths and Coles hold most supermarket sales, a position examined by the ACCC supermarkets inquiry (final report 2025), with Aldi third and Metcash supplying independent IGA stores as wholesaler. Big chain shelf space is reached through national range reviews, usually via an Australian importer or broker rather than direct from an overseas brand.

The channels in this job sit mostly outside that system. Brisbane health food shops and independent grocers buy from specialist importers and health food wholesalers. Sports nutrition is led by national chains and independent supplement stores that range protein snacks beside supplements. Office pantry suppliers and vending operators buy from wholesalers and cash and carry; vending in Queensland is fragmented across many small owner operated businesses, judging by the number of route businesses offered for sale. Corporate gifting and hamper companies buy large seasonal volumes for Christmas. In each case the gatekeeper is the local importer who holds the brand and carries compliant labelling. Searches for this section were cut short by the run's search limit, so channel detail is lighter than usual.

## Barriers to entry

- Importer as gatekeeper: most buyers in these channels will not import direct, so an Australian importer or wholesaler is usually needed; margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Dominant players: Woolworths and Coles control mainstream grocery; sports nutrition and vending have national leaders but remain partly fragmented.
- Seasonality: hamper and gifting buyers commit well ahead of Christmas; exact buying windows are unverified.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted sweeteners and additives are set by the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code; each Diablo sweetener should be checked for its product type before export (not checked line by line here).
- Laxative warning: Standard 1.2.3 requires an advisory statement on foods containing polyols above set levels, to the effect that excess consumption may have a laxative effect.
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar are regulated nutrition content claims; FSANZ approved new no added sugar claim conditions in 2023 (P1062) with a transition period. Whether every Diablo product meets them is unverified.
- Labelling: Australian format nutrition panel, allergen and country of origin labelling are needed, usually applied by the importer.
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed most tariffs on UK goods; the duty line for each product is unverified.
- Shelf life: importers usually want long remaining shelf life on arrival; no fixed rule was verified.
