## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated. Woolworths and Coles together are reported to hold between 70 and 90 per cent of grocery in some areas (Metcash figure, 2024), with Aldi the third chain and Metcash supplying independent IGA and Foodworks stores as wholesaler. Shelf space in the big chains is reached through national category reviews, usually via an Australian importer or broker rather than direct from an overseas brand.

Seasonal and gift shops in Sydney sit outside that system. Independent gift shops, hamper makers, lolly and sweet shops, museum and tourist shops and garden centre gift sections buy from trade only importers and gift wholesalers, often at trade fairs (Reed Gift Fairs in Sydney and Melbourne is the main one, not verified for 2026 dates) and through wholesaler online catalogues. The gatekeepers here are the specialist confectionery and gift importers, who hold the brands and handle compliance labelling, and the hamper companies, who buy large seasonal volumes for Christmas, Easter and Mother's Day. Online, Amazon AU and the chains' own marketplaces matter for health snacks, but gift buyers mostly order from importer catalogues.

## Barriers to entry

- Dominant players: Woolworths and Coles control most grocery shelf space and run tough range reviews; the independent and gift channel is open but fragmented, so a local importer is close to essential.
- Importer and wholesaler margins, credit terms and any listing fees in the gift channel are not verified; expect the importer to want brand exclusivity for Australia.
- Seasonality: gift and hamper buyers commit months ahead (Christmas buying is largely done by mid year), so timing of approach matters.
- Sweeteners: steviol glycosides, inulin and the polyols are permitted under the Food Standards Code, subject to the additive permissions for each food category (check per SKU, not verified here).
- Laxative advisory statement: required where lactitol, maltitol, maltitol syrup, mannitol or xylitol reach 10 g/100 g or more, or erythritol, isomalt, polydextrose or sorbitol reach 25 g/100 g or more (Standard 1.2.3).
- No added sugar claims: tightened under FSANZ proposal P1062; a food cannot carry the claim if it contains added sugars (including honey, malt, maltodextrin or concentrated fruit juice) or more than 10 g/100 g sugars in solid food. The transition end date is unverified, so check current labels against the new conditions.
- Labelling: imported food must carry an English label meeting the Food Standards Code, with an Australian importer name and address and a country of origin statement; the Imported Food Inspection Scheme can hold and test consignments at the border.
- Shelf life at entry: no legal minimum found; importers usually ask for a long remaining shelf life (unverified figure).
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed tariffs on almost all UK goods, so goods of UK origin from B Healthy Ltd should enter duty free if they meet the rules of origin. GST of 10 per cent still applies.
