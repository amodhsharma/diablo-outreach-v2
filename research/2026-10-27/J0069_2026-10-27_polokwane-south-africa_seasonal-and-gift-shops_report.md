## Market structure

Packaged confectionery in South Africa reaches shelf mainly through a few national grocery groups (Shoprite Checkers, Pick n Pay, Spar, Woolworths and Boxer), which buy centrally into their own distribution centres and hold the real gatekeeping power through central buying, rebates and shelf space. Spar works through regional distribution centres serving independently owned stores. Below the chains sits a large independent and informal trade served by wholesalers, cash and carry outlets and buying groups, which the Competition Commission found receive weaker terms than the national chains. Polokwane is a regional hub for Limpopo, but most branded imports reach it from national importers in Gauteng rather than local importers. Seasonal and gift buying in Polokwane runs largely through the grocery chains' festive ranges (for example Woolworths' Christmas range) and through national online florists and hamper companies that deliver there, while local gift and hamper makers mostly trade on social media without websites. This gift picture comes from this run's channel searches and is not verified by market data. Specialist sugar-free ranges sit mainly in Dis-Chem, Clicks, Woolworths and health shops.

## Barriers to entry

- Retailer terms: national chains demand rebates for merchandising, store openings, shelf access and category management, and terms are rigid; exact listing fee levels were not verified.
- Exclusive mall leases have historically protected the big grocers; the Competition Commission recommended phasing them out.
- Distributor margins and credit terms for imported confectionery were not verified in this run; expect a national importer to be needed for chain listings.
- Sweeteners: only sweeteners in the Codex GSFA may be sold (R.733 of 2012); stevia must be labelled "Steviol Glycosides" or "Steviol Extract".
- Polyols above 50 g/kg need the warning "excessive consumption may have a laxative effect".
- Claims (R.146): "free" of sugar means 0.5 g or less mono and disaccharides per 100 g; "no added sugar" is barred where the food contains added sugars as defined. UK or EU wording may need relabelling.
- Draft R.3337 would add front-of-pack warnings for added sugar and certain artificial sweeteners and limit claims; still a draft as of July 2026, timing unclear.
- Trade: South Africa is excluded from immediate duty-free access under the SACUM UK EPA and liberalises gradually, with sensitive lines excluded; the confectionery duty rate was not verified. UK origin (EUR1 or origin declaration) is needed for any preference.
- Shelf life at entry and import permit requirements were not verified.
