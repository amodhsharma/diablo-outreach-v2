## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: Woolworths and Coles hold most supermarket sales, a position examined by the ACCC supermarkets inquiry, with Aldi third and Metcash supplying independent IGA and Friendly Grocer stores. Canberra follows this national pattern; local shares were not verified.

Seasonal and gift shops sit outside that system. Independent gift shops, lolly shops, hamper makers and seasonal retailers mostly buy from Australian importers, gift wholesalers and brand agents, many of whom show their ranges at the Reed Gift Fairs in Melbourne and Sydney and serve Canberra from Sydney or Melbourne (not verified supplier by supplier). Gift retail clusters in shopping centres such as the Canberra Centre (about 400 stores and services, with David Jones and Myer) and in tourist precincts. The ACT recorded 4.5 million visitors and $3.1 billion of visitor spend in the year to September 2025, so tourist and souvenir trade matters, though its share of confectionery is not verified. Hamper and corporate gift buyers order ahead of Christmas, and Easter is the other peak. The gatekeeper for an overseas brand is the Australian importer or gift wholesaler who holds stock and carries compliant labelling.

## Closing analysis

- Gourmet by Design first: Canberra's main food gifting retailer, with 131 items in its chocolate and confectionery collection and a full Christmas range of other companies' biscuits, sweets and chocolate, but no sugar-free option in its hampers.
- National Museum of Australia Museum Shop second: its chocolate, sweets and spreads sections already stock other brands, including foreign gourmet sweets, so Diablo's chocolate and spreads fit the shelf.
- GG's Flowers third: its hampers buy in branded chocolate, wafers and cookies from other companies and it sells a Christmas hamper, a natural place for Diablo's gift boxes and wafers.

## Barriers to entry

- Importer as gatekeeper: gift and lolly shops rarely import direct, so an Australian importer, gift wholesaler or brand agent is usually needed; margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Trade fair calendar: gift buyers source at the Reed Gift Fairs in Melbourne and Sydney, so a brand without a local agent showing there is harder to find.
- Seasonality: Christmas, Easter and school holiday tourist peaks drive gift buying, with orders placed well ahead; exact buying windows are unverified.
- Small local channel: many national hamper and lolly sellers that deliver to Canberra were already researched in earlier jobs, so few new names remain.
- Dominant players: Woolworths and Coles control mainstream grocery, but this channel is fragmented among independents.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted sweeteners are set by the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code; each Diablo sweetener should be checked for its product type before export (not checked line by line here).
- Laxative warning: Standard 1.2.3 requires an advisory statement on foods containing polyols above set levels.
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar are regulated nutrition content claims; FSANZ approved new no added sugar conditions in 2023 (P1062) with a transition period. Whether every Diablo product meets them is unverified.
- Labelling: Australian format nutrition panel, allergen and country of origin labelling are needed, usually applied by the importer.
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed most tariffs on UK goods; the duty line for each product is unverified.
