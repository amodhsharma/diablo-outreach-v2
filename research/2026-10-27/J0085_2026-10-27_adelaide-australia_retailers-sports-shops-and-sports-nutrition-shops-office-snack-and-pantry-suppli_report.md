## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: Woolworths and Coles hold most supermarket sales, a position examined by the ACCC supermarkets inquiry, with Aldi third and Metcash supplying independent stores. South Australia differs from the other states because independent supermarkets are unusually strong there: Foodland, a banner of family-owned SA stores, and the SA-founded Drakes chain both trade widely across Adelaide. Their exact share of SA grocery is not verified in this run. Big chain shelf space is reached through range reviews, usually via an Australian importer or broker rather than direct from an overseas brand, while the independents give a local buyer more room to list niche lines.

The other channels in this job sit mostly outside that system. Sports nutrition shops buy from national supplement distributors. Office pantry suppliers and vending operators buy from wholesalers and cash and carry, and Adelaide vending is spread across small local operators. Hamper and corporate gifting companies buy large seasonal volumes for Christmas. Many national wholesalers serve Adelaide from Melbourne or Sydney. In each case the gatekeeper is the Australian importer who holds the brand and carries compliant labelling.

## Barriers to entry

- Importer as gatekeeper: most buyers in these channels will not import direct, so an Australian importer or wholesaler is usually needed; margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Dominant players: Woolworths and Coles control much of mainstream grocery, but in SA Foodland and Drakes independents are a real alternative route to shelf.
- Distance: many national wholesalers serve Adelaide from the eastern states, adding freight time.
- Seasonality: hamper and gifting buyers commit well ahead of Christmas; exact buying windows are unverified.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted sweeteners are set by the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code; each Diablo sweetener should be checked for its product type before export (not checked line by line here).
- Laxative warning: Standard 1.2.3 requires an advisory statement on foods containing polyols above set levels, to the effect that excess consumption may have a laxative effect.
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar are regulated nutrition content claims; FSANZ approved new no added sugar claim conditions in 2023 (P1062) with a transition period. Whether every Diablo product meets them is unverified.
- Labelling: Australian format nutrition panel, allergen and country of origin labelling are needed, usually applied by the importer.
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed most tariffs on UK goods; the duty line for each product is unverified.
