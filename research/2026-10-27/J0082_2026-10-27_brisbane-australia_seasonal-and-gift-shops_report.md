## Market structure

Australian grocery is highly concentrated: Woolworths and Coles hold most supermarket sales, a position examined by the ACCC supermarkets inquiry, with Aldi third and Metcash supplying independent IGA stores. Seasonal chocolate is a large event category in that system; Finder estimated Australians would spend about $4.8 billion on Easter in 2025, though that figure covers all Easter spending, not chocolate alone.

Seasonal and gift shops in Brisbane sit mostly outside the supermarket system. Lolly shops, gift and homewares stores, hamper makers and duty free or souvenir shops buy from Australian importers, confectionery wholesalers and gift distributors, often meeting suppliers at the Reed Gift Fairs held in Sydney and Melbourne. Most are small owner run businesses with one or a few shops plus an online store, and many hamper makers buy heavily for Christmas. The real gatekeeper is the local importer or wholesaler that holds the brand, carries compliant Australian labelling and supplies small minimum orders. The share of gift shop sales that is confectionery is not verified.

## Barriers to entry

- Importer as gatekeeper: gift and lolly shops rarely import direct, so an Australian importer or gift wholesaler is usually needed; margin and credit norms were not verified in this run.
- Seasonality: hamper and gift buyers commit to Christmas and Easter ranges months ahead; exact buying windows are unverified.
- Dominant players: Woolworths and Coles dominate seasonal chocolate in mainstream grocery; specialist gift retail is fragmented.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted sweeteners are set by the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code; each Diablo sweetener should be checked for its product type before export (not checked line by line here).
- Laxative warning: Standard 1.2.3 requires an advisory statement on foods containing polyols above set levels, to the effect that excess consumption may have a laxative effect.
- Claims: sugar free and no added sugar are regulated nutrition content claims; FSANZ approved new no added sugar claim conditions in 2023 (P1062) with a transition period. Whether every Diablo product meets them is unverified.
- Labelling: Australian format nutrition panel, allergen and country of origin labelling are needed, usually applied by the importer.
- Duty: the Australia United Kingdom Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 31 May 2023 and removed most tariffs on UK goods; the duty line for each product is unverified.
- Shelf life: gift buyers holding stock for a season usually want long remaining shelf life on arrival; no fixed rule was verified.
