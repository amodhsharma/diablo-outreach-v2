## Market structure

Packaged confectionery reaches South African shoppers through two routes. Modern trade is concentrated in a few national grocery groups: Shoprite Checkers (including Usave and Sixty60 delivery), Pick n Pay (with Boxer in townships), SPAR (a voluntary trading group supplied from its own distribution centres) and Woolworths, with Shoprite widening its lead in 2026. These chains buy centrally into their own distribution centres, so a national listing goes through head office buyers, often via an appointed local distributor or importer that handles merchandising. Traditional trade (spaza shops, independents, forecourts and tuck shops) is a large share of volume and is served by wholesalers and cash and carry groups, buying groups and van or field sales distributors. Pharmacy chains (Clicks, Dis-Chem) and their wholesale arms are a separate gate for health positioned snacks. Online, Checkers Sixty60, Pick n Pay asap! and Takealot matter, but they buy through the same suppliers. In practice the gatekeepers are the big retailers' central buyers and the national FMCG distributors that already hold their listings. Exact market shares were not verified for this report.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentrated buying: a handful of chains control most modern trade; listings usually come with listing fees, rebates and promotional support. Exact fee levels were not verified.
- The Competition Commission's Grocery Retail Market Inquiry (2019) found long term exclusive mall leases held by big chains and recommended ending them; it also looked at supplier rebates and fees.
- Distributors usually expect exclusivity for the country or a region and set margin and credit terms; typical figures were not verified for this report.
- Sweeteners: permitted sweeteners and their levels are set by the Sweeteners in Foodstuffs regulations (R733 of 2012). Whether every polyol in the range is permitted for each product type needs checking against that schedule.
- Labelling and claims: R146 (2010) governs labelling and claims today; the draft R3337 (2023) would add front of pack warning labels and tighten claims, but its final form and timing were not confirmed. "Sugar free" and "no added sugar" claim conditions and any laxative statement for polyols must be checked against the regulations in force. Treat the detail as unverified.
- Labels must be in English and carry South African importer details; confirm with the importer before shipping.
- Health Promotion Levy applies to sugary beverages only, not to confectionery (SARS policy), so it does not affect this range.
- Trade: the SACUM UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives UK goods preferential tariffs into SACU; the exact duty for each confectionery tariff line was not verified and should be checked in the SACUM preferential tariff document.
