## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in South Africa reach shelf through two routes. Modern trade is led by the big grocery groups (Shoprite Checkers, Pick n Pay, SPAR, Woolworths) and the pharmacy chains Clicks and Dis-Chem, which buy centrally into their own distribution centres. Traditional trade, made up of spaza shops, township independents and informal traders, is large and is reached through wholesalers and cash and carry (such as Makro and Jumbo in the Massmart group) and through FMCG and field sales distributors that run van sales and rep calls; independent operators are described as a major force in South African retail. Pharmacy distribution also runs through wholesalers such as UPD, which the Clicks group owns. Current shares by route were not verified in this run because the search budget ran out.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: a few grocery groups and two pharmacy chains hold most modern trade shelf space; traditional trade needs a distributor with its own fleet and reps.
- Listing and promotional fees: national chains commonly ask for listing fees, promotional support and rebates; exact levels unverified.
- Margins and credit: chains and wholesalers usually expect long payment terms; figures for this range were not verified.
- Sweeteners: permitted sweeteners and use levels are set by the Sweeteners in Foodstuffs regulations (R733 of 2012); stevia, inulin and polyol use needs checking product by product.
- Laxative warning: foods with added polyols above set levels need a warning; the exact wording and threshold should be confirmed.
- Claims: "sugar free" and "no added sugar" claims are controlled under the labelling regulations (R146, with later amendments and a draft revision); UK pack wording may not comply.
- Labelling: a South African importer name and address and local format labelling are required, so a sticker or local pack is likely.
- Duty: the SACUM UK Economic Partnership Agreement gives UK origin goods preferential access; the exact rate for confectionery should be checked in the SACUM preferential tariff reference; VAT of 15% applies.
Sources: [Sweeteners in Foodstuffs R733 of 2012](https://www.health.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Sweeteners-in-Foodstuffs-R-733-of-2012.pdf), [R146 (Georgetown Global Food Laws)](https://globalfoodlaws.georgetown.edu/documents/no-r146/), [GOV.UK: SACUM UK EPA summary](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/summary-of-the-sacum-uk-economic-partnership-agreement-epa), [SACUM preferential tariff reference](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a0dd2105c3c79da61662e4d/The_SACUM_Preferential_Tariff_Reference_Document_v1.8.docx)
