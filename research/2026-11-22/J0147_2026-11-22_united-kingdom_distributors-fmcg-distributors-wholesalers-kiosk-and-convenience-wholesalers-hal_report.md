## Market structure

Note: the shared web search budget for this run was used up by the Channel helpers before the location research started, so this section is written without fresh searches and every point should be treated as unverified.

Packaged confectionery and snacks in the United Kingdom reach shelf through two main routes. The big grocers (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Aldi, Lidl and the Co-op among them) buy direct from brand owners or their UK agents into their own depots, so the buyer at head office is the gatekeeper. The impulse and convenience trade (independent shops, symbol groups, forecourts and newsagents) is served mainly by wholesale: large cash and carry and delivered groups such as Booker and Bestway, buying and symbol groups such as Unitas and the Wholesale Group, and regional confectionery specialists and van sales distributors. Specialist routes add health food wholesalers, ethnic and halal cash and carries, vending suppliers and online marketplaces such as Amazon. Most of the national and regional wholesalers are already in Diablo's contact list, so the remaining gaps are smaller independents and niche specialists.

## Barriers to entry

- Grocery listings usually carry promotional funding and listing support costs; terms are negotiated and not published (unverified).
- Wholesale and buying groups often expect promotional funding for their promotions schemes and a margin that covers delivery to small shops (unverified, figures not checked).
- Big wholesale groups dominate the independent trade, so being listed with a few of them decides reach in convenience.
- Sweeteners and polyols must be permitted for the food category under retained food additives law (assimilated Regulation 1333/2008); polyol products above 10% added polyols need the "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects" warning.
- "Sugar free" and "no added sugars" claims follow the assimilated nutrition and health claims rules (Regulation 1924/2006); claims are checked by trading standards.
- Labelling must meet UK food information rules with a UK or NI address for the food business operator; Northern Ireland follows EU rules under the Windsor Framework.
- Diablo's exporter B Healthy Ltd is a UK company, so import duty applies only to goods made outside the UK; rules of origin under the UK and EU trade deal should be checked for each line (unverified).
