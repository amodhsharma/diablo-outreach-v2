## Market structure

Packaged confectionery and snacks in the United Kingdom reach shelf through two main routes. The first is the large grocery multiples (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Aldi, Lidl, the Co-op group and Waitrose), which buy centrally into their own depots; their category buyers are the main gatekeepers, and exact current market shares should be taken from the latest Kantar release (not verified here). The second is the wholesale route into independent and symbol convenience stores, forecourts and impulse outlets: cash and carry and delivered wholesalers, buying groups such as Unitas and Bestway or Booker linked symbol fascias, and van sales and field sales distributors who service smaller shops directly. Pharmacies are reached mostly through pharmacy wholesalers and the big health and beauty chains, which also buy centrally. Specialist routes such as kosher and health food distributors serve concentrated communities and independents. Online grocery (Ocado and the multiples' own sites) and Amazon matter for range extension. The practical gatekeepers are central buyers at the multiples and the large wholesalers, plus the regional field sales distributors that place stock in independents. Many of the largest players are already in Diablo's contact list, so new partners are mainly smaller regional or specialist businesses.

## Barriers to entry

- Listing and supplier terms: the large grocers are bound by the Groceries Supply Code of Practice, overseen by the Groceries Code Adjudicator, which limits but does not ban listing payments and promotional contributions; actual fee levels are not public and unverified.
- HFSS rules: in England, products in scope of the high fat, sugar and salt rules face location restrictions (no checkouts, store entrances or aisle ends) in larger stores, and volume price promotion rules have been delayed; Wales and Scotland have their own timetables. Whether each Diablo product counts as HFSS depends on its nutrient profile score, so sugar free does not automatically mean exempt (unverified per product).
- Sweeteners and polyols: GB keeps the EU based additives rules as retained law; steviol glycosides and polyols are permitted in confectionery within set conditions, and products with more than 10% added polyols must carry "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects".
- Claims: "sugar free" (no more than 0.5 g sugars per 100 g) and "no added sugars" follow the retained GB nutrition and health claims rules; a "no added sugars" product with natural sugars must state "contains naturally occurring sugars".
- Labelling: GB and Northern Ireland differ under the Windsor Framework; goods moving to Northern Ireland retail under the Retail Movement Scheme need "Not for EU" marking, and wider GB "Not for EU" labelling plans have changed over time, so the current scope should be checked.
- Margins and credit: wholesaler and distributor margins and payment terms are commercial and not public; 30 to 60 day terms are typical in grocery but this is unverified for this run.
- Duty and trade: Diablo exports from B Healthy Ltd (UK), so it is a domestic supplier; any duty depends on where each product is made, and EU origin goods can enter tariff free under the UK EU Trade and Cooperation Agreement when rules of origin are met (origin of each SKU not verified here).
