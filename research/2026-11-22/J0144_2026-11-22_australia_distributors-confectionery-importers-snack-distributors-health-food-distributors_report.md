## Market structure

Warning: the run's shared web search budget was used up by the Channel helpers before this section could be researched, so it was written without fresh searches and every point below is unverified and should be checked before use.

Packaged confectionery and snacks in Australia reach shelf mainly through the grocery majors: Woolworths and Coles hold most of the supermarket market, with Aldi a strong third and Metcash supplying the independent IGA network as the main wholesale gatekeeper. Woolworths and Coles generally buy centrally into their own distribution centres, so a range needs either a direct listing or a local importer or distributor that already holds supplier accounts. Convenience and route trade (petrol stations, newsagents, vending) is served by specialist wholesalers and cash and carry operators. Health food shops, pharmacies (notably the Chemist Warehouse group) and online grocery are the likeliest first channels for a sugar free range, usually through a local importer that handles compliance labelling, storage and sales calls. Amazon Australia and the grocers' own online shops matter for online orders. (Unverified.)
Sources: [Food Standards Australia New Zealand: Food Standards Code](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/food-standards-code)

## Barriers to entry

- Grocery majors expect promotional support and trading terms from suppliers; exact listing fees are not public (unverified).
- Local distributors commonly ask for exclusivity by channel or nationally and work on margins plus marketing support; figures not verified this run.
- Sweeteners such as steviol glycosides and polyols must be permitted for the food category under the Food Standards Code (Standard 1.3.1 and Schedule 15), to be checked product by product.
- Foods with added polyols above set levels need an advisory statement that excess consumption may have a laxative effect (Standard 1.2.3); check thresholds per product.
- "Sugar free" and "no added sugar" are nutrition content claims with conditions under Standard 1.2.7; UK pack wording must be checked against them.
- Imports need Australian labelling (importer name and address in Australia, Country of Origin Labelling) and are subject to imported food inspection; shelf life at entry is usually set by the importer (unverified).
- The Australia UK Free Trade Agreement (in force from 31 May 2023) removed most tariffs on UK origin goods, subject to rules of origin.
