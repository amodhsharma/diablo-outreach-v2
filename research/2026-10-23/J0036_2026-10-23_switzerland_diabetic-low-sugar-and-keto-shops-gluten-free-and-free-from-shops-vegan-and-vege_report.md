## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly. Coop holds about 43% of food and near food sales and Migros about 37.4% (Nielsen Retail Monitor, reported November 2025), so their central buying teams are the main gatekeepers for mainstream shelf space. Lidl is expanding and Aldi is holding its store count; Denner (Migros group) is the main discounter (Denner share unverified). Outside the duopoly, packaged confectionery for special diets reaches consumers through independent health food shops (Reformhäuser), organic and vegan shops, online diet shops, sports nutrition shops and community food shops. Halal and kosher food reaches shelf mainly through a small number of importers and the community shops they supply, often bought in from Germany, France or Belgium rather than imported direct (this pattern is unverified for Diablo's categories). Specialist wholesalers and importers that hold Swiss stock and handle trilingual labelling are the realistic route in for a UK brand; the duopoly is a later step. This run's web search limit was reached early, so these points rest on a small number of sources.

## Barriers to entry

- Dominant players: Coop and Migros together hold roughly four fifths of food retail and run strong own label ranges, so listing is hard and slow for a new brand.
- Listing fees, exclusivity and distributor margins: not verified in this run; expect a Swiss importer to ask for exclusivity and to carry the labelling cost (unverified).
- Trade agreement: the Switzerland UK trade agreement keeps the preferences of the Switzerland EU free trade and agriculture agreements; confectionery (HS chapters 17 and 18) falls under processed agricultural rules, not the full industrial duty exemption, so some duty may apply. Check the Swiss tariff (Tares) per product.
- Rules of origin: UK and Swiss cumulation is allowed, but EU inputs need more than minimal processing in the UK; UK origin of Diablo's products should be confirmed.
- Sweeteners and claims: Swiss food law broadly follows EU rules on sweeteners, polyols, the laxative warning and sugar free or no added sugar claims, but this run could not verify the Swiss texts; treat as unverified.
- Labelling: labels must be in at least one Swiss official language, and multilingual labels are the norm for national listing (not verified in this run).
- Shelf life at entry: not verified in this run.
