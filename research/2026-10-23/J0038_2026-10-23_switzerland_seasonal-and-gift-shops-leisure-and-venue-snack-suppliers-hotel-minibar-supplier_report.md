## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly: Coop holds about 43% of food and near food sales and Migros about 37.4% (Nielsen, reported November 2025), so their central buyers remain the main gatekeepers for packaged sweets and snacks. The channels in this job sit at the edges of that duopoly and are thin. Leisure venues, hotel minibars and vending are largely supplied by foodservice wholesalers and vending operators (Selecta is the long-standing vending leader and Migros now runs its own snack machines), most of which were already researched in earlier jobs. Seasonal and gift shops are mostly chocolatiers and deli basket makers selling their own goods, snack subscription boxes are few and small, and promotional merchandise firms treat sweets as a printed, made-to-order sideline. For Diablo the realistic route into these channels is through the stockholding wholesalers that already carry other brands' packaged snacks, rather than the channels themselves. ## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Coop and Migros together hold about 80% of food sales and Migros is also a vending operator, so the duopoly reaches into snacking away from home too.
- Hotel and leisure supply: runs mostly through large foodservice wholesalers and vending operators, so a Swiss stockholding wholesaler is likely needed (helper finding, unverified).
- Listing fees, margins and credit: no verified Swiss figures found; expect central listing negotiations and promotional contributions (unverified).
- Labelling: mandatory information in at least one official language, minimum 1.2 mm font, the name and address of the responsible business and the country of production.
- Claims: "zuckerfrei" and similar nutrition claims are governed by the FDHA Ordinance on Food Information (LIV, Article 29 and Annex 13); exact Swiss wording for "no added sugar" not checked here.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted uses are set by the Swiss Additives Ordinance (ZuV), which broadly follows EU rules; steviol glycosides are permitted (FSVO stevia page); exact polyol uses and the laxative warning not verified in this run.
- Duty: under the UK Switzerland trade agreement UK processed agricultural products get the same treatment as EU ones if UK rules of origin are met, but Switzerland still charges agricultural components on goods such as chocolate, so duty is not zero.
- Promotional use: branded giveaway firms expect logo printing or custom packs, which Diablo does not offer (helper finding).
- Shelf life at entry: no Swiss specific rule found; minimums are set by each buyer (unverified).
