## Market structure

Packaged confectionery reaches Swiss shoppers mainly through the national grocery chains, which buy centrally. Coop and Migros dominate food retail: a Nielsen Retail Monitor comparison reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% and Migros at 37.4% of food and near food sales. Below them sit the discounters (Denner, Aldi, Lidl), drugstore and pharmacy chains with a food shelf, health food shops supplied by specialist wholesalers, kiosk and convenience formats and online snack shops. Petrol station shops are largely run by or with the big retailers and a few oil brands, and airport and railway travel retail is concentrated in a small number of operators; both buy centrally or through appointed suppliers (operator names and shares not verified in this run). Sports and sports nutrition shops are a smaller, more open channel for bars and snacks. For a foreign brand the real gatekeepers are the central category buyers of Coop and Migros and the importers and wholesalers that supply independents, kiosks and forecourts. ## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Coop and Migros together hold about 80% of food sales, so a brand outside them depends on discounters, specialists, kiosks, forecourts and travel retail. Coop has bought jointly with other European retailers through buying alliances, which strengthens its terms with brands.
- Listing fees, margins and credit: no verified Swiss figures found; assume central listing negotiations and promotional contributions (unverified).
- Labelling: mandatory information in at least one official language, minimum 1.2 mm font, the name and address of the responsible business (the head office need not be in Switzerland) and the country of production.
- Claims: nutrition claims such as "zuckerfrei" are governed by the FDHA Ordinance on Food Information (LIV, Article 29 and Annex 13); the claim must be listed there and its conditions met. Exact Swiss wording for "no added sugar" not checked here.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted additives are set by the Swiss Additives Ordinance (ZuV), revised in 2024, which broadly follows EU additive rules; exact uses for steviol glycosides, polyols and the laxative warning not verified in this run.
- Duty: under the UK Switzerland trade agreement UK processed agricultural products face no higher tariffs than EU ones if UK rules of origin are met. Switzerland still charges agricultural components on processed goods such as chocolate, so duty is not zero.
- Shelf life at entry: no Swiss specific rule found; minimums are set by each buyer (unverified).
