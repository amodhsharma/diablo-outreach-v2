## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly: Coop holds about 43% of food and near food sales and Migros about 37.4% (Nielsen, reported November 2025), so their central buyers are the main gatekeepers for mainstream shelf space. The channels in this job sit around that duopoly and are thin. Workplace snacking runs through vending and smart fridge operators: Selecta is the long-standing leader, Migros has launched its own snack machines priced like its shops, and FELFEL supplies offices with smart fridges (both reported in the Swiss press). Many smaller operators are coffee-led. Hospital and care home kiosks are mostly run by the hospitals' own catering teams or by chains such as Valora, Coop and Migros (helper finding, not independently verified). Kosher retail is a handful of community shops in Zurich, Basel and Geneva, and corporate gifting is led by deli basket makers and chocolatiers selling their own goods. For Diablo the realistic route into these channels is through the wholesalers and vending operators that already stock other brands' packaged snacks, rather than the channels themselves.

## Barriers to entry

- Concentration: Coop and Migros together hold about 80% of food sales and Migros is now also a vending operator, so the duopoly reaches into workplace snacking too.
- Vending and office supply: Selecta and the duopoly's own machines lock up much of the vending space; smaller operators buy through wholesalers, so a Swiss stockholding wholesaler is likely needed (unverified).
- Listing fees, margins and credit: no verified Swiss figures found; expect central listing negotiations and promotional contributions (unverified).
- Labelling: mandatory information in at least one official language, minimum 1.2 mm font, the name and address of the responsible business and the country of production.
- Claims: "zuckerfrei" and similar nutrition claims are governed by the FDHA Ordinance on Food Information (LIV, Article 29 and Annex 13); exact Swiss wording for "no added sugar" not checked here.
- Sweeteners and polyols: permitted uses are set by the Swiss Additives Ordinance (ZuV, revised 2024), which broadly follows EU rules; steviol glycosides are permitted (FSVO stevia page); exact polyol uses and the laxative warning not verified in this run.
- Duty: under the UK Switzerland trade agreement UK processed agricultural products get the same treatment as EU ones if UK rules of origin are met, but Switzerland still charges agricultural components on goods such as chocolate, so duty is not zero.
- Kosher and halal: kosher shops expect certified products; which Diablo lines carry kosher certification should be confirmed before approaching them.
- Shelf life at entry: no Swiss specific rule found; minimums are set by each buyer (unverified).
