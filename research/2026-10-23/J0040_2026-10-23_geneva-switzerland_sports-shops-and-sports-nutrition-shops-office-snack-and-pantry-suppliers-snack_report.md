## Market structure

Swiss grocery is held by two cooperatives. Nielsen Retail Monitor figures reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% of food and near-food sales and Migros at 37.4%, so their central buying desks are the main gatekeepers for any branded snack; Lidl is expanding and Aldi is also present, but this run could not verify discounter shares. Geneva follows the national picture, with French as the trading language. For the channels in this job, product reaches shelf through Swiss importers and wholesalers that list foreign brands for specialist retail, through the few operators that stock vending machines and office pantries (largely national players already researched, such as Selecta), and through online shops delivering across Switzerland. This run found very few independent operators in these channels in Geneva itself: most fitting companies are already on Diablo's list, and many local sports, gift and hamper businesses sell their own products or no packaged food. The shared web search budget ran out early in this run (about 200 searches across all helpers and the manager), so treat the channel picture as incomplete and unverified beyond the sources below.

## Barriers to entry

- Retailer concentration: with Coop and Migros together above 80% of food and near-food sales (Nielsen, November 2025), a listing with either is decisive and hard to win. Listing fees, exclusivity and margin norms were not verified in this run.
- Vending and office channels are concentrated in a few national operators, several of which hold direct partnerships with large confectionery brands (for example Selecta with Mars Wrigley), which limits space for a new brand. Operator margin and credit terms were not verified.
- Sweeteners and polyols: Switzerland's Additives Ordinance (ZuV, SR 817.022.31) sets permitted sweeteners and their uses in its annexes and defines "no added sugar" foods (Art. 15). The exact permitted list per product type was not checked here, so each SKU's sweetener system needs checking against the annexes.
- Laxative warning: products with polyols must carry the statement that excessive consumption may have a laxative effect (ZuV Art. 9a para. 2 c). The ordinance text seen sets no percentage threshold; whether a 10% threshold applies as in the EU was not verified.
- Claims and labelling: "sugar free" and "no added sugar" claim conditions and the language rules for Swiss labels (at least one official language, French in Geneva) were not verified in this run because the search budget ran out; check the Food Information Ordinance (LIV) before shipping.
- Shelf life at entry: no Swiss norm was verified in this run.
- Trade agreement: the Switzerland-UK trade agreement carries over the Switzerland-EU free trade agreement and the agriculture agreement, so preferential treatment continues for processed agricultural products such as confectionery. Origin rules apply (Protocol 3, bilateral cumulation); the exact duty on each tariff line, including any agricultural component, was not verified.
