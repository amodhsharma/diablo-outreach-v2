## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly: Nielsen data reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% of food and near-food sales and Migros at 37.4%, with Denner, Lidl, Aldi and Volg behind them. Coop is headquartered in Basel, and private label is strong at both cooperatives, so a foreign branded snack competes for limited branded shelf space and their central buying desks are the main gatekeepers. Basel sits on the German and French borders, and Swiss shoppers regularly buy groceries in neighbouring Germany and France where prices are lower, which puts extra price pressure on local specialist shops (the size of the Basel effect was not verified in this run). For the channels in this job, product reaches shelf through Swiss importers and wholesalers that list foreign brands for specialist retail, through a few national vending and office pantry operators (most already on Diablo's list, such as Selecta and FELFEL) and through online shops delivering across Switzerland. This run found very few independent Basel companies that fit these channels: most fitting companies are already known to Diablo, and many local sports, gift and hamper businesses sell their own products or no packaged food.

## Barriers to entry

- Two cooperatives control most grocery shelf space and push own label hard; listing terms and fees are not public (unverified).
- Distributor margin, credit and exclusivity norms could not be verified in this run; expect importers to ask for national exclusivity (unverified).
- Cross-border shopping into Germany and France caps the retail price a Basel shop can charge (unverified for this range).
- UK goods: the UK Switzerland trade agreement says UK processed agricultural goods face tariffs no higher than EU goods; rates must be checked per tariff line, and preferential origin needs an EUR1 or origin declaration.
- Swiss food law broadly follows EU rules on sweeteners, polyols and nutrition claims, including a laxative warning for products with more than 10% added polyols; the exact Swiss ordinance wording was not verified in this run.
- Labels must be in at least one Swiss official language (German for Basel); other Swiss specific labelling details were not verified.
- Shelf life at entry and fees in the vending and office channels: not verified.
