## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly: Nielsen data reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% of food and near-food sales and Migros at 37.4%, with Denner, Lidl, Aldi and Volg behind them. Both cooperatives push strong private label, so a foreign branded snack competes for limited branded shelf space and their central buying desks are the main gatekeepers for mainstream retail. Bern is the German speaking federal capital, a large public sector and office market rather than a retail hub, and the specialist channels in this job are thin there. Sports nutrition reaches Bern shoppers mostly through national chains and Swiss online shops, nearly all already known to Diablo. Corporate gifting and hampers are dominated by firms built around Swiss regional and gourmet products, and seasonal and gift shops in Bern are led by confiseries selling their own production. Specialist product therefore reaches shelf through Swiss importers and wholesalers that list foreign brands, and through online shops delivering across Switzerland. This run found very few independent Bern companies new to Diablo that fit these channels.

## Barriers to entry

- Two cooperatives control most grocery shelf space and push own label hard; listing terms and fees are not public (unverified).
- Distributor margin, credit and exclusivity norms could not be verified in this run; expect importers to ask for national exclusivity (unverified).
- Hamper and gift buyers in Bern favour Swiss regional products, so a UK brand must win on the sugar-free angle rather than origin (observation from this run, not a published figure).
- UK goods: the UK Switzerland trade agreement says UK processed agricultural goods face tariffs no higher than EU goods; rates must be checked per tariff line, and preferential origin needs an EUR1 or origin declaration.
- Swiss food law broadly follows EU rules on sweeteners, polyols and nutrition claims, including a laxative warning for products with more than 10% added polyols; the exact Swiss ordinance wording was not verified in this run.
- Labels must be in at least one Swiss official language (German for Bern); other Swiss specific labelling details were not verified.
- Shelf life at entry and fees in the gifting and seasonal channels: not verified.
