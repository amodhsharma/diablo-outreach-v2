## Market structure

Swiss grocery is a duopoly: Migros and Coop together hold about 70% of grocery retail, with Denner, Lidl, Aldi and Volg behind them. Nielsen data reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% of food and near-food sales and Migros at 37.4%. Private label runs at around half of sales and Migros alone has about 250 own brands, so a foreign branded snack competes for limited branded shelf space and the buying desks of the two cooperatives are the main gatekeepers. Small formats matter in Zurich: Coop Pronto and Migrolino run convenience and petrol station shops, and the discounters are expanding. Outside the big chains, product reaches sports shops, health shops, offices, vending machines and gift shops through Swiss importers and wholesalers, plus a growing online channel (about 11.8% of retail sales in 2020). For the channels in this job, the gatekeepers are the importers who list foreign brands for specialist retail and the operators who stock vending and office pantries. This run found few independent operators in these channels in Zurich itself; the search budget ran out before the market was fully mapped, so treat the channel picture as incomplete.

## Barriers to entry

- Two cooperatives control most grocery shelf space and push own label hard; listing terms and fees are not public (unverified).
- Distributor margin, credit and exclusivity norms could not be verified in this run; expect importers to ask for national exclusivity (unverified).
- UK goods: the UK Switzerland trade agreement says UK processed agricultural goods face tariffs no higher than EU goods; product specific rates must be checked per tariff line. Preferential origin needs an EUR1 or origin declaration, and simple packing or labelling does not confer origin.
- Swiss food law broadly follows EU rules on sweeteners, polyols and nutrition claims, including a laxative warning for products with more than 10% added polyols; the exact Swiss ordinance wording was not verified in this run.
- Labels must be in at least one Swiss official language (German for Zurich); details of Swiss specific labelling (for example origin of raw materials) were not verified in this run.
- Shelf life at entry and listing fees for vending and office channels: not verified.
