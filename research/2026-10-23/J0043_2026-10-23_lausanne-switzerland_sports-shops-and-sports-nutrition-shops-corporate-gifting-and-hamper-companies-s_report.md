## Market structure

Swiss grocery is held by two cooperatives. Nielsen Retail Monitor figures reported in November 2025 put Coop at 43% of food and near-food sales and Migros at 37.4%, so their central buying desks are the main gatekeepers for any branded snack, with Denner, Lidl and Aldi behind them. Lausanne is the French speaking capital of Vaud and trades in French; the Migros regional cooperatives of French speaking Switzerland have moved to shared marketing, so Romandie buying is increasingly run from a few desks. For the channels in this job, specialist product reaches shelf through Swiss importers and wholesalers that list foreign brands, through national sports nutrition chains and Swiss online shops (most already known to Diablo) and through a small number of independent shops. Lausanne gifting and hamper firms are built around Swiss regional and artisanal products, and seasonal and gift shops are led by chocolatiers selling their own production. This run found very few independent Lausanne companies new to Diablo in these channels.
Sources: [Bluewin: Coop overtakes Migros in food](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/coop-overtakes-migros-in-core-business-2944481.html), [Migros: French speaking cooperatives to share marketing](https://corporate.migros.ch/en/news/cooperatives-in-french-speaking-switzerland-to-share-marketing-in-future)

## Barriers to entry

- Two cooperatives control most grocery shelf space and push own label hard; listing terms and fees are not public (unverified).
- Distributor margin, credit and exclusivity norms could not be verified in this run; expect importers to ask for national exclusivity (unverified).
- Gift and hamper buyers in Lausanne favour Swiss regional and artisanal products, so a UK brand must win on the sugar-free angle rather than origin (observation from this run, not a published figure).
- UK goods: the UK Switzerland trade agreement says UK processed agricultural goods face tariffs no higher than EU goods; rates must be checked per tariff line, and preferential origin needs proof of origin.
- Swiss food law broadly follows EU rules on sweeteners, polyols and nutrition claims, including a laxative warning where added polyols exceed 10%; the exact Swiss ordinance wording was not verified in this run.
- Labels must be in at least one Swiss official language (French is the practical choice for Lausanne); other Swiss specific labelling details were not verified.
- Shelf life at entry and fees in the gifting and seasonal channels: not verified.
