## Market structure

Swiss grocery is held by two cooperatives, Coop and Migros, whose central buying desks are the main gatekeepers for any branded snack, with Denner, Lidl and Aldi behind them. Lugano is Italian speaking and sits in the area of Migros Ticino, a regional cooperative; how much listing power sits with it rather than the national desk was not verified. Ticino retail is unusually exposed to shopping across the border in Italy: Migros reported a sharp fall in Ticino sales in 2022 as cross-border shopping returned after the pandemic, so local shoppers compare prices with Italian stores. For the channels in this job, specialist product reaches shelf through Swiss importers and wholesalers that list foreign brands, national sports nutrition chains and Swiss online shops (most already known to Diablo) and a small number of independent Ticino shops. Seasonal and gift buying in Lugano leans on confectioners selling their own production and on national chains and florist networks (observation from this run's research, not a published figure).

## Barriers to entry

- Two cooperatives control most grocery shelf space and push own label hard; listing terms and fees are not public (unverified).
- Ticino prices are compared with nearby Italian stores, which squeezes retail margins for imported snacks (inference from reported cross-border shopping, not a published figure).
- Distributor margin, credit and exclusivity norms could not be verified in this run; expect importers to ask for national exclusivity (unverified).
- Nutrition claims such as "sugar free" may only be used if listed in Annex 13 of the Swiss food information ordinance (LIV), with its conditions; Swiss rules broadly follow the EU, but exact wording for "no added sugar" was not checked in this run.
- Sweeteners and polyols are permitted under Swiss additive rules broadly aligned with the EU, including a laxative warning where added polyols exceed 10%; the exact Swiss text was not verified in this run.
- Labels must be in at least one Swiss official language; Italian is the practical choice for Lugano.
- Switzerland abolished industrial tariffs on 1 January 2024, but processed agricultural goods such as chocolate and biscuits keep an agricultural component; under the UK Switzerland trade agreement UK goods face no higher tariffs than EU goods, checked per tariff line with proof of origin.
- Shelf life at entry and fees in the sports and gift channels: not verified.
