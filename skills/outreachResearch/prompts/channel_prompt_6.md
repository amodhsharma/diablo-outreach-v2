HARD RULES (decided by Diablo on 21 and 22 Oct 2026, and on 10 Nov 2026 for big partners only. They apply to every channel, and HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY under WHAT THIS CHANNEL MEANS says how for this one. A company that breaks any rule is never listed: put it in checked_and_excluded with the reason in brackets. 1. No company that mainly supplies its own products, whether bakery and pastry goods, raw materials or semi-finished products ("own products"). We sell our own branded, labelled products, so the company must sell other companies' brands.
2. No company whose business is its own catalogue, own brand or own label: it will not sell ours ("own range"). A distributor that mainly sells other companies' brands may also have a house brand or two; it is out only when its own brands are the main business. Do not mention such a house brand in the text.
3. No company that sells generic goods it makes itself ("makes its own goods").
   For rules 1 to 3, read the About page in the local language for signs that the company makes or brands what it sells (own production, own manufacturing, own brand, "Eigenproduktion", "fabrication maison", "produzione propria", "producción propia", "fabricação própria").
4. The company must buy and hold the products itself, in its own storage ("does not hold stock"). Sales agencies, brokers, merchandising companies and brand representation companies that never buy the goods are out ("agency"), even when they call themselves distributors.
   - For distributors and wholesalers, storage must be seen: a warehouse, depot, distribution centre or logistics centre, a cash and carry, its own delivery fleet, or a B2B web shop with stock and prices. Write what you saw after "Storage: " in the fit rationale ("no storage seen" is a reason to reject).
   - For supermarket chains, their stores are their storage; no storage line is needed.
5. The company must already sell at least one category from DIABLO'S CATALOGUE as a real part of its range, seen on its own website: product pages, product categories, or a brand list or principals page ("nothing from our catalogue").
   - A brand list or principals page that names confectionery, chocolate, biscuit or snack brands (for example Ferrero, Mondelez, Nestlé, Mars, Haribo, Hershey's, Lotus, Bauducco, Arcor, Eti) proves it. Name the categories and the brands in "distributes".
   - A directory, registry or trade listing alone never proves it.
   - One catalogue word in a range that is mostly something else does not count. Table-top sweeteners, honey, flour, baking ingredients, fruit puree, nut mixes and Italian pastry are not our catalogue.
   - When the company's own website will not open for you (rule 9), its own social pages or a brand owner's page naming it as distributor may show the range instead.
   - The exception for importers: an importer whose About page says its business is bringing foreign packaged food or snack brands into the market's shops and building them there passes this rule even when its brand list shows no sweets. Write "imports and builds packaged food brands" in "distributes".
6. Food must be one of the company's main businesses ("not a food business"). Out: pharmacy and drugstore wholesalers whose main lines are medicines, medical equipment or OTC products; packaging suppliers; drinks-only and wine-only merchants; supplement sellers; coffee-led businesses. In: FMCG distributors with a packaged food, confectionery or snacks division next to personal care or household products (for example Distribuidora Corripio, Muller & Phipps).
7. No suppliers to kitchens ("ingredients for professionals"). Out: suppliers of ingredients, raw materials, semi-finished products or bulk catering packs to bakeries, pastry makers, restaurants, hotels and caterers, and broadline foodservice wholesalers whose customers cook with what they buy. A distributor with a foodservice division still counts when it also supplies shops with branded packs.
8. No delicatessen, gourmet or single-cuisine specialists ("deli or cuisine specialist"): fine food and gourmet importers, importers of one country's cuisine, ethnic and world food wholesalers.
   - The exception: branded packaged sweets, biscuits or snacks for resale are a clear part of their range and they sell to shops, not mainly to restaurants.
   - Importers of British branded packaged food, sweets and snacks for shops are not cuisine specialists: Diablo is a British brand.
   - Halal and kosher distributors and wholesalers are not cuisine specialists when their own website shows branded packaged sweets, biscuits or snacks for resale and they sell to shops. Halal butchers, kosher bakeries and caterers are still out.
   - Searching in a local language must not turn into a search for that country's cuisine.
9. The company must have its own website, it must open, and it must show that the company still sells food today ("no website", "website does not open", "no longer sells food").
   - Before rejecting a website that will not load, try the domain again with and without "www". Large companies' sites sometimes block automated visitors: when the site still will not open but the company's own social pages, a brand owner's distributor page or a recent news article show it trading as a distributor with Diablo's categories, say so in the fit rationale and list it at tier 2, at medium confidence.
   - Give the current domain: when an old domain redirects to a new name, give the new one.
   - A company that is closing down or in liquidation is out ("closing down").
10. No chilled or frozen food specialists: Diablo's products are ambient ("chilled or frozen specialist").
11. No low confidence and no hedged fit ("weak fit").
   - Never list a company whose fit itself depends on something you could not confirm, for example whether it sells sweets or snacks at all. When your own fit rationale would need words such as "range extension", "would be new to its range", "not its core", "outside its focus", "side line", "add-on", "conditional", "check first", "confectionery not confirmed", "rather than a distributor" or "lower priority", the company is rejected.
   - A missing detail that does not decide the fit (exact delivery area, customer names) is never a reason to reject. List the company and state the detail plainly.
12. The company must be big enough to take Diablo's range to many shops ("too small", decided 10 Nov 2026). It passes when its own website, a stock market listing or a recent news article shows at least one of these:
   - it supplies at least about 300 shops, outlets or trade customers, or names several supermarket, convenience or pharmacy chains among its customers;
   - it covers a whole region, state or province, or the whole country (several branches, depots or distribution centres, or delivery across the region or country);
   - a warehouse or distribution centre of about 1,000 m2 or more, its own fleet of about 10 delivery vehicles or more, or a field sales force of about 10 people or more;
   - it is the appointed distributor for international food or confectionery brands in the market;
   - it is listed on a stock exchange or part of a large group.
   For supermarket chains: at least about 10 stores, or a regional or national chain that buys centrally.
   Out: single shops, one-site family businesses, small importers that sell mainly through their own web shop, and any company whose website shows it reaches only a handful of customers. Write what you saw after "Scale: " in the fit rationale, before "Storage: ", for example "Scale: 8,000 points of sale across Rio Grande do Sul, 48-hour delivery."
The check refuses low confidence, hedged wording, own-product wording, directory-only evidence, a missing "Scale: " or "Storage: " line, tier 3 and any company whose "distributes" names nothing from the catalogue. In the text, never mention own production, own brands, own manufacturing or own label, not even to say the company has none, and never use "rather than": the check reads those words as a reason to refuse.

HOW TO CHECK EACH COMPANY (in this order; stop at the first rule it breaks)
1. Open its own website. No website, or it does not open: rule 9.
2. Read the About page: what the business is, whether it makes or brands its own products (rules 1 to 3), how big it is and where it delivers (rule 12), whether it still sells food (rule 9), and who its customers are (rules 7 and 8).
3. Read the brand list, principals page or product categories: which of Diablo's categories it sells, as other companies' brands (rule 5); whether food is one of its main businesses (rule 6); whether it is chilled or frozen led (rule 10).
4. For distributors and wholesalers, find how they hold stock: a warehouse, depot, distribution centre, cash and carry, own fleet or B2B web shop (rule 4).
5. Only then decide: list it, or reject it with its reason.
