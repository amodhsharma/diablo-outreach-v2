CLIENT CONTEXT
The client is Diablo Sugar Free, Europe's largest no-added-sugar and sugar-free snacks company: 117+ products across 11 categories, sweetened with stevia and inulin, never aspartame, selling in more than 124 countries. Exporting entity: B Healthy Ltd (UK). It is looking for route to market partners in {{MARKET}}.
Diablo sells only its own branded, labelled, finished products in consumer packs, for resale to shoppers. It never sells ingredients, raw materials, semi-finished products, bulk catering packs or own-label goods. So a partner must be a business that sells other companies' brands to shoppers, or to the shops that serve them.

WHO DIABLO WANTS (decided by Diablo on 21 Oct 2026, from its review of all the Swiss research)
A company that buys other companies' branded, packaged sweets, chocolate, biscuits or snacks, holds them in stock and sells them on as they are: to supermarkets, independent grocers, kiosks, petrol station shops, convenience stores, vending, health food shops, pharmacies and drugstores that sell food, online shops, or directly to shoppers. The best partners say on their own website that this is their business, and their About page often shows it most clearly.

HARD RULES (decided by Diablo on 21 Oct 2026; they apply to every channel, and HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY under WHAT THIS CHANNEL MEANS says how for this one. A company that breaks any rule is never listed: put it in checked_and_excluded with the reason in brackets.)
1. No company that mainly supplies its own products, whether bakery and pastry goods, raw materials or semi-finished products ("own products"). We sell our own branded, labelled products, so the company must sell other companies' brands.
2. No company that sells its own catalogue, own brand or own label: it will not sell ours ("own range").
3. No company that sells generic goods it makes itself ("makes its own goods").
   For rules 1 to 3, read the About page in the local language for signs that the company makes or brands what it sells (own production, own manufacturing, own brand, "Eigenproduktion", "Herstellung", "fabrication maison", "produzione propria", "brand in its own right").
4. The company must buy and hold the products itself ("does not hold stock"). Sales agencies, brokers and brand representation companies that never buy the goods are out ("agency"). Distributors and wholesalers must have their own warehouse, depot or logistics centre; confirm it and write it after "Storage: " in the fit rationale ("no storage confirmed").
5. The company must already sell at least one category from DIABLO'S CATALOGUE as a real part of its range, seen on its own website: product pages, product categories or brand list ("nothing from our catalogue").
   - A directory, registry or trade listing alone never proves it.
   - One catalogue word in a range that is mostly something else does not count. Table-top sweeteners, honey, flour, baking ingredients, fruit puree, nut mixes and Italian pastry are not our catalogue.
   - Name the categories in "distributes" with the catalogue's words. When the company carries a known sweets or biscuit brand (for example Eti, Haribo, Trolli, Lotus), name that category too.
   - One exception: an importer whose About page says its business is bringing foreign packaged food or snack brands into the market's shops and building them there passes this rule even when its brand list shows no sweets. Write "imports and builds packaged food brands" in "distributes".
6. Food must be the company's main business ("not a food business"). Out: pharmacy and drugstore wholesalers whose main lines are medicines, medical equipment, OTC or generic pharmacy products; packaging suppliers; drinks-only and wine-only merchants; sports nutrition and supplement sellers without snacks; coffee-led service companies such as office coffee and vending operators that run their own machines. A wholesaler that supplies vending operators with packaged snacks still counts.
7. No suppliers to kitchens ("ingredients for professionals"). Out: suppliers of ingredients, raw materials, semi-finished products or bulk catering packs to gelato and pastry makers, bakeries, restaurants, hotels and caterers, even when the range includes chocolate, syrups or toppings; and broadline foodservice wholesalers whose customers cook with what they buy (restaurant, hotel, canteen, hospital and care home kitchens). Diablo's packs are sold to shoppers as they are. A supplier of snack counters and kiosks that sell packs as they are (for example in leisure parks or pools) still counts, at tier 3.
8. No delicatessen, gourmet or cuisine specialists ("deli or cuisine specialist"). Out: fine food, delicatessen, gourmet and artisanal specialists (cheese, charcuterie, truffles, wine, preserves, panettone); importers of one country's or one region's cuisine (for example Italian, Mexican, Spanish, Greek, Japanese or Asian food); ethnic and world food wholesalers.
   - The exception: branded packaged sweets, biscuits or snacks for resale are a clear part of their range (their own website has a sweets or snacks section) and they sell to shops, not mainly to restaurants. A Balkan importer with its own "snacks and sweets" section selling to food shops counts; so does a Turkish wholesaler supplying supermarkets with branded biscuits such as Eti.
   - Searching in a local language must not turn into a search for that country's cuisine: in an Italian speaking city, look for confectionery and snack distributors, not Italian food importers.
9. The company must have its own website, it must open, and it must show that the company still sells food today ("no website", "website does not open", "no longer sells food").
   - A marketplace seller with no website of its own: its storefront must open.
   - Give the current domain: when an old domain redirects to a new name, give the new one.
   - Directories and trade press can be years old. A food and OTC distributor found that way had stopped selling food the year before.
10. No chilled or frozen food specialists: Diablo's products are ambient ("chilled or frozen specialist").
11. No low confidence and no hedged fit ("weak fit").
   - Low confidence is not a reason to drop a company that looks right: open its own website, and when the range is confirmed there, list it at medium confidence. If it cannot be confirmed, reject it.
   - Never list a company whose fit depends on something you could not confirm. When your own fit rationale would need words such as "range extension", "would be new to its range", "not its core", "outside its focus", "side line", "add-on", "conditional", "check first", "confectionery not confirmed", "rather than a distributor" or "lower priority", the company is rejected.
   The check refuses low confidence, hedged wording, own-product wording, directory-only evidence and any company whose "distributes" names nothing from the catalogue.

HOW TO CHECK EACH COMPANY (in this order; stop at the first rule it breaks)
1. Open its own website. No website, or it does not open: reject (rule 9).
2. Read the About page: what the business is, whether it makes or brands its own products (rules 1 to 3), whether it brings other brands to shops (the rule 5 exception), whether it still sells food (rule 9), and who its customers are (rules 7 and 8).
3. Read the product categories or brand list: which of Diablo's categories it sells, as other companies' brands (rule 5); whether food is its main business (rule 6); whether it is chilled or frozen led (rule 10).
4. For distributors and wholesalers, find the warehouse or depot (rule 4).
5. Only then decide: list it, or reject it with its reason.
