WHO COUNTS
Importers and distributors that buy other companies' branded, packaged sweets, chocolate, biscuits or snacks, hold them in their own warehouse and resell them as they are to supermarkets, independent grocers, kiosks, petrol station shops, convenience stores, vending, health food shops, pharmacies and drugstores that sell food, or online shops in the market. Mid-size, owner-run importers whose business is bringing foreign brands into the market and building them there count fully, including importers of British brands.

LEAVE OUT
Retailers that do not wholesale, brand owners, exporters and logistics-only providers (3PLs, freight forwarders) that never buy the goods. Sales and marketing agencies, brokers, import agents and brand representation companies that do not buy and hold stock, even when they call themselves distributors. Everything the HARD RULES leave out: own products or own label, kitchens and ingredients, delicatessen and cuisine specialists, pharmacy, drinks and coffee businesses, chilled and frozen specialists, no website, and anything that sells nothing from Diablo's catalogue.

WHERE TO LOOK
Start where trade buyers are listed: national importer and supplier association member lists, confectionery and snack trade bodies, importer directories (for example bestfoodimporters.com, Europages, Kompass) and the country's grocery, convenience and confectionery trade press. Also trade show exhibitor and visitor lists (sweets and snacks fairs), retailer supplier pages, company registries and import shipment databases (for example Volza, ImportGenius). Search for the right kind of business: confectionery importer, sweets and snacks distributor, kiosk supplier, and the market's words for them. Use a directory only to find a company, then check it on its own website.

WHAT GOOD FIT LOOKS LIKE
The model: a distributor that buys other companies' branded sweets, chocolate, biscuits and snacks, holds them in its own warehouse and sells them on to supermarkets, independent shops, kiosks or vending (in the Swiss review: Cruspi, R.C. Andreae, easytrade distribution, Rufenberg, Conaxess). Prioritise, in this order: imports European or UK branded confectionery and snacks; a mid-size, owner-run importer where a decision maker can be reached; named access to retail, convenience, kiosk or pharmacy chains in the market; already carries sugar-free or better-for-you lines without a direct competitor; import licence and storage suited to chocolate. A mid-size specialist importer may outrank a large generalist.

HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY
All eleven apply in full. Rule 4: confirm the distributor's own warehouse, depot or logistics centre and write it after "Storage: " in the fit rationale. Rule 5: a brand-building importer (the rule 5 exception) belongs here and ranks tier 1 when owner-run. Rule 6: pharmacy distributors count only when packaged food or confectionery is a main line, not a side group next to medicines, OTC products and medical supplies.
