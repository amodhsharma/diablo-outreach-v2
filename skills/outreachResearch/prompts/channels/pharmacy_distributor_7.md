WHO COUNTS
Companies that buy, hold and sell other companies' branded consumer goods into pharmacies, parapharmacies, chemists and drugstores across a region or the whole country, when branded sweets, chocolate, biscuits, snack bars or other packaged food are a real part of what they sell (added 21 Nov 2026). This includes OTC and consumer health distributors, parapharmacy distributors, health and beauty distributors that supply pharmacies, and full-line pharmacy wholesalers with a consumer or front-of-shop range that already holds packaged sweets or snacks. Sugar-free sweets and snacks already sell in pharmacies in many markets, and pharmacies take a higher shelf price, so these companies can put Diablo's range on pharmacy shelves.

LEAVE OUT
Wholesalers that supply only medicines, prescriptions or medical equipment; supplement brands and sellers whose business is their own range; pharmacy buying groups, banner groups and franchise networks that never buy and hold the goods themselves; sales agencies, brokers and brand representation companies that do not buy stock; single pharmacies and small groups of pharmacies; online-only pharmacies. Everything else the HARD RULES leave out.

WHERE TO LOOK
Start with the brands that already sell sweets and snacks in pharmacies: who distributes sugar-free sweets, lozenges, pastilles, protein and snack bars and better-for-you biscuits to pharmacies in the market (the brand owners' distributor pages and "where to buy" pages, and the distributors' "our brands" or "principals" pages). Then the national pharmaceutical wholesalers' association, consumer health and OTC distributors' associations, pharmacy trade press and rankings, pharmacy trade show exhibitor lists, and the supplier lists of the big pharmacy chains. Search with the trade's own words, for example: pharmacy wholesaler, chemist wholesaler, OTC distributor, consumer health distributor, parapharmacy distributor; Apothekengroßhandel, Pharmagrosshandel, OTC-Distributor; grossiste-répartiteur, distributeur parapharmacie; distributore parafarmacia, grossista farmaceutico; distribuidor de parafarmacia, mayorista farmacéutico; hurtownia farmaceutyczna; drogisterij groothandel. Use a directory only to find a company, then check it on its own website.

WHAT GOOD FIT LOOKS LIKE
The model: a distributor that already takes branded sweets, lozenges, snack bars or functional foods from other companies into hundreds of pharmacies, with its own reps calling on them and named pharmacy chains among its customers. Prioritise: national or regional reach into pharmacies; already carries branded confectionery or snacks for pharmacies; supplies pharmacy chains; its own field sales team; already carries sugar-free lines without a direct competitor. Galexis was rejected in the Swiss review of 21 Oct 2026 as not a food business; under this channel a full-line pharmacy wholesaler like it counts only when its own range shows branded sweets or snacks for pharmacy shelves.

HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY
Rule 6 for this channel: a pharmacy, OTC or consumer health distributor counts when branded sweets, chocolate, biscuits, bars or other packaged food are a real line in what it sells to pharmacies, even when medicines or personal care are its biggest lines. A wholesaler with medicines only is still out ("not a food business").
Rule 5: its own website must name at least one category from DIABLO'S CATALOGUE that it already sells (hard sweets, pastilles and lozenges sold as sweets count as sweets; protein and snack bars count as bars). Name the categories and brands in "distributes".
Rule 4 for this channel: write after "Storage: " whatever shows that it buys and delivers the goods itself (a warehouse or depot address, its own deliveries or van routes, trade ordering from stock). Reject as "does not hold stock" only when what you see shows that it is an agency that never buys the goods.
Rule 12 for this channel (21 Nov 2026, scaled down and read with judgement): it passes when it shows any one of these: about 200 or more pharmacies or other outlets supplied; regional or national coverage; a warehouse of about 700 m2 or more; about 7 delivery vehicles or more; about 7 sales reps or field agents or more; it is the appointed distributor for international brands; it is listed or part of a large group. When none of these numbers is shown, a company that calls itself a distributor or wholesaler still passes on ONE solid piece of evidence: a principals or brand list, a warehouse, depot or cash and carry address, a delivery fleet or van routes, a trade or B2B ordering portal or trade account sign-up, named pharmacies or chains it supplies, or membership of a wholesale or trade association. Its own word alone is never enough. Write the solid thing after "Scale: ".
All the other rules apply in full.
