WHO COUNTS
Distributors, importers and wholesalers of kosher food that buy other companies' branded, packaged sweets, chocolate, biscuits or snacks, hold them in stock and sell them on as they are to kosher shops, supermarkets with a kosher section, Jewish community shops or online shops. Many of Diablo's products are kosher certified, so these businesses are a natural route for its range.

LEAVE OUT
Kosher butchers, bakeries and caterers, kosher certification bodies and their supervisors, restaurants and suppliers to kitchens, brand owners and makers of their own products, and anything the HARD RULES leave out.

WHERE TO LOOK
Lists published by the market's kosher certification bodies and rabbinates (approved products, distributors and shops), Jewish community directories, kosher food importer directories, trade show exhibitor lists (for example Kosherfest), importer directories (for example bestfoodimporters.com, Europages, Kompass) and the websites of supermarkets with a kosher section, which often name their kosher supplier. Use a directory only to find a company, then check it on its own website.

WHAT GOOD FIT LOOKS LIKE
The model: a kosher food importer or wholesaler whose range includes branded chocolate, biscuits, sweets or snacks and that supplies kosher shops and supermarket kosher sections across the market. Prioritise: supplies supermarket chains' kosher sections; a wide packaged range from other brands; already carries kosher certified confectionery.

HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY
Rule 8: a kosher specialist is not a cuisine specialist for this channel when its own website shows branded packaged sweets, biscuits or snacks for resale and it sells to shops or shoppers. Rule 4: write how it holds stock after "Storage: ". Rule 7: kosher catering and suppliers to kitchens are out. All the other rules apply in full.
