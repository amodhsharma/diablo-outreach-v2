WHO COUNTS
Kosher shops, kosher supermarkets and grocers, and online kosher shops that buy other companies' branded, packaged sweets, chocolate, biscuits or snacks and sell them to shoppers. Many of Diablo's products are kosher certified. Independent shops count when they buy direct or sell online, at tier 3 when they are small.

LEAVE OUT
Kosher butchers, bakeries and restaurants, caterers, shops that sell only what they make, and anything the HARD RULES leave out.

WHERE TO LOOK
Shop lists of the market's kosher certification bodies and rabbinates, Jewish community directories and guides, local business listings (local.ch and search.ch in Switzerland, Google business listings and the country's yellow pages) searched with the town and words such as "koscher", "casher" or "kosher", and online kosher shops that deliver in the market.

WHAT GOOD FIT LOOKS LIKE
The model: a kosher supermarket or grocer with a sweets and snacks shelf of other brands, ideally with an online shop. Prioritise: several shops or an online shop; a wide sweets and biscuits range; already stocks kosher certified sugar free or no added sugar products without a direct competitor.

HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY
Rule 8: a kosher shop is not a cuisine specialist for this channel when it sells branded packaged sweets, biscuits or snacks to shoppers. Rule 4: it must buy stock and sell it from its own shop or online shop; no storage line is needed. Rule 9: when its website will not load but its business listing shows it trading, list it at tier 3 and say so. All the other rules apply in full.
