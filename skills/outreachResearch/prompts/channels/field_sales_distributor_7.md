matches: field sales, van sales, direct store delivery, traditional trade, tobacco
---
WHO COUNTS
Distributors and wholesalers that buy other companies' branded, packaged sweets, chocolate, biscuits or snacks, hold them and take them to corner shops, kiosks, convenience stores, petrol station shops and the traditional trade (spaza shops, kiranas, bodegas, tiendas de barrio, baqalas, milk bars, newsagents and tobacconists) across a region or the whole country (added 21 Nov 2026). This includes van sales and direct store delivery distributors, impulse and confectionery route distributors, tobacco and confectionery distributors that also carry sweets and snacks, and traditional trade distributors in emerging markets. Many of them have their own reps or vans calling on the shops: that is a sign of reach, not a condition for being listed.

LEAVE OUT
Single shops, kiosks and convenience stores themselves; cash and carry wholesalers that only sell to shops that collect (they belong to the wholesalers channel); tobacco-only, drinks-only and newspaper-only distributors; sales agencies, merchandising companies and brokers that never buy the goods; brand owners and their own route-to-market arms selling only their own brands. Everything else the HARD RULES leave out.

WHERE TO LOOK
Start with the brands: who distributes Ferrero, Mondelez, Mars, Nestlé, Haribo, Perfetti Van Melle, Storck, Lotus and the market's leading local brands to small shops (the brand owners' distributor pages, and the distributors' "brands we represent" pages). Then the convenience and independent retail trade press, wholesale and convenience distribution associations (for example the Federation of Wholesale Distributors in the UK), symbol group and convenience trade show exhibitor lists, and business listings searched region by region. Search with the trade's own words, for example: van sales distributor, direct store delivery, route to market, impulse distributor, confectionery and tobacco distributor, CTN supplier, spaza shop supplier, informal trade distributor; Süßwaren Fahrverkauf, Kiosk-Großhandel; distributeur confiserie, livraison épiceries; distribuidora de golosinas, preventa y autoventa; distribuidora a tiendas de barrio. Use a directory only to find a company, then check it on its own website.

WHAT GOOD FIT LOOKS LIKE
The model: a distributor that already carries international sweets and snack brands and reaches thousands of small shops directly (in Diablo's own list: Suy Sing, from sari-sari stores to supermarkets; Muller & Phipps, 50,000 outlets; Activ Distribution, 15,000 direct points of sale; Spanish Market, "from kiosks to hypermarkets"; regionally Dipam Gaúcha). Prioritise: number of shops reached and how often they are visited; regional or national coverage; already carries European or UK branded confectionery and snacks; its own reps or vans; already carries sugar-free or better-for-you lines without a direct competitor.

HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY
Rule 4 for this channel: write after "Storage: " whatever shows that it buys and delivers the goods itself (a warehouse or depot address, its own vans or van routes, deliveries to shops, trade ordering from stock). Reject as "does not hold stock" only when what you see shows that it is an agency that never buys the goods.
Rule 5: its own website must name at least one category from DIABLO'S CATALOGUE that it sells as other companies' brands. Name the categories and brands in "distributes".
Rule 12 for this channel (21 Nov 2026, scaled down and read with judgement): it passes when it shows any one of these: about 200 or more shops or outlets supplied; regional or national coverage; a warehouse of about 700 m2 or more; about 7 vans or delivery vehicles or more; about 7 sales reps or field agents or more; it is the appointed distributor for international brands; it is listed or part of a large group. When none of these numbers is shown, a company that calls itself a distributor or wholesaler still passes on ONE solid piece of evidence: a principals or brand list, a warehouse, depot or cash and carry address, a delivery fleet or van routes, a trade or B2B ordering portal or trade account sign-up, named shops or chains it supplies, or membership of a wholesale or trade association. Its own word alone is never enough. Write the solid thing after "Scale: ".
All the other rules apply in full.
