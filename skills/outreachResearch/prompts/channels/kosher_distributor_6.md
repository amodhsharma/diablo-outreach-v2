WHO COUNTS
Big distributors, importers and wholesalers of kosher food that buy other companies' branded, packaged sweets, chocolate, biscuits or snacks, hold them in their own warehouses and sell them on as they are to kosher shops, supermarket chains with a kosher section and community shops across a region or the whole country. Many of Diablo's products are kosher certified, so these businesses are a natural route for its range.

LEAVE OUT
Kosher shops and supermarkets themselves, kosher butchers, bakeries and caterers, kosher certification bodies and their supervisors, restaurants and suppliers to kitchens, brand owners and makers of their own products, small traders (rule 12), and anything else the HARD RULES leave out.

WHERE TO LOOK
Lists published by the market's kosher certification bodies and rabbinates (approved products and distributors), kosher food importer directories, trade show exhibitor lists (for example Kosherfest), importer directories (for example bestfoodimporters.com, Europages, Kompass) and the websites of supermarket chains with a kosher section, which often name their kosher supplier. Use a directory only to find a company, then check it on its own website.

WHAT GOOD FIT LOOKS LIKE
The model: a kosher food importer or wholesaler whose range includes branded chocolate, biscuits, sweets or snacks and that supplies kosher shops and supermarket chains' kosher sections across the market. Prioritise: supplies supermarket chains; a wide packaged range from other brands; already carries kosher certified confectionery.

HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY
Rule 8: a kosher specialist is not a cuisine specialist for this channel when its own website shows branded packaged sweets, biscuits or snacks for resale and it sells to shops. Rule 4: write how it holds stock after "Storage: ". Rule 7: kosher catering and suppliers to kitchens are out. Rule 12: write what shows its size after "Scale: ". All the other rules apply in full.
