matches: retail, shop, store, supermarket, grocer, hypermarket, pharmacy chain, drugstore, chemist, convenience chain, chain
---
WHO COUNTS
Retailers that buy other companies' branded, packaged sweets, chocolate, biscuits or snacks directly from brands, importers or distributors and sell them to shoppers in the market: supermarket and grocery chains, convenience and kiosk chains, petrol station and railway station convenience chains, health food, organic and vegan store chains, sports nutrition and fitness shops that sell bars, cookies or snacks to health-conscious shoppers, pharmacy and drugstore chains with a food or snacks shelf, speciality sweet shops that sell other brands, department store food halls, and online grocery, health, sweets and snack shops. Prefer chains and multi-store groups; an independent shop counts when it buys direct or sells online, at tier 3 when it is small.

LEAVE OUT
Single small shops that neither buy direct nor sell online, the marketplaces themselves (for example Amazon or Temu), brand owners, restaurants and cafes, and retailers that only stock their own label. Also non-food discount and gift chains with no food department, and online pharmacies based outside the market. Everything the HARD RULES leave out, as HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY explains for shops.

WHERE TO LOOK
Retailer websites and store locators, supplier or "sell with us" pages, retail trade press, store opening news, chain rankings and company registries. Check the retailer's own online shop or category pages for the sweets, biscuits and snacks it already stocks.

WHAT GOOD FIT LOOKS LIKE
The model: a chain that already sells many branded sweets, chocolate, biscuits and snacks from other companies to shoppers, ideally with a free from, sugar free or health shelf. Prioritise: already stocks sugar-free, no-added-sugar or better-for-you confectionery without a direct competitor; a clear supplier onboarding route (a supplier portal or "become a supplier" page); number of stores or online reach in the market; already stocks European brands. Mention the store count in the fit rationale when known.

HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY
Rules 1 to 3: leave out retailers that mainly sell what they make themselves (bakeries, patisseries, chocolatiers and sweet shops with their own production) and retailers that stock only their own label. Rule 4: the retailer must buy stock and sell it from its own shops or its own online shop; no storage line is needed. Rule 5: its own shop or website must show that it already sells at least one category from Diablo's catalogue from other brands. Rule 6: pharmacy and drugstore chains count when they sell packaged food or snacks on their shelves, and sports nutrition shops count when they sell bars, cookies or snacks; leave out those selling only medicines, care products or supplements, and drinks, wine or coffee shops. Rule 7: leave out shops for trade professionals only. Rule 8: leave out delicatessens, gourmet shops, wine shops and single cuisine grocers unless branded sweets, biscuits or snacks are a clear part of the shelf. Rules 9, 10 and 11 apply in full.
