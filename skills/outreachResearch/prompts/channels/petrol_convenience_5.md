matches: petrol, forecourt, fuel station, gas station, service station, convenience chain
---
WHO COUNTS
Petrol station shop chains, railway and travel station convenience chains, and the operators and buying groups behind them, that buy other companies' branded, packaged sweets, chocolate, biscuits or snacks and sell them to shoppers. Many are run by oil companies, retailers or franchise operators that buy centrally, so the buying company is the target, not one station.

LEAVE OUT
Single stations that do not buy direct, fuel companies with no shops, car washes, and anything the HARD RULES leave out. The wholesalers that supply these shops belong to the wholesaler channel.

WHERE TO LOOK
Fuel and convenience retail trade press, petrol station chain and franchise websites (store locators and "become a supplier" pages), convenience retail associations, retailer group websites (for example the convenience arms of supermarket groups) and local business listings for station shops.

WHAT GOOD FIT LOOKS LIKE
The model: a chain of station or convenience shops with a sweets, chocolate and snacks shelf of other brands and central buying (for example Coop Pronto or migrolino in Switzerland). Prioritise: number of shops; central buying or a supplier page; already stocks better-for-you or sugar free snacks without a direct competitor. Mention the shop count in the fit rationale when known.

HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY
Rule 4: the chain must buy stock and sell it from its own shops; no storage line is needed. Rule 5: its shops or website must show that it sells at least one category from Diablo's catalogue (sweets, chocolate, biscuits or snacks shelves count). Rule 6: a fuel company counts only through its shop business. All the other rules apply in full.
