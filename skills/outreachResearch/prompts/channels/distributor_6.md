WHO COUNTS
Big importers and distributors that buy other companies' branded, packaged sweets, chocolate, biscuits or snacks, hold them in their own warehouses and sell them as they are to supermarket and hypermarket chains, independent grocers, kiosks, petrol station shops, convenience stores, pharmacies and vending across a region or the whole country. This includes FMCG, consumer goods and "mass consumption" distributors whose range also covers other food, personal care or household products, when packaged food, confectionery or snacks is one of their main lines, and importers whose business is bringing foreign brands into the market and building them there, including British brands.

LEAVE OUT
Retailers that do not wholesale, brand owners, exporters and logistics-only providers (3PLs, freight forwarders) that never buy the goods. Sales and marketing agencies, merchandising companies, brokers, import agents and brand representation companies that do not buy and hold stock, even when they call themselves distributors. Small importers that sell mainly through their own web shop, and distributors that reach only a handful of shops (rule 12). Everything else the HARD RULES leave out.

WHERE TO LOOK
Start with the brands: the brand owners' own distributor pages and country pages, and the distributors' "our principals" and "brands we represent" pages (see SEARCH THROUGH THE BRANDS FIRST). Then the national wholesalers' and distributors' association, food importers' associations, rankings of the largest distributors in the trade press, trade show exhibitor lists, importer directories (for example bestfoodimporters.com, Europages, Kompass) and import shipment databases (for example Volza, ImportGenius). In a large country, search state by state for the strong regional distributors. Use a directory only to find a company, then check it on its own website.

WHAT GOOD FIT LOOKS LIKE
The model: a distributor that already carries international confectionery, biscuit and snack brands for their owners, holds them in its own distribution centres and reaches thousands of outlets, from small shops to supermarket chains (in Diablo's own list: Stuart Alexander, Muller & Phipps, Spanish Market, Hasbah Kenya, Activ Distribution, Suy Sing, Prambanan Kencana, Cencocal; regionally Univale and Dipam Gaúcha). Prioritise, in this order: national reach or the leading player in its region; already carries European or UK branded confectionery and snacks; named access to supermarket, convenience, pharmacy or kiosk chains; its own field sales force; already carries sugar-free or better-for-you lines without a direct competitor.

HOW THE HARD RULES APPLY
All twelve apply in full. Rule 4: write how the distributor holds stock after "Storage: " (warehouse, depot, distribution centre, own fleet or B2B web shop). Rule 5: a principals or brand list naming confectionery, chocolate, biscuit or snack brands proves the range; a brand-building importer (the rule 5 exception) belongs here. Rule 6: an FMCG distributor counts when packaged food or confectionery is one of its main divisions; pharmacy distributors count only when packaged food is a main line, not a side group next to medicines. Rule 12: write what shows its size after "Scale: " (outlets or customers, branches or distribution centres, warehouse size, fleet, coverage).
