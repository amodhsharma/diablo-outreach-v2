5. The company must already sell at least one category from DIABLO'S CATALOGUE above as a real part of its range, seen on its own website (product pages, product categories or brand list). 6. Food must be the company's main business. Leave out pharmacy and drugstore wholesalers whose main lines are medicines, medical equipment or generic pharmacy products, packaging suppliers, drinks-only or wine-only merchants, and coffee-led service companies such as office coffee and vending operators that run their own machines ("not a food business"). 7. Leave out suppliers of ingredients, raw materials, semi-finished products or bulk catering packs for professionals (gelato and pastry makers, bakeries, restaurants, hotels and caterers), even when their range includes chocolate, syrups or toppings ("ingredients for professionals"). 8. Leave out fine food, delicatessen, gourmet and artisanal specialists (cheese, charcuterie, truffles, wine, preserves), importers of one country's or one region's cuisine (for example Italian, Mexican, Greek or Asian food) and ethnic or world food wholesalers (for example African, Asian, Middle Eastern or Latin American foods for ethnic shops), unless branded packaged confectionery or biscuits for resale are a clear part of their range, meaning their own website has a sweets or snacks section and they sell to shops, not mainly to restaurants ("deli or cuisine specialist"). Searching in a local language must not turn the search into a search for that country's cuisine: in an Italian speaking city look for confectionery and snack distributors, not Italian food importers.
9. Open the company's own website (a marketplace seller with no website of its own: its storefront). If it does not load, leave the company out ("website does not open"). Give its current domain: when an old domain redirects to a new name, give the new one.
10. Leave out chilled and frozen food specialists: Diablo's products are ambient ("chilled or frozen specialist").
11. Never list a company at low confidence.
